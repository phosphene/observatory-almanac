# Animals

The relationship between humans and animals spans every dimension of lived experience—ecological, practical, and ethical. We share habitat with wild species whose behaviors and needs intersect with our own; we depend on domestic animals for companionship and care; and we bear responsibility for understanding both. This section brings together four practical reference guides organized around that responsibility.

**Backyard Wildlife Guide** documents the birds, mammals, reptiles, and amphibians most commonly encountered in residential outdoor spaces across North America, with identification criteria, behavioral ecology, habitat associations, and dietary profiles for each species.

**Wildlife Safety Tips** addresses the principles and protocols governing safe coexistence with wild animals, from general distance and attractant management to species-specific guidance for bears, mountain lions, coyotes, alligators, venomous snakes, and other wildlife known to cause human injury when encounters are mishandled.

**Pet First Aid** covers emergency stabilization for dogs, cats, and small animals—CPR, bleeding control, heatstroke, hypothermia, poisoning, and seizure response—grounded in guidance from the ASPCA, AKC, and CDC. First aid is preparatory knowledge; it does not replace veterinary care, but it can determine outcomes in the time before professional help is available.

**Home Remedies for Pets** provides evidence-informed protocols for managing common minor ailments at home—digestive upset, skin irritation, minor wounds, ear maintenance, and stress responses—alongside a clear framework for recognizing when home care is inappropriate and veterinary attention is required.
