# The Body-Grammar Substrate Hypothesis

## Statistical and Typological Evidence for Deep-Time Structural Inheritance

**Jan Ritch-Frel — June 2026**

## Abstract

This paper proposes that grammatical structure — stripped of phonology and lexicon — can survive at time depths where conventional historical linguistics has no tools. Two analytically distinct phenomena are examined. Track A: the grammaticalization of "head" nouns into reflexive pronouns (HEAD→SELF) clusters in the Caucasus at rates far above the cross-linguistic background. A Bayesian generalized linear mixed model with language-family random intercepts and geographic covariates yields a Caucasus effect of OR = 12.26 (95% credible interval [1.28, 87.83]; posterior P > 0: 0.984), based on 2,096 languages across 105 families from Grambank. The same feature reconstructs to proto-Afro-Asiatic (~10,000–15,000 ya), confirmed in five of six branches with independent lexical roots. Track B: Great Andamanese and Kusunda — both isolates in geographic refugia on the coastal dispersal route — share a structurally parallel body-zone noun classification architecture attested in no other documented language family. A peer-reviewed reconstruction of Proto-Kusunda (Spendley 2024) independently identifies the parallel. The paper proposes that body-zone classification is the full elaboration of the HEAD→SELF tendency under conditions of extended small-population isolation. Seven supplementary data tables provide the full dataset for independent replication.

Supplementary data tables (S1–S10) supporting all analyses are available from the author on request. The underlying data sources are Grambank v1.0.3 (Skirgård et al. 2023), CLICS4 (Tjuka et al. 2025), and Lexibank v2.1.
