# "Did a 5G Cellular Network Test Cause Hundreds of Birds to Die?"


## 1. The Claim

**Core assertion**: On November 5, 2018, conspiracy and alternative health blogger Erin Elizabeth published on her website "Health Nut News" an article headlined "Hundreds of Birds Dead During 5G Experiment in the Hague, The Netherlands." The article described a situation in which, allegedly, a 5G cellular network test was conducted in a park in The Hague (Huijgenspark) in late October 2018, and simultaneously hundreds of birds — primarily starlings — fell dead from the trees surrounding the park. The claim implies a direct causal relationship: 5G test → electromagnetic radiation beam → mass bird death.

**Structure of the original claim**: The claim rested on two factual sub-claims that could be evaluated independently:
1. A real event — hundreds of starlings died over a period of approximately two weeks at Huijgenspark in The Hague (October–November 2018)
2. A fabricated causal claim — that a 5G cellular network test was conducted near the park at the same time, and that this test caused the deaths

**Origin of the narrative**: The Health Nut News article was not original reporting. Erin Elizabeth's post was a verbatim reproduction of a series of Facebook posts authored by John Kuhles, who operates several anti-5G conspiracy websites and social media pages. Kuhles had no direct access to veterinary investigation reports, telecommunications regulatory records, or any other primary source. His narrative presented the coincidence of bird deaths and (alleged) 5G infrastructure as causal evidence, adding dramatic color: "Immediately afterward, birds fell dead from the trees. And the nearby ducks that were swimming seemed to react very oddly as well; they were simultaneously putting their heads underwater to escape the radiation."

**Rapid viral spread**: The article and the underlying narrative spread rapidly through anti-5G communities online and had been shared over 125,000 times on Facebook by the time the Snopes investigation was published in November 2018. The story capitalized on a moment when 5G technology was transitioning from specialized telecommunications journalism to mainstream awareness, producing both genuine curiosity and concern about an unfamiliar technology.

**Connection to broader 5G conspiracy ecosystem**: The Hague bird deaths claim did not emerge in isolation. It was part of a wider wave of claims in 2018–2019 asserting that 5G technology posed health risks to humans and animals — claims that would later intensify dramatically during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, when some conspiracy theories directly linked 5G networks to coronavirus transmission. The Hague bird claim served as a prototype for the genre: an unexplained natural event, a nearby 5G installation, and a causal narrative that required no physical mechanism because it relied solely on temporal and spatial proximity.


## 2. What's Actually True

**The bird deaths were real**: Between October 19 and November 3, 2018, 337 dead starlings and 2 dead common wood pigeons were indeed found in and around Huijgenspark in The Hague. The municipal government of The Hague confirmed these deaths, implemented a precautionary dog ban in the park pending investigation, and engaged multiple expert organizations to investigate: Wageningen Bioveterinary Research, the Dutch Wildlife Health Centre at Utrecht, Erasmus University in Rotterdam, and Ghent University in Belgium. Initial viral testing ruled out West Nile virus and the usutu virus, but the cause of death had not been definitively determined as of November 12, 2018.

**Mysterious bird deaths are not uncommon**: The dramatic framing of the Huijgenspark deaths — "you likely haven't heard a lot about this because keeping it quiet was the plan all along" — misrepresents the base rate of unexplained bird mortality events. The Associated Press reported in 2011, covering a wave of similar "aflockalypse" events in Arkansas, Louisiana, and Sweden, that the US Geological Survey's National Wildlife Health Center has tracked mass bird mortality events since the 1970s, logging an average of 163 such events per year in the United States alone (Borenstein, S., Associated Press, 7 January 2011). These events have causes ranging from disease and poisoning to weather events, collision with structures, and viral infection. Many individual events remain unexplained permanently. The Huijgenspark deaths were newsworthy in Dutch local media for several days — not suppressed — but they did not receive international coverage because unexplained local bird deaths are, statistically, unremarkable events.

**No 5G test occurred near Huijgenspark in October 2018**: The central factual claim underlying the conspiracy narrative — that a 5G test was conducted near Huijgenspark simultaneously with the bird deaths — is false. Snopes investigators contacted Dutch telecommunications companies and regulatory bodies directly. A representative of KPN, the largest mobile operator in The Netherlands, stated unequivocally via Twitter: "I can be very clear about this matter; there are no 5G tests in Den Haag. This is a complete hoax." The Dutch equivalent of the FCC (Agentschap Telecom) also asserted that no such test had been licensed or conducted. The operator of the train station (Holland Spoor) allegedly involved in the 5G test, NS (Nederlandse Spoorwegen), stated that its representatives were "unaware that recent 5G tests were conducted at this location."

**The only documented 5G test was months earlier**: One documented 5G network test did occur in The Hague area — but it was on June 28, 2018, not in late October 2018, and it was located in Voorburg, a suburb geographically distinct from Huijgenspark. This test was conducted by Huawei using 100 MHz of C-band spectrum at 3.5 GHz at the KPN offices in Voorburg, under a special one-day permit issued by the Dutch telecommunications regulator. No bird deaths were associated with this June test. The temporal gap between the June 2018 test (over four months before the bird deaths) and the October 2018 bird deaths is not consistent with a causal relationship even by proximity reasoning.

**The conspiracy originator's retraction**: On November 8, 2018 — three days after Erin Elizabeth republished his posts — John Kuhles himself posted again on Facebook, effectively abandoning the 5G-specific claim while maintaining conspiratorial framing. He wrote: "My 5G conclusions are assertions/conjecture with a QUESTION mark!" and "Some think there is 'no 5G' in Den Haag. That is a lie (or mis-perception)..." His revised position moved goalposts from "a 5G test caused this" to "maybe it was lots of 4G LTE networks" — an even less specific and less falsifiable claim. Erin Elizabeth's article, which had already gone viral, was not updated to reflect Kuhles's retraction.

**The physics of 5G and non-ionizing radiation**: The scientific question of whether 5G-frequency electromagnetic radiation could cause bird deaths — even if a test had occurred — can be addressed through established radiation physics and toxicology. 5G networks in Europe primarily operate in three frequency bands:
- A low-band coverage layer at ~700 MHz
- A primary service band at 3.4–3.8 GHz
- A theoretical millimeter-wave "super data layer" at 24.25–27.5 GHz (largely undeployed as of 2018)

All of these frequencies fall within the non-ionizing electromagnetic spectrum — radiation that lacks sufficient energy per photon to ionize atoms or break chemical bonds. The International Commission on Non-Ionizing Radiation Protection (ICNIRP) evaluated the safety of electromagnetic fields in the 100 kHz–300 GHz range in 1998 and reaffirmed its guidelines in 2009 (ICNIRP, 2009, *Health Physics*, reconfirmation of 1998 guidelines). The ICNIRP concluded that "the scientific literature published since the 1998 guidelines has provided no evidence of any adverse effects below the basic restrictions" and that "the plausibility of the various non-thermal mechanisms that have been proposed is very low."

**Expert assessment of 5G and bird mortality specifically**: Dr. Eric van Rongen, a member of the Health Council of the Netherlands and chairman of the ICNIRP, stated to Snopes directly: "The levels of exposure would be comparable to those of the current (3G and 4G) networks, and thus very low, much lower than the exposure limits." On the specific question of bird mortality, van Rongen stated: "The only way one could imagine death of birds as a result of exposure to electromagnetic fields is with very high level exposure that results in considerable heating... But the levels that are used by mobile telecom antennas are not strong enough for this to happen. There are maybe millions of such antennas around the world and this has never been reported."


## 3. Why People Believe This

**Epistemic novelty — the unfalsifiability architecture of infrastructure conspiracy theories**:

The 5G-bird-deaths claim demonstrates one of the most epistemically sophisticated forms of modern conspiracy misinformation: the **infrastructure conspiracy**. Infrastructure conspiracies differ from simpler false-event claims (like the fake asteroid video) in a specific way: they exploit genuinely physical, real-world technological infrastructure as their causal mechanism, making it difficult for non-expert audiences to assess the plausibility of claimed effects.

5G cellular infrastructure is real, physically present in many cities, and involves electromagnetic phenomena that most people do not understand in detail. This genuine opacity creates a productive ambiguity that conspiracy narratives exploit. If you are a lay person and you encounter the claim that "5G radiation kills birds," you face an immediate epistemic problem: you know you don't fully understand 5G, you know electromagnetic radiation is real and can have biological effects at some intensities, and you may have a general prior that technology companies and government regulators are not fully honest about the risks of their products. In this epistemic context, dismissing the claim entirely requires a kind of confident expert knowledge that most people reasonably admit they lack.

This is the core epistemological vulnerability: technology creates genuinely opaque causal infrastructure. The opacity is not manufactured — it's real. Most people genuinely do not know the difference between ionizing and non-ionizing radiation, do not know the ICNIRP exposure limits or how they were derived, and do not have intuitions about how much electromagnetic energy is required to produce thermal effects in biological tissue. In the absence of this specific technical knowledge, the precautionary emotional response ("I don't understand this, so I can't be sure it's safe") can be rationally appropriate for novel technologies.

The conspiracy narrative exploits this rational precaution by providing a seemingly evidential anchor: the bird deaths. The bird deaths were real, documented, and unexplained at the time of the claim's original circulation. The narrative connected a real, verified, unexplained phenomenon (bird deaths) to a real, physical, opaque infrastructure (5G networks). The causal link was entirely fabricated — there was no 5G test, and no physical mechanism would explain the claimed effect — but the audience did not have access to the technical confirmation of this fabrication without substantial investigative effort. The conspiracy narrative had the structural advantage: it was specific, emotionally vivid, and built on verified partial facts. The debunking required tracking down telecommunications regulators, querying mobile operators, and assessing ICNIRP radiation guidelines.

The conspiracy's temporal structure also exploited human causal cognition. Post hoc ergo propter hoc — after this, therefore because of this — is a well-documented cognitive bias in which temporal correlation is interpreted as causation. Even if audiences were aware of the June 28 Huawei demonstration (four months before the deaths), the brain's causal-inference systems tend to seek nearby causal explanations for effects and to find satisfaction in temporal adjacency narratives. The conspiracy narrative needed only to place a 5G installation in the story at roughly the right time; precise dates were not something most audiences would verify.

A final dimension: the Erin Elizabeth platform and the broader anti-5G media ecosystem created an ambient informational environment in which 5G health fears were pre-seeded before the specific Hague incident occurred. Audiences who had previously encountered anti-5G content were epistemically prepared to interpret the Hague bird deaths as 5G-caused — they had a ready-made causal framework that the new event could slot into without requiring construction from scratch. This preparatory work of ambient conspiracy culture reduces the activation energy needed for any specific new claim to gain traction.


## 4. The Broader Pattern

The 5G-bird-kills-birds claim was an early and prototypical example of a conspiracy theory genre that would reach significant scale during the COVID-19 pandemic. By April 2020, 5G conspiracy theories had evolved to claim that 5G networks caused COVID-19, triggered its spread, or were responsible for symptoms attributed to the disease. These claims led to arson attacks on cellular towers across the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, and other countries, as well as documented harassment and assault of telecommunications workers (Wardle, C. and Derakhshan, H., 2017, *Council of Europe report on information disorder* — general framework; specific 5G-arson documentation from BBC and Reuters reporting, April–May 2020).

The Hague bird-deaths story was significant not primarily as misinformation about birds but as a template and precedent for the broader 5G health conspiracy genre. It established the master narrative: 5G infrastructure → unexplained biological harm → institutional cover-up. This template was robust enough to absorb new content as the 5G rollout expanded globally. Every unexplained animal death near cellular infrastructure, every health complaint from people living near cell towers, and ultimately the COVID-19 pandemic itself could be slotted into this template. The Hague birds were the template's founding instance.

It is notable that John Kuhles — the claim's originator — essentially admitted its speculative character within three days of it going viral, but that the subsequent viral life of the claim was completely independent of its originator's views. Once a claim has been widely distributed, its relationship to its source is severed. Corrections from the originator, institutional refutations from credible experts, and systematic fact-checking reach only a fraction of the audience that received the original claim. The infrastructure conspiracy is, in this structural sense, a particularly durable form of misinformation: it cannot be killed at the source.


## 5. Evidentiary Assessment

**EARC assignment: R (Refuted)**

The claim is refuted on two independent dimensions that each would be independently sufficient:

**Dimension 1 — factual premise refuted**: No 5G test occurred near Huijgenspark in October–November 2018. This was confirmed by Dutch telecommunications regulator Agentschap Telecom, by mobile operator KPN, and by train station operator NS. The only documented 5G test in The Hague occurred in June 2018 in a different location, was not followed by bird deaths, and was conducted under a single-day license with regulatory oversight.

**Dimension 2 — causal mechanism refuted**: No physical mechanism exists by which 5G-frequency non-ionizing radiation at regulatory exposure levels could cause rapid bird mortality. Dr. Eric van Rongen (ICNIRP chairman) directly stated that 5G exposure levels are comparable to existing 3G/4G networks, which have not been associated with bird mortality despite millions of installations globally. ICNIRP (1998, 2009) established exposure limits for the relevant frequency range and confirmed no evidence of adverse effects below those limits.

**Gap category: electromagnetic-health-claims**

This entry addresses a large and persistent gap in public understanding of non-ionizing radiation, the distinction between ionizing and non-ionizing electromagnetic spectrum effects, and the evidentiary standards for evaluating technology health claims.


## 6. Verdict and Implications

**Verdict**: The claim is **false**. No 5G test occurred in The Hague at the time of the bird deaths. Even if a 5G test had occurred, no physical mechanism exists by which it could have caused the observed mortality. The claim originated with a single conspiracy blogger who admitted its speculative character days after publication; it was amplified without caveat by a medical misinformation website. The actual bird deaths were a real but unremarkable event — mysterious local bird die-offs are documented hundreds of times per year in the United States alone — that was latched onto as a narrative anchor for pre-existing anti-5G conspiracy frameworks.

**What is genuinely unresolved**: The cause of the Huijgenspark starling deaths in October–November 2018 was not definitively established in the public record. Possible causes including viral disease, pesticide exposure, and other environmental contaminants were investigated by Wageningen Bioveterinary Research, the Dutch Wildlife Health Centre, and university partners. The absence of a confirmed alternative explanation is not evidence for the 5G hypothesis — it is simply a scientific unknown. Unsolved bird die-offs are common; the unknown cause does not support any particular causal attribution.

**Implications for 5G health discourse**: The Hague bird claim and its successors illustrate a specific responsibility that accompanies the rollout of novel technological infrastructure: transparent, proactive communication of what is known about health effects, what the regulatory basis for exposure limits is, and how those limits were derived. The ICNIRP framework is scientifically credible and independently maintained; its conclusions do not depend on telecommunications industry funding. Making that credibility visible and accessible to non-specialist audiences is a form of public health work that institutions have an obligation to undertake before, not after, conspiracy narratives fill the vacuum.
