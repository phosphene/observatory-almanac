# Was a 240-Pound, 7-Foot-Long, 100-Year-Old Fish Found in Detroit River?


## 1. The Claim

**Core assertion**: On April 30, 2021, the Alpena Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office — a branch of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service — shared a Facebook post describing what it called a "once in a lifetime" catch from their Detroit River native species survey work. The post described a fish that tipped the scales at 240 pounds, measured 6 feet 10 inches in length, and had a girth of nearly 4 feet. The fish was identified as a lake sturgeon (*Acipenser fulvescens*). Based on its extraordinary size, the biologists estimated it to be over 100 years old — potentially born around 1920, when Detroit was the fourth-largest city in America. The Facebook post and its remarkable photograph went viral, generating widespread national media coverage. The claim is that this fish existed as described, is genuinely enormous for its species, and is indeed likely a centenarian.

**The emotional valence of the claim**: Part of what made this story compelling to the public was not merely the biological facts — it was the temporal bridge. A fish born in 1920 would have lived through the rise and fall of Detroit's automotive golden age, the Great Depression, World War II, the mid-century industrial expansion of the Great Lakes region, and the subsequent ecological collapse of those same waters due to pollution and overharvest. The survival of such a fish through a century of industrial transformation of the Great Lakes watershed carries a powerful narrative weight. It also represents, implicitly, a conservation success story — the fish is alive, and the biologists returned it to the river.

**What was being questioned**: The viral spread of the story prompted several forms of skepticism: Was the fish really as large as claimed? Was the age estimate credible? Was the photograph accurately representing the fish? And perhaps most fundamentally — given the long history of "monster fish" hoaxes and exaggerated fishing stories — was this real at all?

**Why it matters**: Lake sturgeon are among the most ecologically significant freshwater fish in North America, and their populations were devastated throughout the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries by commercial overharvesting, habitat loss from dam construction, and water quality degradation. The presence of a century-old individual in the Detroit River is not just a viral moment — it is an indicator of the partial success of restoration efforts that have been ongoing for decades.


## 2. What's Actually True

**The fish is real and was captured by government scientists**: The catch was made by biologists from the Alpena Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, during systematic native species survey work in the Detroit River. The USFWS is a credible federal agency, and its Alpena office's stated mission includes the conservation, restoration, and management of fishery resources in the Great Lakes Basin. This was not a recreational catch or an informal report — it was a government-operated scientific survey that generated documented physical measurements.

**The measurements check out**: The fish measured 6 feet 10 inches (approximately 82 inches) in length and weighed 240 pounds, with a maximum girth of nearly 48 inches. These measurements were taken by professional biologists and are not self-reported fishing claims. USFWS biologist Justin Chiotti confirmed the measurements to the Detroit Free Press. These figures make the fish one of the largest lake sturgeon on record in the United States, which is consistent with the USFWS Alpena office's characterization of it as a "once in a lifetime" catch.

**The age estimate is scientifically grounded but carries real uncertainty**: Determining the age of a lake sturgeon is not an exact science, as the USFWS itself acknowledged. Traditional methods involve examining the growth rings of certain skeletal materials — particularly pectoral fin rays — analogously to counting tree rings. These methods have been partially validated using radiocarbon dating by researchers including Auer (1996, *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*) and have been further refined since. However, all age estimation methods carry increasing uncertainty at extreme sizes, and the Detroit River fish appears to exceed the validated range of most published aging standards.

**The Michigan DNR age chart and its limitations**: The Michigan Department of Natural Resources publishes a chart estimating lake sturgeon age based on length and girth measurements. Using this chart, a fish 80 inches long with a 35-inch girth would be estimated at approximately 153 years old. The Detroit River fish — at 82 inches and 48 inches girth — exceeds the chart's range on both axes. The closest approximation suggests an age of at least 100 years under conservative parameters, but this extrapolation carries "a great deal of uncertainty," as USFWS biologist Chiotti confirmed. He told the Detroit Free Press: "We don't know the exact age of the fish. But, to be 7-foot long and 240 pounds, the fish was likely 100 years old or older, and I think that's a minimum estimate."

**The fish was released alive**: After being processed for scientific data collection, the fish was returned to the Detroit River. This is consistent with the USFWS Alpena office's restoration mission and with best practices for managing large, old individuals of protected species — such individuals represent disproportionate reproductive investment and population genetic value.


## 3. Epistemic Novelty — What This Case Reveals Beyond the Verdict

The verdict here is True. But the genuinely interesting questions in this fact-check are not about the verdict — they are about what the fish represents biologically, ecologically, and in terms of the limits of scientific measurement.

**The epistemology of fish aging**: Aging long-lived fish remains one of the more technically demanding problems in fishery biology. The standard technique for lake sturgeon involves sectioning pectoral fin rays and counting growth increments under a microscope. Each annual growth increment appears as a band, similar in principle to tree rings. However, growth rings can be difficult to distinguish in very old individuals because growth rates slow and rings compress. The result is systematic underestimation of age in the oldest individuals — which means that published maximum ages for lake sturgeon may themselves be underestimates driven by methodological artifacts rather than biological reality (Campana, 2001, *Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries*).

**Radiocarbon dating as a corrective**: The development of radiocarbon dating methods for fish otoliths and other skeletal structures (Kalish, 1993, *Marine Ecology Progress Series*) has provided an independent check on traditional aging methods. For lake sturgeon, partial validation studies using radiocarbon confirm that traditional fin ray methods tend to undercount age in very large individuals. This means that the Detroit River fish, conservatively estimated at 100+ years, could plausibly be older than even the most aggressive age extrapolation suggests. The epistemic floor — "at least 100" — is itself a floor beneath an open-ended ceiling.

**Lake sturgeon as living links to pre-industrial ecology**: Lake sturgeon are a genuinely ancient species. The genus *Acipenser* has existed in approximately its current form for well over 100 million years, making individual sturgeon something like living paleontological specimens. A century-old sturgeon in the Detroit River would have been born into a watershed that was heavily industrialized even in 1920, yet survived through some of the most severe ecological degradation ever experienced by a major North American river system — including catastrophic pollution events in the 1950s–1970s, the introduction of multiple invasive species, and dramatic changes in the river's physical structure. Its survival is not a passive historical fact but an active biological achievement.

**The Great Lakes restoration context**: The USFWS Alpena office's Native Species Restoration Program is part of a multi-decade effort to recover lake sturgeon populations that were driven to near-extirpation in the Great Lakes by the late nineteenth century. Sturgeon were once so abundant in the Great Lakes that commercial fishers considered them a nuisance that tangled nets and disposed of them by burning piles of them on shore (Harkness & Dymond, 1961, *The Lake Sturgeon*). By the early twentieth century, commercial overharvesting had reduced populations to a tiny fraction of their historical abundance. The presence of a 100+-year-old individual in 2021 represents not just survival but the beginning of recovery — and it suggests that the Detroit River, despite its industrial history, retains some capacity to support centenarian fish.

**The precision illusion in age estimation**: The claim that the fish was likely born "around 1920 when Detroit became the 4th largest city in America" is a compelling piece of narrative journalism, but it is also a precision illusion. The true range of uncertainty around the age estimate is quite wide — the fish could be anywhere from 80 to 150+ years old. The "born in 1920" framing collapses that uncertainty into a single memorable date that anchors the story in human historical time. This is excellent science communication but poor epistemology. The more accurate and honest statement — "a minimum of 100 years old, likely older, with high uncertainty" — is less narratively satisfying but more scientifically correct.


## 4. Evidence Assessment and Source Quality

**USFWS documentation**: The catch was reported by the Alpena Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office via its official Facebook page on April 30, 2021. This constitutes official government documentation. The USFWS Alpena office's mission, procedures, and personnel are publicly documented on its federal website. Biologist Justin Chiotti's confirmation of the measurements to the Detroit Free Press adds named-source journalistic corroboration.

**Scientific literature on age estimation**: The claim that the fish is approximately 100 years old rests on documented size-age relationships for *Acipenser fulvescens* published in the scientific literature. The Michigan DNR's age estimation chart is itself derived from the scientific literature, including studies by Auer (1996) using validated aging methods. The extrapolation beyond the chart's established range is acknowledged as uncertain by both the USFWS and the Michigan DNR. The use of radiocarbon validation to partially corroborate traditional aging methods (as discussed in Campana, 2001) contextualizes the uncertainty appropriately.

**Peer-reviewed context**: Scientific literature on lake sturgeon age estimation includes Auer, N.A. (1996). Importance of habitat and migration to sturgeons with emphasis on lake sturgeon. *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*, 125, 500–514; and Campana, S.E. (2001). Accuracy, precision and quality control in age determination, including a review of the use and abuse of age validation methods. *Journal of Fish Biology*, 59(2), 197–242. These studies establish both the methods used and their acknowledged limitations.

**Media reporting quality**: The Detroit Free Press story cited in Snopes includes a direct quote from a named USFWS biologist, providing journalistic corroboration. Multiple national outlets covered the story, all citing the same USFWS source. No competing accounts challenged the basic measurements.

**Gap assigned**: **R (Research Gap)** — Age estimation for extreme-size lake sturgeon lacks validated methodology at the upper end of the size range. No published study specifically validates aging techniques for individuals at the 82-inch / 240-pound scale; all estimates for fish this large involve extrapolation with acknowledged uncertainty. The actual age of this specific individual is permanently unknowable with available technology unless bone or otolith material is radiocarbon-dated.


## 5. Broader Context — Lake Sturgeon, the Great Lakes, and the Long Ecology of Recovery

Lake sturgeon (*Acipenser fulvescens*) are the largest freshwater fish native to North America, capable under exceptional conditions of reaching lengths over 6 feet and weights over 200 pounds. They are also among the most long-lived freshwater vertebrates: females may not reach sexual maturity until age 20–25, and individuals can live for 100–150 years under favorable conditions. These life history characteristics make lake sturgeon extraordinarily vulnerable to overexploitation — once a population collapses, recovery is measured in generations, not years (Dettmers et al., 2012, *Journal of Great Lakes Research*).

The history of lake sturgeon in the Great Lakes is one of the most dramatic wildlife overexploitation stories in North American history. In the mid-nineteenth century, lake sturgeon were commercially harvested at industrial scale for their meat, oil, eggs (caviar), and swim bladders (used in clarifying beer and wine). By the 1880s, populations had plummeted to commercial non-viability. Subsequent decades of habitat loss from dam construction, navigational dredging, and water quality impairment continued the population decline into the twentieth century. The Detroit River itself was significantly altered by channelization and industrial development, eliminating historic spawning habitat.

Recovery efforts have been underway since the 1980s and 1990s, coordinated through the Great Lakes Fishery Commission and federal and state fishery agencies. The USFWS Alpena office's work in the Detroit River represents the operational frontline of these efforts. Sturgeon are restocked from hatchery programs, spawning reef habitats are being restored through artifical reef placement, and annual surveys like the one that captured the 240-pound fish provide population monitoring data. The presence of a very large, presumably very old female in active Detroit River habitat in 2021 is a meaningful biological signal that the restoration is producing results, even if population-level recovery remains incomplete.

The sex of the Detroit River fish was inferred from its large size — female lake sturgeon typically grow larger than males and live longer. Females of reproductive age represent cumulative spawning investment that is irreplaceable at short timescales. The decision to return this fish to the river was therefore not merely protocol — it was a conservation imperative. Each large, old female in a recovering population carries disproportionate reproductive value because she has survived to an age that many females do not reach.

The Detroit River context also raises questions about the relationship between industrial ecosystems and biological resilience. The river runs through one of the most heavily industrialized urban corridors in North America, and yet it has supported — or at least not extirpated — a fish that may have been born before the Ford River Rouge Complex was completed. The coexistence of century-old lake sturgeon with an industrial river landscape is ecologically remarkable and points to the importance of refugia — small pockets of intact or recovering habitat within otherwise degraded landscapes — in species survival under human-altered conditions.


## 6. Verdict Summary and Takeaways

**Snopes Verdict: True**

A 240-pound, 6-foot-10-inch lake sturgeon was genuinely captured in the Detroit River in late April 2021 by USFWS biologists, measured, processed, and released. The fish is credibly estimated to be at least 100 years old based on documented size-age relationships for the species, with the caveat that this estimate carries significant uncertainty at the upper end and the fish could be substantially older.

**Key takeaways**:

1. **The catch is verified by government scientists**: This is not a fishing story. It is the documented output of a federal fishery survey, with named biologists and institutional accountability. The measurements are reliable.

2. **Age estimates are scientifically grounded but genuinely uncertain**: The "100 years old" figure is a scientifically reasonable minimum estimate, not a precise reading. The fish could be 80 years old or 150 years old — available methods cannot resolve the uncertainty at this size range. The "born in 1920" framing is compelling narrative but should be understood as illustrative rather than precise.

3. **Lake sturgeon conservation is the real story**: The presence of such a large, old individual in the Detroit River is a data point in a multi-decade restoration effort. It is encouraging evidence that the ecosystem retains some capacity to support the full life cycle of a species that was nearly eliminated from these waters.

4. **Life history vulnerability**: Lake sturgeon's slow maturation and longevity make them among the most overexploitation-vulnerable freshwater fish in North America. Population recovery requires protection of large females across multiple decades. The handling of this fish — scientific processing, data collection, and release — represents best practice.

5. **Epistemic gap**: No method currently available can determine the precise age of a living sturgeon this large. Radiocarbon dating of skeletal material would require killing the fish. The true age of the Detroit River individual will remain unknown unless the fish dies and its bones are later analyzed.
