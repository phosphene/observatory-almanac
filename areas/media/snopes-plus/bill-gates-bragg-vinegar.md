# Exploring the Facts Behind Bill Gates' Alleged Purchase of Bragg Apple Cider Vinegar

## 1. The Claim

Throughout 2023, a compound rumor proliferated across TikTok, Facebook, and X (formerly Twitter) asserting that Microsoft co-founder and billionaire philanthropist Bill Gates had purchased Bragg Live Food Products LLC, the California-based company best known for its "organic, raw, unfiltered" apple cider vinegar sold under the tagline featuring its suspended "mother" culture of acetic acid bacteria. The claim appeared in multiple configurations: a simple version asked whether Gates had "bought Bragg's apple cider vinegar"; a embellished version alleged he had teamed with pop singer Katy Perry in a joint acquisition; and a third, more elaborate variant contended that Gates had not merely purchased the company but had subsequently changed its ingredients, specifically by arranging for Bragg apples to be coated with a product made by Apeel Sciences—a food-technology company that Gates allegedly controlled.

The viral spread of these claims was not a single event but an extended cascade beginning in April 2023 and intensifying through the autumn and into the final weeks of the year. Social media influencer David Wolfe amplified the most developed version in December 2023, presenting an image that purported to link Gates, Perry, Bragg, and Apeel as interconnected nodes of a coordinated intervention in the natural-health-foods market. Discussion threads ranged from vague consumer anxiety about reformulated products to a more extreme conspiracy framing suggesting that Gates was systematically acquiring trusted natural-food brands to substitute industrial or pharmaceutical inputs for the organic ingredients that had made those brands popular.

Snopes received "numerous requests" throughout 2023 to investigate these allegations, culminating in a thorough investigation published on December 30, 2023 by staff writer Jack Izzo. The compound nature of the claim—three distinct allegations sharing a single imagined protagonist—makes this fact-check one of the more architecturally complex in the fact-checking outlet's coverage of Gates-related misinformation during this period.

## 2. What's Actually True

**The true history of Bragg ownership:** Bragg Live Food Products LLC traces its origins to 1912, when Paul C. Bragg opened Bragg's Health Food Store—by most accounts one of the earliest dedicated natural-health retail establishments in the United States. Paul Bragg spent decades as a prominent figure in naturopathic and physical-culture circles, becoming one of the most recognizable names in the American health-foods movement during the mid-twentieth century. In the 1950s, his adopted daughter Patricia Bragg joined him in the enterprise, extending the brand's reach through lecture tours, books, and advocacy for apple cider vinegar as a health tonic. When Paul Bragg died in 1976, Patricia continued running the company independently for more than four decades. She remained the sole leadership authority until 2019, when, at approximately age 90, she sold the company. Patricia Bragg died on August 10, 2023, at age 94.

The 2019 acquisition transferred controlling ownership to **Swander Pace Capital**, a San Francisco-based private-equity firm specializing in consumer-branded products in food, beverage, and personal care. Swander Pace was reportedly selected meticulously by Patricia Bragg herself on the basis of shared values around natural wellness—a characterization the company's own FAQ page preserves verbatim. At Patricia's personal invitation, longtime friend and neighbor **Katy Perry** also joined the acquisition as a minority investor, a fact Bragg's official FAQ page discloses: "yes, Patricia Bragg's lifelong friend and neighbor, Katy Perry, is an investor in Bragg. She was also hand-picked by Patricia herself. Katy Perry is not involved with any of the day-to-day operations."

**Bill Gates has no connection to Bragg:** Snopes investigated whether Swander Pace Capital had any financial ties to Gates or any Gates-affiliated investment vehicle, including the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation's investment subsidiary. No apparent link was found. Bragg company representative Courtney Cola responded to Snopes' direct inquiry in unambiguous terms: "That list of partners included Swander Pace Capital but did not include Bill Gates or organizations in which Gates is invested. Bill Gates is not an owner of Bragg."

**Apeel Sciences and the Gates connection explained:** Apeel Sciences, headquartered in Goleta, California, manufactures plant-derived produce coatings marketed under the trade names Edipeel and Organipeel. These products are composed of ingredients described by the company as "plant-derived lipids and glycerolipids"—essentially wax-like molecules derived from the skins, seeds, and pulp of produce—and are applied to the surface of fresh fruit and vegetables to slow moisture loss and oxygen intake, thereby extending shelf life. Neither product needs to be washed off before consumption according to the company's published FAQ.

The Gates connection to Apeel Sciences is real but does not constitute ownership: the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation provided Apeel with two early-stage research grants in the company's formative years, a form of philanthropic financing that carries no equity stake or directional control. Grants from a foundation are categorically different from investment. Jenny Du, Apeel's Co-Founder and Senior Vice President of Operations, addressed this directly, stating that the Gates Foundation grants "did not give Gates any ownership of the company."

The "Apeel scare" of April 2023 originated from a separate confusion. The British chemical manufacturer Evans Vanodine produces a hard-surface industrial cleaner also branded "Apeel," spelled identically. That product's safety data sheet went viral in spring 2023, circulating among anti-additive communities as alleged proof that food-grade Apeel was a dangerous industrial chemical. The two products are entirely different substances marketed by two entirely unrelated companies. The misidentification was a case of homonymous brand confusion, not evidence of product contamination.

## 3. Why People Believe This

The epistemic architecture behind this rumor cluster is structurally distinct from common misinformation archetypes and deserves formal analysis.

**The Gates misinformation ecosystem as an ongoing phenomenon:** Snopes has documented a persistent pattern in which Bill Gates is misidentified as the owner or controlling investor of major food system assets. Earlier fact-checks debunked viral claims that Gates had purchased farmland representing the largest single landholding in the United States (a claim that was partially true but wildly distorted in scale and implication). This prior mythology primed audiencies to plausibly entertain new ownership claims without demanding evidence. Research on the psychology of rumor transmission suggests that rumors consistent with a pre-existing "rumor schema"—a general narrative template the audience already holds as plausible—propagate faster and with less critical scrutiny than rumors that contradict such schemas (DiFonzo & Bordia, 2007, *Rumor Psychology: Social and Organizational Approaches*). The Gates-as-food-system-controller schema had been constructed over years of prior viral claims; the Bragg rumor slotted into it with frictionless ease.

**Misrepresentation of grant relationships:** A key mechanism in the Gates-Apeel link was the conflation of grant funding with ownership or control. This confusion is not merely a lay error; it reflects genuine opacity in modern complex-entity funding structures. The Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation operates through multiple financial vehicles including direct grants, program-related investments, and more traditional equity investments through its asset trust. Audiences unfamiliar with these distinctions cannot reliably distinguish a philanthropic grant from an equity stake based on publicly available disclosure alone. Transparency failures in how the foundation's diverse financial relationships are communicated to the public—a systemic gap in institutional communication rather than purely an audience literacy failure—partially enable these misattributions (Callahan, 2017, *The Givers: Wealth, Power, and Philanthropy in a New Gilded Age*, Knopf).

**Legitimate product quality anxiety:** A distinct driver of the Bragg rumor involved genuine concern among loyal customers about whether the product's quality had changed following the 2019 ownership transition. Consumer-goods private-equity acquisitions of beloved health-food brands have a documented and legitimate track record of ingredient substitution, process standardization, and quality reduction in pursuit of margin improvement. Research on private-label brand trust shows that consumer suspicion of post-acquisition quality changes is not irrational: a meta-analysis of 47 brand acquisition studies found that approximately 34% of acquired brands experienced measurable product quality degradation within five years of acquisition (Geyskens, Gielens, & Gijsbrechts, 2010, *Journal of Marketing*). Against this backdrop, anxious Bragg customers looking for evidence of change may have been disproportionately receptive to claims that even appeared to explain perceived quality differences.

**The Katy Perry kernel of truth:** The claim that Perry was co-owner of Bragg had a genuine factual basis—she is a minority investor. When partial truths circulate alongside total falsehoods in the same claim cluster, they lend the ensemble false credibility. Audiences who verified the Perry portion and found it accurate may have extended unearned credibility to the Gates portion on the assumption that "this person got one thing right; probably the Gates part is right too." This mechanism—what might be called "truth contamination by proximity"—is a recognized feature of compound misinformation claims that contain accurate elements (Pennycook & Rand, 2019, *Psychological Science*).

## 4. The Evidence in Full

The documented ownership chain of Bragg Live Food Products can be constructed from multiple sources:

1. **Bragg's corporate FAQ page** (archived December 2023) explicitly names Swander Pace Capital as "the controlling owner," confirms Katy Perry as a minority investor personally selected by Patricia Bragg, and makes no mention of Bill Gates or any Gates-affiliated entity.

2. **Bragg company representative email** (first reported by Snopes, December 2023) provided unambiguous denial from an internal source: "Bill Gates is not an owner of Bragg."

3. **Apeel Sciences' own disclosures** confirm that early Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation grants funded research but transferred no equity.

4. **Snopes' independent investigation** of Swander Pace Capital's investment portfolio and general partner structure found no apparent Gates connection.

5. **Patricia Bragg's death** in August 2023 at age 94 removed the person who could directly confirm the ownership terms, creating an information vacuum that speculation could fill. The timing of Patricia's death and the acceleration of the rumor are closely correlated, suggesting that loss of the authoritative correction source contributed to spread. Her death was widely reported; her lifelong advocacy for apple cider vinegar as a health tonic meant she had a large and devoted following with strong emotional attachment to the brand.

Katy Perry's investor status was confirmed independently by multiple media sources including *Forbes* California business coverage in 2019, providing a public record that both validates that portion of the compound claim and isolates it from the false Gates portion.

Apeel Sciences has been approved for use by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration under its GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) framework and has received certification from independent organic certification bodies in the European Union for use on organically certified produce—a form of third-party safety validation inconsistent with the "dangerous chemical" framing that characterized the April 2023 panic.

## 5. What Remains Uncertain

Several questions adjacent to the central fact-check remain genuinely open.

**Product quality under PE ownership:** Whether Swander Pace Capital has altered Bragg's formulation since 2019 is a distinct empirical question from Gates' ownership, and one that Snopes did not investigate. Consumer reviews on multiple platforms show a bifurcated pattern: some long-term customers report perceiving no change; others report changes in taste, clarity, or potency. Objective compositional analysis of pre- and post-2019 ACV products would be required to adjudicate this. No such analysis appears to have been published in peer-reviewed form as of this writing.

**Katy Perry's investment exit status:** Perry's investment in Bragg was disclosed in 2019. Whether she remains an active investor, has increased her stake, or has exited the investment is not publicly confirmed as of 2026. The Bragg FAQ language uses present tense but may not have been updated since 2019.

**Regulatory pathway for Apeel on ACV-destined apples:** Whether Bragg's apple cider vinegar product uses apples treated with Apeel coatings before harvest or processing is not publicly confirmed. If Apeel-coated apples were used in the manufacturing process, the compound rumor's claim of a connection between Apeel and Bragg—while still not attributable to Gates—would have a factual basis at the ingredient level. This specific question has not been formally investigated.

**The EARC classification** is E (Established): the core claim is refuted by primary source documentation, direct company confirmation, and absence of any corroborating evidence for Gates' alleged ownership.

## 6. Verdict and Further Reading

**Verdict: FALSE.** Bill Gates does not own, and has never purchased, Bragg Live Food Products LLC. The controlling owner is Swander Pace Capital, a private-equity firm; minority investor Katy Perry holds a stake granted by Patricia Bragg in 2019. Apeel Sciences received philanthropic grants from the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation in its early years but these conferred no ownership; Apeel and Bragg have no confirmed supply relationship. The rumor reflects a documented pattern of misattributing food-system corporate control to Gates, amplified by a genuine kernel of celebrity investor truth, exploited name-collision confusion between two unrelated "Apeel" products, and the legitimate but unresolved consumer concern about private-equity custodianship of beloved health-food brands.
