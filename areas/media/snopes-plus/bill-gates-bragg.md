# Does Bill Gates Own Bragg Apple Cider Vinegar?

## 1. The Claim

The claim examined here is, at its most distilled, a simple Yes/No question: Does Bill Gates own the company that makes Bragg apple cider vinegar? This is the root claim from which a branching cluster of more elaborate allegations grew throughout 2023, including the associated sub-claims that Gates and singer Katy Perry jointly purchased the company, that Gates subsequently altered its ingredients, and that a food-coating company called Apeel Sciences was being used to contaminate naturally produced foods at Gates' direction. The root question was investigated by Snopes journalist Jack Izzo and published on December 20, 2023.

The vehicle for the claim's spread was diverse: TikTok videos by wellness influencers and skeptical consumer advocates, Facebook posts circulating screenshot compilations, and X (formerly Twitter) posts including a high-engagement post by influencer David Wolfe that included an image designed to visually connect Gates, Perry, Bragg, and Apeel Sciences as nodes in a food-system control narrative. The claim appeared particularly among audiences already attuned to health-food brand news, natural-wellness communities, and communities that had developed prior skepticism about Bill Gates in connection with other alleged corporate and land holdings. It received enough public attention that Snopes designated it as a separate, standalone entry rather than folding it into the longer compound investigation.

The answer is FALSE.

## 2. What's Actually True

**The Bragg ownership history:** Bragg Live Food Products LLC was founded by Paul C. Bragg in 1912 under the name Bragg's Health Food Store, making it one of the oldest continuously operated natural-health-food companies in the United States. Paul Bragg was a central figure in American naturopathic and physical-culture movements of the early and mid-twentieth century, eventually becoming closely identified with apple cider vinegar as a health product—promoting it in books, lecture tours, and product marketing over several decades. Paul's adopted daughter Patricia Bragg joined the enterprise in the 1950s, extending the brand's reach and public presence considerably. When Paul died in 1976, Patricia assumed full command and maintained it for more than four decades.

In 2019, Patricia Bragg—then approaching 90 years of age—sold the company. She retained personal control over the partner selection process and, by her own and the company's account, chose her buyers on the basis of value alignment with the Bragg family's philosophy of natural wellness. The disclosed result of that process was:

- **Swander Pace Capital** as the controlling owner. Swander Pace is a private-equity firm headquartered in San Francisco whose portfolio has historically focused on consumer-branded products in food and personal care. The firm is not publicly listed; its investors are institutional and private. There is no documented connection between Swander Pace Capital and Bill Gates, the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, or any Gates-affiliated investment vehicle.

- **Katy Perry** as a minority investor. Perry is a personal friend and neighbor of Patricia Bragg's from Santa Barbara, California. Bragg's company FAQ page uses specifically warm language to describe Perry's involvement: "yes, Patricia Bragg's lifelong friend and neighbor, Katy Perry, is an investor in Bragg. She was also hand-picked by Patricia herself. Katy Perry is not involved with any of the day-to-day operations—that's the Bragg team."

Patricia Bragg died on August 10, 2023, at age 94. She was widely celebrated as one of the most sustained personal advocates for apple cider vinegar and natural-health living in American consumer culture.

**Direct denial by Bragg:** Snopes reached out directly to Bragg Live Food Products and received email confirmation from company representative Courtney Cola: "In 2019, Patricia Bragg handpicked partners who she believed shared the values of the Bragg Family and the vision for Bragg Live Foods. That list of partners included Swander Pace Capital but did not include Bill Gates or organizations in which Gates is invested. Bill Gates is not an owner of Bragg."

This is a primary-source, on-record denial from a company spokesperson—specifically confirming not merely the absence of Gates' name from public filings, but that investigations into Gates-affiliated investment vehicles (presumably including the Gates Foundation's various subsidiary structures) found no confirming link.

**The Apeel Sciences confusion:** A separate but related claim tied Gates' supposed Bragg ownership to Apeel Sciences, a company that manufactures plant-derived preservation coatings for fresh produce. Apeel received early-stage grants from the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, but those grants did not constitute equity ownership—a distinction that Apeel Sciences co-founder Jenny Du confirmed upon inquiry. The Gates Foundation's philanthropic grant program operates entirely separately from its investment trust; grants fund research rather than acquiring equity or directional control. Apeel's GRAS certification from the FDA and its EU organic certification provide additional independent regulatory validation of the product's safety profile. Neither of those regulatory processes links Apeel to Gates-controlled corporate ownership.

## 3. Why People Believe This

The question "Does Bill Gates own [major food company]?" is not a randomly arising query. It appears within a structured misinformation ecosystem that has been building since approximately 2020, characterized by a specific set of narrative templates applied iteratively to new targets. Understanding this ecosystem reveals information about the sociology of food conspiracy claims that is both epistemically important and underexplored in popular discourse.

**The Gates farmland template and its generalization:** In 2021, Snopes investigated viral claims that Bill Gates had become the largest private farmland owner in the United States. That claim was partially true but severely distorted: Gates and his ex-wife Melinda's holdings represented a significant investment in American farmland (approximately 270,000 acres at various documented points) but did not make him the largest landholder by any standard measure and were operated as commercial agricultural investments rather than the "control of the food supply" that conspiratorial framing implied. That fact-check established a mental template—Gates buys land → Gates controls food—which subsequent claims about food companies could readily exploit. Once a audience holds a strong prior belief that an actor is purchasing food-system assets for potentially nefarious purposes, new ownership claims pass a lower verification threshold. Psychological research on prior belief effects in claim evaluation consistently finds that individuals are substantially less demanding of evidence for claims that confirm pre-held beliefs than for claims that contradict them (Ditto & Lopez, 1992, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*).

**The "Epstein effect" and guilt-by-associational celebrity:** Paula Bragg's personal friend Katy Perry did invest in the company, and that investment was confirmed and publicly disclosed. The fact that a celebrity connection existed created a credibility foothold for the claim that other notable figures might also be investors. If Perry—someone not typically associated with food-company investment—was an investor, why not Gates? This reasoning is structurally flawed (Perry's involvement neither confirms nor implies Gates' involvement), but it represents a legitimate logical pattern—appeal to precedent—applied in an illegitimate context. Once the precedent of an unusual investor is established, subsequent unusual investor claims benefit from that precedent's credibility rub-off.

**The epistemic novelty of private-equity opacity:** Swander Pace Capital is not a publicly traded company. Its ownership structure, limited-partner base, and investment mandate are not subject to the same disclosure requirements as public corporations. This opacity is structurally compatible with conspiratorial interpretation: when ownership information is genuinely difficult to verify, audiences who distrust official sources may fill the gap with motivated inference rather than withholding judgment. The appropriate response to verification difficulty is epistemic humility and escalated effort; the actual behavioral response in many audiences is escalated suspicion. Research on ambiguity aversion in belief formation suggests that in political and social domains, ambiguous evidence is often processed in the direction of pre-existing belief rather than producing genuine uncertainty (Fryer, Harms, & Jackson, 2022, *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*).

**Patricia Bragg's death as misinformation accelerant:** The timing correlation between Patricia Bragg's death in August 2023 and the intensification of ownership rumors through the autumn and December 2023 is notable. Patricia Bragg was the living authoritative source on the ownership transition: she had made the decision, she knew the partners, she had a public record of statements about the values-alignment basis for the sale. Her death removed the most credible and accessible correction source. Audiences encountering the Gates rumors after her death could not simply seek out her voice as a counterweight. The general principle—that the death of an authoritative source accelerates misinformation in domains where that source was the primary correction mechanism—is well established in historical cases but has not, to this author's knowledge, been formally quantified in contemporary social-media misinformation epidemiology.

## 4. The Evidence in Full

The evidentiary record supporting the FALSE verdict consists of:

1. **Bragg's official FAQ page** (archived December 2023): Names Swander Pace Capital as controlling owner, names Katy Perry as minority investor, mentions no other investors. Gates is absent from the list.

2. **Bragg company spokesperson email** (Courtney Cola, to Snopes): Explicitly states Gates is not an owner and that companies in which Gates is invested were also found not to be owners.

3. **Snopes' independent investigation of Swander Pace Capital**: Found no apparent financial connection to Gates or Gates-affiliated entities.

4. **Apeel Sciences official position**: Grants from Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation in early years; no equity ownership by Gates. Co-founder Jenny Du confirmed.

5. **Apeel Sciences regulatory profile**: GRAS certification (FDA), EU organic certification—demonstrating product access to official regulatory review. The "cleaning chemical safety data sheet" confusion traced to Evans Vanodine, an entirely different British company making an identically named industrial cleaner.

6. **Historical record of Gates farmland misinformation**: Snopes' existing documentation of Gates' prior misattributed ownership claims establishes the pattern in which this claim fits.

7. **Patricia Bragg's public statements** (pre-death): Multiple interviews and social media engagements in which she described the 2019 sale in terms of values alignment and personal selection of partners, without mentioning Gates.

No evidence can be found—no SEC filings, no private equity disclosures, no investigative reporting from any source—corroborating Gates' ownership.

## 5. What Remains Uncertain

**Post-acquisition Bragg product composition:** The fact-check rigorously establishes who does not own Bragg; it does not evaluate whether the product's quality or composition has changed under Swander Pace Capital's management. Consumer forums contain divided reports: some long-term users report no change; others describe changes in taste, color depth, or the behavior of the "mother" culture. Whether Swander Pace has standardized inputs, modified fermentation protocols, or changed ingredient sourcing is a separate and unresolved question.

**Katy Perry's current investor status:** Perry is confirmed as a minority investor as of the 2019 transaction. Whether she remains an investor, has increased her position, or has exited is not publicly documented as of the date of this writing.

**Long-term Apeel-Bragg supply intersection:** Whether Bragg's current apple ingredient sourcing involves any Apeel-coated apples at the input stage is not confirmed or denied by any publicly available source. This edge question—while not relating to Gates' ownership—is a fair consumer-transparency question that neither Bragg nor Apeel has specifically addressed in public documentation.

**EARC classification:** Classified E (Established). The core ownership question is answered by primary-source documentation with no countervailing evidence.

## 6. Verdict and Further Reading

**Verdict: FALSE.** Bill Gates does not own Bragg Live Food Products LLC, nor has he purchased a stake in it. The company is controlled by Swander Pace Capital, a private-equity firm, with Katy Perry holding a minority investor role personally granted by founder Patricia Bragg. Apeel Sciences received philanthropic grants from the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation but is not Gates-owned; no supply relationship between Apeel and Bragg is confirmed. The claim reflects a documented template of Gates ownership misinformation applied to a new target, amplified by celebrity investor truth-kernels, private-equity opacity, and the removal of the authoritative correction source following Patricia Bragg's death in August 2023.
