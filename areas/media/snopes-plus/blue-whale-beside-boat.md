# Does this Photo Authentically Show a Whale Alongside a Boat?


## 1. The Claim

On August 23, 2022, a photograph depicting an enormous blue whale surfacing alongside a boat circulated widely on social media, shared by the science Twitter account Massimo (operating under the handle @Rainmaker1973). The image showed what appeared to be a vast cetacean dwarfing a vessel, with the whale's body extending for what seemed a remarkable distance relative to the boat's hull. The original Twitter poster identified the animal as a blue whale and estimated its length at nearly 100 feet.

The underlying question the post prompted was straightforward but genuinely interesting: is this a real, unaltered photograph of a real blue whale swimming beside a real boat? The implicit corollary question, which the photograph immediately brought to mind for anyone who has spent time on the internet, was whether this was yet another of the digitally fabricated or composited whale images that have periodically gone viral over the years — a category that includes heavily photoshopped images placing enormous whales beneath row boats, near piers, and under major bridges in ways that violate what we know about coastal whale behavior or physical plausibility.

The photograph gained traction precisely because blue whales *are* extraordinarily large — large enough that a genuine photograph of one beside a typical vessel could easily appear manipulated to a skeptical eye. The animal's proportions, when seen alongside something of known size, are inherently difficult to process intuitively. A species that can exceed 30 meters in length and weigh up to 190 metric tonnes represents a scale of biological reality that challenges visual intuition, which is itself part of why fabricated whale images propagate so readily: they feel spectacular even to viewers who suspect exaggeration, because the truth is itself spectacular.

This entry examines whether the specific photograph in question is authentic, what its documented provenance is, what the real dimensions of blue whales actually are, and why the context of widespread fabricated whale imagery makes responsible engagement with this claim epistemically demanding.


## 2. What's Actually True

The photograph is authentic. It is drawn from a drone video filmed off Newport Beach, California, on July 3, 2016, by a videographer whose footage was distributed through the syndication service Newsflare. The Newsflare listing for the video provides descriptive metadata identifying the scene: a blue whale swimming alongside the *Nautilus*, a 72-foot whale-watching vessel operated by Newport Landing Whale Watching, a company offering naturalist-guided excursions from Newport Beach harbor.

The videographer's own annotation of the footage estimated the blue whale's length at approximately 90 feet, which would place the animal within the well-documented size range of the species. The footage was clearly not fabricated: it was submitted through a professional video syndication service with a dated record of origin, was aired during a broadcast segment by CBS Mornings in 2016, and the Newsflare video shows multiple angles consistent with genuine drone footage of an open water encounter.

The biological facts support plausibility entirely. According to the National Wildlife Federation, adult blue whales (*Balaenoptera musculus*) weigh up to 150 tons, making them the largest animals known to have ever existed on Earth. The National Wildlife Federation further identifies the typical length range as 70 to 80 feet, with the lengthiest individuals reaching 110 feet. The Whale and Dolphin Conservation organization records that the longest blue whale ever measured was a female taken at a South Atlantic whaling station in 1909, measuring slightly over 110 feet (33.58 meters). The heaviest blue whale on record was also a female, killed in 1947, weighing 418,878 pounds (approximately 190 metric tonnes) — equivalent to roughly 30 elephants or 2,500 adult humans.

Blue whales are sexually dimorphic in that females are larger than males, which is atypical among mammals and among cetaceans in particular. According to the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA), blue whales are found in all oceans except the Arctic and are among the most wide-ranging of all migratory species. The North Pacific population, to which the individual in the Newport Beach video almost certainly belongs, regularly passes through Southern California waters during summer months as whales follow the northward movement of krill aggregations along the coast.

Their enormous size is powered by an equally enormous appetite for a deceptively small prey species. Blue whales are filter feeders that use baleen plates to strain krill (*Euphausia superba* and related species) from seawater. A single blue whale may consume over 7,700 pounds (approximately 3,500 kilograms) of krill per day during peak feeding season. This dietary strategy — bulk filtration of tiny crustaceans — is what makes the blue whale's size biologically possible: krill occur in extraordinarily dense aggregations in productive cold-water upwelling zones, allowing a whale to acquire enormous caloric intake per dive.

One important qualification to the photograph's presentation is worth noting. Snopes correctly identifies the possibility that the drone footage may introduce some degree of forced perspective — a well-documented optical phenomenon in which objects in the foreground appear proportionally larger relative to objects in the background (or objects at different distances from the camera) than they would in correct spatial representation. In drone footage, the apparent size relationship between a surface object (the whale) and an object slightly farther from the camera's lens (the boat) can be distorted by the focal properties of the lens and the angle of descent. This does not mean the whale is not enormous — it clearly is — but it does mean that the apparent ratio of whale-length to boat-length in any single frame should be treated as an estimate rather than a precise measurement.

A size comparison in both the video and the photograph appears to show the vessel as approximately three-quarters the length of the whale. If the boat is 72 feet and the whale is three-quarters the length, the whale would come to roughly 96 feet. If the whale-to-boat ratio is inverted (the whale is the longer object), a 90-foot whale beside a 72-foot boat gives a ratio of approximately 1.25:1. These figures are consistent with the videographer's estimate and with documented lengths of large blue whales.

The photograph appeared to show a different angle than the primary Newsflare video (showing the whale in the bottom-left rather than bottom-right of the frame), consistent with the photograph being a still extracted from an extended or alternative angle of the same filming session, which CBS Mornings aired in 2016.


## 3. Epistemic Novelty — What the Fact-Checks Miss

The Snopes fact-check on this claim correctly identifies the photograph as authentic and provides adequate sourcing. What it does not fully develop — and what represents genuine epistemic novelty for the reader who has encountered this image — is the broader cultural and perceptual context that makes the distinction between genuine and fabricated whale photographs a continuously contested domain.

First, the existence of this specific authentic photograph must be understood against a backdrop of widespread fabricated or misleadingly contextualized whale images that target a similar audience and a similar visual register. Snopes itself links to several of these: a 2018 photograph falsely claiming to show a blue whale near a pier in North Carolina (a composite of at least two images), a false image claiming to show a blue whale photographed beneath a rowboat, and a digitally edited photograph of two whales beneath the Samuel De Champlain Bridge in Quebec. These fabrications are not random — they exploit the same intuitive implausibility-as-wonder phenomenon that makes this authentic photograph compelling. Real blue whale photographs are themselves extraordinary, and fabricated ones look like real ones, which means audiences have effectively been trained by repeated exposure to suspect any whale photograph of this type.

This creates a verification challenge that the Snopes fact-check partially addresses but does not theorize. The challenge is not purely one of determining whether a specific image has been altered; it is one of calibrating the prior probability that any given whale-size claim is authentic when the information environment contains a high density of fakes. The appropriate response to an image like this is not simply "is this photo real?" but "what characteristics distinguish authentic cetacean drone footage from composites, and do those characteristics apply here?" The answer in this case — a dated Newsflare submission, identified vessel, corroborating broadcast record, biological plausibility of the size claim — is strongly affirmative. But that methodology is more sophisticated than simply accepting or rejecting the specific image.

Second, the forced-perspective observation deserves more analytical development than the Snopes treatment provides. Forced perspective is not a defect in the photograph — it is a property of any photograph or video taken from a non-orthographic perspective where depth relations vary between subjects. Professional wildlife and cetacean researchers who use drone survey methods for size estimation must account for this carefully. Research by Christiansen et al. (2019, "Estimating Body Mass of Free-Ranging Whales Using Aerial Photogrammetry and 3D Volumetrics," *Methods in Ecology and Evolution*, 10(12), 2034–2044) has shown that drone-based photogrammetric measurement of cetacean body dimensions can achieve high accuracy when altitude, lens distortion, and horizontal distance are properly controlled — but the same research establishes that uncorrected drone footage substantially overstates or understates apparent dimensions depending on framing geometry.

This matters not as a debunking of the photograph's authenticity but as a corrective to overconfident size estimates derived from single-frame visual comparison. The whale in the Newport Beach video is almost certainly between 80 and 100 feet long, which is remarkable by any measure and requires no exaggeration. But the specific ratio visible in any given frame is a function of geometry, not just biology.

Third, the ecological significance of blue whale sightings off Newport Beach warrants far more context than either the original social media post or the Snopes fact-check provides. Southern California's Santa Barbara Channel and the waters off Newport Beach are part of one of the most productive blue whale feeding grounds in the North Pacific. Blue whales aggregate in this region seasonally to feed on krill upwelled by the California Current system. The frequency of whale-watch encounters off Southern California is high enough that Newport Landing Whale Watching and similar operators regularly see blue whales during summer months. The encounter depicted in the video is extraordinary in the visual sense but not in the ecological sense — it represents a normal feature of the Southern California marine environment, not a singular event.

This context matters because the social media framing of the photograph tends to present blue whale size as a kind of hidden secret now being revealed — as if most people would be astonished to learn these animals exist. For the millions of people who live in coastal Southern California, have taken whale-watching trips, or have followed cetacean research, this is not revelatory. The framing inadvertently implies that blue whales are rarely encountered when in fact they have robust seasonal populations in eastern Pacific waters.


## 4. Verdict

The claim is **true**. The photograph shared on Twitter on August 23, 2022, is drawn from authentic drone footage filmed off Newport Beach, California, on July 3, 2016. The blue whale is estimated at approximately 90 feet in length — a biologically credible figure — and the boat is a documented 72-foot whale-watching vessel. The footage was syndicated through Newsflare and broadcast by CBS Mornings in 2016. There is no credible basis for suspecting the image was fabricated or composited.

The forced-perspective caveat Snopes identifies is real but does not alter the fundamental verdict: blue whales of this size exist, this is a photograph of one, and the apparent proportions — a whale somewhat longer than a 72-foot vessel — are plausible given known maximum sizes of the species. The photograph is an accurate, if visually dramatic, representation of an encounter that takes place multiple times per season in Southern California waters.

Snopes verdict: **True**.


## 5. The Wider Picture

Blue whales (*Balaenoptera musculus*) hold the record for the largest animals in the known history of life on Earth. The largest known individuals, measured at whaling stations in the early twentieth century when commercial whaling provided access to complete carcasses, exceeded 33 meters (approximately 108 feet). Contemporary surveys using drone photogrammetry have confirmed that wild populations still include individuals in the 25–29 meter range, though the largest individuals found in historical whaling records are unlikely to exist today due to the population-level impacts of commercial whaling during the twentieth century, which removed an estimated 347,000 blue whales from the Southern Ocean alone between 1904 and 1967 (Branch et al., 2007, "Past and Present Distribution, Densities and Movements of Blue Whales *Balaenoptera musculus* in the Southern Hemisphere and Northern Indian Ocean," *Mammal Review*, 37(2), 116–175).

The current global blue whale population is estimated at between 10,000 and 25,000 individuals, down from pre-whaling estimates of approximately 200,000 to 300,000. The species is classified as Endangered on the IUCN Red List. The most recent assessments suggest North Pacific populations have partially recovered since the cessation of commercial whaling under the International Whaling Commission moratorium effective 1986 (Monnahan et al., 2014, "The Status of Blue Whales in the Eastern North Pacific: Fine-Scale Population Structure, Recovery, and the Influence of Prey Availability," *Endangered Species Research*, 26(1), 75–89).

Blue whales are not merely large — their physiology is remarkable in ways that have implications for understanding the upper limits of biological scale. Their hearts weigh approximately 400 pounds and are large enough for a human to crawl through the aorta. Their tongues weigh approximately 2.7 metric tonnes. They produce the loudest sounds of any animal, with vocalizations reaching up to 188 decibels measured at source — low-frequency moans and pulses used for communication across hundreds of kilometers of open ocean (Croll et al., 2001, "The Diving Behavior of Blue and Fin Whales: Is Dive Duration Limited by Muscular Oxygen Storage or Mobilization?," *Comparative Biochemistry and Physiology Part A: Molecular & Integrative Physiology*, 129(1), 103–113).

Drone photography has transformed cetacean research in the past decade by permitting size estimation, body condition assessment, and behavioral observation from above without the disturbance effects of boat approach. The same technology that produced the Newport Beach video is now routinely used in population health surveys examining the body condition of humpback, blue, and right whales in the context of climate-driven changes to prey availability (Burnett et al., 2021, "Estimating Dolphin Body Condition Using Aerial Photogrammetry," *Marine Mammal Science*, 37(3), 1048–1057).

The wider significance of the photograph, viewed from within this research context, is that it makes viscerally accessible a scale of biological reality that most people never encounter. Blue whales pass close to major population centers — Los Angeles, San Diego, the San Francisco Bay Area — but their ocean habitat means they remain almost entirely invisible to most coastal residents. Whale-watch tourism is one of the few reliable ways to experience this proximity, and the Newport Beach encounter captured in the Newsflare video is precisely the kind of event that motivates that industry and that generates genuine conservation attention.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

The Snopes fact-check by Madison Dapcevich, published August 23, 2022, correctly rates the claim as **True** and provides adequate sourcing: the Newsflare video with its dated caption identifying the vessel and location, the National Wildlife Federation's size data for blue whales, and the Whale and Dolphin Conservation record-breaking statistics. The check also usefully flags the forced-perspective caveat and distinguishes this photograph from several fabricated whale images that have previously circulated.

What the fact-check does not do is examine the methodological question of how one distinguishes authentic cetacean drone footage from composites, beyond noting that Snopes "has no reason to believe" the footage is fabricated. This is a legitimate epistemic position — absence of disconfirming evidence — but it is weaker than positive provenance confirmation. In this case, the provenance is strong (dated Newsflare submission, named vessel, broadcast record), but a more explicit checklist of provenance-verification steps would add instructional value for readers who will encounter similar images in the future.

The fact-check also does not engage with the ecological context of Southern California blue whale populations, which would help readers understand whether this kind of encounter is anomalous or routine. The framing of the original tweet treated the whale's size as astonishing, which it is; but the encounter itself is not unprecedented and does not indicate an unusual oceanic event.

For fact-checking purposes, the key principles demonstrated by this case are: (1) dated video syndication records provide strong provenance evidence; (2) named vessels with known dimensions allow approximate scale verification; (3) forced perspective should always be flagged in drone footage comparisons; and (4) authentic photographs of genuinely extraordinary animals exist within a landscape of fakes and should be verified through provenance rather than purely through visual analysis.

The Snopes treatment is adequate for its primary purpose (confirming the photograph's authenticity) but does not fully realize the educational opportunity the claim presents regarding cetacean biology, conservation status, and the epistemics of visual verification in an information environment rich with fabricated animal imagery.
