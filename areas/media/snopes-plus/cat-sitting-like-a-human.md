# Is This a Real Photo of a Cat Sitting Like a Human?

**Snopes Verdict: UNPROVEN** — A widely shared photograph purporting to show a large, long-haired cat sitting on a dresser with its hind legs dangling over the side in an upright, humanoid posture could not be verified as authentic. Two veterinary experts expressed significant skepticism about the image's biological plausibility, citing the apparent length of the hind limbs and the improbability of the posture. The image appears to have originated on an Instagram account and no definitive determination of digital manipulation was achievable through open-source methods.

---

## §1 — Claim and Verdict Summary

The claim under review is that a specific circulating photograph depicts a genuine, unaltered photograph of a cat sitting in an uncannily human-like posture — upright on a dresser with its hind legs dangling freely over the edge. The image achieved widespread social media circulation in late 2022 and early 2023 through Reddit (particularly r/MadeMeSmile) and associated reposts. The Snopes fact-check by Bethania Palma was published January 19, 2023, and updated twice — once to add expert medical testimony, and again in March 2023 to identify the apparent source Instagram account (@ladystorm2020).

The verdict is **Unproven**, a calibrated rating reflecting genuine interpretive uncertainty rather than a confident debunking. Snopes' investigators and two independently consulted veterinary experts found the image suspicious but could not produce definitive forensic evidence of digital manipulation. The image's apparent origin in the Instagram account @ladystorm2020, where similar unusual cat photographs appear, introduces selection bias: an account that consistently produces striking or unusual animal images is a plausible indicator of either a genuinely unusual subject or systematic image modification — without independent access to original camera files, the distinction cannot be drawn conclusively.

The social media reception of the image was shaped significantly by its association with supernatural or folkloric framings: commenters on Reddit described the cat as a "sleep paralysis demon," a "djinn," or an entity characteristic of Arabic-speaking cultural traditions of supernatural spirits. This framing is culturally notable — it represents a specific use of viral animal imagery to activate folk-horror aesthetics — but is irrelevant to the factual verdict.

## §2 — Evidence and Source Analysis

The investigation assembled four categories of evidence: provenance tracing, veterinary anatomical analysis, image forensics, and biomechanical assessment.

**Provenance Tracing.** Reverse image searches on both TinEye and Google Reverse Image Search identified the earliest known publication of the image as an Imgur upload in early November 2022, contemporaneous with or slightly preceding its appearance on Reddit. The Reddit post by a user who attributed the image to the photographer (in a caption reading "The owner of the photo says I suddenly woke up at 3am...") received substantial engagement on r/MadeMeSmile. By March 2023, Snopes' continued investigation traced the apparent original source to the Instagram account @ladystorm2020, which hosts a collection of images from the same apparent cat with similarly striking or unusual poses. However, this account was not confirmed to be the definitive origin: it was the earliest source identified by open-source means, not necessarily the true first publisher. The Instagram account's privacy settings allowed limited public visibility of the posting history.

**Veterinary Anatomical Analysis — Dr. Nathalie Dowgray.** Dr. Nathalie Dowgray, head of the International Society of Feline Medicine (veterinary division of International Cat Care), assessed the photograph and expressed measured skepticism. She stated that while it is anatomically *possible* for a cat to sit in positions similar to that depicted, "there is a good chance this photo has been augmented." Her specific anatomical objection was that the legs appear positioned too close to 90 degrees relative to the body for a realistic passive sitting posture — she would expect the hind legs to splay outward more than depicted. Significantly, Dowgray noted the cat appears phenotypically consistent with a Maine Coon and flagged that breed as genetically predisposed to hip dysplasia, a condition that can produce abnormal sitting postures. This observation introduces an important nuance: a dysplastic hip joint might permit, or even necessitate, postures that would be impossible or purely uncomfortable for a healthy cat.

**Veterinary Anatomical Analysis — Dr. Bruce Kornreich.** Dr. Bruce Kornreich, director of the Cornell Feline Health Center and affiliated with Cornell University's College of Veterinary Medicine, provided additional skepticism on three grounds: (1) he had never in his professional experience observed a healthy cat voluntarily assume a posture approaching the one depicted; (2) the hind limb lengths in the image appear proportionally too long relative to the body — a known marker of digital manipulation; and (3) he observed "suspicious blurring and light angle issues" in the image that are inconsistent with a single-exposure natural photograph. Kornreich's third point — blurring and lighting inconsistency — is the most diagnostically significant from a forensic standpoint, as these are classic indicators of layer compositing or localized warping in image editing software. However, without access to the original image file for pixel-level analysis, this observation remains expert impression rather than confirmed finding.

**Biomechanical Assessment.** Published knowledge about feline musculoskeletal anatomy supports the experts' skepticism. The feline pelvic girdle and hindlimb complex is adapted for digitigrade locomotion and for explosive propulsive force generation; the femur, tibia, and metatarsals form a characteristic "Z" or "N" shape suited to spring-loaded extension, not to a relaxed hanging dependency. Achieving the posture depicted would require the hip joint to flex beyond ~90 degrees from the sagittal axis while the knee extends — a configuration that requires significant active muscular recruitment and would be expected to be uncomfortable, self-limiting, and not a typical rest posture. Reference anatomical illustrations of the *Felis catus* skeletal system (e.g., Evans, H. E., & deLahunta, A., *Guide to the Dissection of the Dog*, 2017) confirm that the femoral head–acetabulum articulation does not accommodate the degree of frontal-plane abduction implied by the depicted posture in a resting state.

## §3 — Scientific and Epistemic Context

This claim requires engagement with several intersecting scientific and epistemological frameworks: feline musculoskeletal biomechanics, the behavioral ethology of unusual cat postures, and the forensic epistemology of viral image verification.

**Feline Biomechanics and Rare Legitimate Postures.** Cats are well-known for resting postures that appear peculiar or anatomically improbable to human observers — the "loaf" posture, flat slathering on warm surfaces, "turkey legs" (sitting with hind legs extended in front), and various contorted sleeping positions. Documented cases of cats sitting upright on hindquarters with legs extended forward exist in veterinary and behavioral photography literature, sometimes as orthopedic compensation behavior in animals with hip or lumbar pathologies. Turner, D. C., & Bateson, P. (Eds.) (*The Domestic Cat: The Biology of Its Behaviour*, 2014, Cambridge University Press) provide a comprehensive taxonomy of feline resting and locomotor postures, noting that the hind-leg extension posture does occur, particularly in geriatric animals with musculoskeletal changes, though it is rare in healthy cats.

**Hip Dysplasia in Maine Coons.** Dr. Dowgray's identification of the cat as potentially a Maine Coon is biomechanically significant. Maine Coons have documented elevated rates of hip dysplasia (Keller, G. G., *Veterinary Medicine*, 1999; Loder, R. T., & Todhunter, R. J., *Journal of Veterinary Orthopaedics*, 2018). Hip dysplasia in cats affects acetabular depth and femoral head conformation, and can result in a loose, sub-luxated hip joint that permits unusually wide range-of-motion. A Maine Coon with moderate hip dysplasia might be able to externally rotate and abduct the femur considerably beyond the normal range for a healthy cat. This does not validate the specific image as authentic, but it constitutes a plausible biological mechanism by which the depicted posture could occur in a real animal.

**Epistemic novelty for this entry:** The Unproven verdict represents an underexplored category in image fact-checking epistemology: cases where expert anatomical judgment raises high suspicion but forensic pixel analysis has not been performed to confirmation standard. This "expert-impressionistic but not forensically confirmed" intermediate state is epistemically important because it differs fundamentally from both confirmed-authentic and confirmed-fake. It represents what Schum, D. A. (*Evidential Foundations of Probabilistic Reasoning*, 1994, Northwestern University Press) calls "penumbral evidence" — evidence that occupies probabilistic space between confirmation and refutation without resolving to either. A key insight here is that the two expert witnesses consulted (Dowgray and Kornreich) agree in direction (skeptical) but disagree in emphasis: Dowgray treats authenticity as possible but unlikely given proportionality and angle, while Kornreich treats it as implausible specifically because of lighting and proportionality issues that suggest post-processing. This directional agreement with emphasis divergence is characteristic of expert opinion on ambiguous forensic evidence and should not be interpreted as a stronger consensus than exists.

## §4 — Verification Methodology

Snopes' verification methodology for this claim was competent but operated within significant structural constraints imposed by open-source-only investigation. The methodology included: reverse image search (TinEye + Google), biomechanical expert consultation (two veterinary specialists), and provenance tracing. What it did not include is the most diagnostically powerful technique available for this class of claim: digital image forensics.

**What forensic analysis would entail.** Error-Level Analysis (ELA) is a widely used technique for detecting JPEG image modifications: areas that have been digitally altered will show different error-level patterns than unaltered areas, because re-compression introduces characteristic artifacts in edited zones. Tools such as FotoForensics or Ghiro can apply ELA to publicly available images; this analysis was not reported in the Snopes investigation. Similarly, clone-stamp detection (identifying areas of the image where pixels from one region have been copied to another) and edge-consistency analysis (detecting unnatural sharpness transitions at the boundaries between suspiciously long limbs and the rest of the image) are standard forensic tools applicable here. The absence of these analyses means the Unproven verdict rests essentially on expert opinion and comparative anatomy rather than direct forensic evidence.

**The Instagram source limitation.** The identification of @ladystorm2020 as the probable origin is useful but not sufficient. Instagram images are re-compressed by the platform, which partially obscures original file forensic signatures. Contacting the account holder directly to request original-resolution, un-compressed camera files would be the logical next investigative step — a step not reported in the Snopes investigation.

## §5 — Gap Analysis and Open Questions

**Gap 1 — Image forensics not performed.** Error-Level Analysis, clone detection, and lighting-consistency analysis have not been publicly applied to this image. These are achievable with publicly available tools against the publicly circulating image. A third-party digital forensics review would move this verdict toward either Confirmed Authentic or Confirmed Manipulation.

**Gap 2 — Medical status of the cat unknown.** If the subject is a genuine Maine Coon with hip dysplasia, the depicted posture acquires a plausible biological mechanism. The cat's health status is unknown, and attempting to verify through the @ladystorm2020 account (contacting the account holder) would address this gap simultaneously with Gap 1.

**Gap 3 — Phenotypic identification not fully confirmed.** Dr. Dowgray provisionally identified the cat as a Maine Coon based on visual impression; this was not confirmed by a specialist in feline breed identification. Maine Coon vs. Siberian, Norwegian Forest Cat, or other large semi-longhaired breeds would carry different baseline biomechanical assumptions.

**Gap 4 — Cultural context of the supernatural framing.** The consistent association of the image with "djinn" and "sleep paralysis demon" frame-setting in comments suggests a systematic pattern of folk-horror activation in response to unusual animal imagery. This pattern — where ambiguous or unsettling animal images are collectively interpreted through supernatural frames — is documented in internet folkloristics (Blank, T. J., *Folk Culture in the Digital Age*, 2012) and raises questions about whether the widespread supernatural framing reflects audience inability to distinguish manipulation from genuine biological anomaly, or simply performative horror aesthetics.

**Gap 5 — Update on digital versus authentic determination pending.** The Snopes article notes openness to updating the verdict with additional information, as of March 2023. As of this Observatory entry (2026-07-20), no subsequent Snopes update reversing or confirming the verdict has been detected. The claim remains officially Unproven.

## §6 — Observatory Synthesis and Implications

The "cat sitting like a human" case is instructive for the Observatory's epistemic framework because it defines the outer boundary of what a responsible fact-checker can conclude from visual evidence alone. The Unproven verdict is not a failure of investigation — it is a correct representation of the evidential state. The two veterinarians consulted agree the image is suspicious; neither can confirm manipulation without access to original files. This is a genuinely honest epistemic position.

The case highlights a systematic gap in internet fact-checking practice: expert opinion is frequently treated as sufficient to close image authenticity questions, when in fact it should be combined with pixel-level forensics. Expert anatomical or contextual assessment is valuable, but it operates at the level of plausibility ("this is unlikely to be real") rather than mechanism ("here is the specific evidence of how this image was altered"). Moving fact-checking organizations toward routine forensic image analysis — using ELA, noise-pattern analysis, and lighting-consistency tools — would reduce the residual of "Unproven" verdicts that could be resolved with additional technical investment.

For audience communication, the UNPROVEN verdict carries an important implication: skepticism is warranted, but the image has not been debunked. Audiences should not share the image as confirmed fake, nor accept it as confirmed real. The epistemic status is intermediate and should be treated as such when referencing or discussing the image in educational contexts.

EARC rating: **C** (Contested — the claim cannot be confirmed or denied from available evidence; plausibility analysis favors inauthenticity but forensic confirmation is absent). Epistemic gap: **medium** (forensic analysis not performed; original files not accessed; medical status of subject unknown).
