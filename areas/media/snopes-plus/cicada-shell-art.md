# Are These Photos of Cicada Shell Art Real?


## 1. The Claim

In May 2021, as Brood X periodical cicadas began their seventeen-year emergence across the eastern United States — an event anticipated years in advance by entomologists and periodically covered by the media as a natural spectacle of significant ecological and cultural interest — a collage of photographs began circulating on Twitter, Reddit, and other social media platforms. The images showed striking sculptures: a Transformer-like robotic figure, an Alien Baltan (the arthropod-like villain from the Japanese television series *Ultraman*), a Godzilla-like monster, and generic humanoid and creature forms, all apparently constructed from cicada exoskeletons. The photographs were circulated in the context of the Brood X emergence, with framing that implied these sculptures were being made from freshly emerged Brood X shells.

The claim examined by Snopes, published May 24, 2021 and authored by Dan Evon, is that the photographs show genuine sculptures made from cicada shells. The rating is **True**: the sculptures are real, the photographs are authentic, and the artworks were genuinely constructed from cicada exoskeletons (the shed nymph casings left on tree bark and other surfaces when cicada nymphs complete their final molt to adult form). The qualification is that none of the images were created with Brood X shells — they predate the 2021 emergence by three to four years and were made by Japanese artists working in Japan with locally collected shells from Japanese cicada species.

This case belongs to a category of viral art-and-nature intersection content that generates a verification question not of deception or fabrication but of provenance and context: the images are what they appear to be at a propositional level ("sculptures made of cicada shells"), but the implied contemporary and geographic context embedded in their circulation alongside Brood X coverage was inaccurate.


## 2. What's Actually True

The sculptures are documented as genuine works by two primary Japanese artists, with a third briefly mentioned.

**@ride_hero_'s monster sculpture (2017)**: The Transformer-like robotic monster visible in the top-left position of the widely circulated collage was created in 2017 by a Japanese art student who posts under the Twitter handle @ride_hero_. The artist documented the work on a personal portfolio site, providing both the finished sculpture photographs and the conceptual framing behind the work. The artist's statement (translated from Japanese) articulates a philosophical engagement with the cicada exuvia as a material that carries the trace of its previous occupant — a "soul vessel" in the artist's framing, connecting to Japanese Buddhist and Shinto conceptions of material-spiritual continuity. The artist describes the specific impetus for cicada shell work as a moment in high school when they accidentally crushed a shed cicada shell in a corridor and experienced the Japanese sensation of *mottainai* — roughly, the feeling that something of intrinsic value has been wasted — which motivated a practice of collecting and transforming shells rather than letting them decompose anonymously.

The artist's statement situates the work within a tradition of Japanese thinking about monsters (*kaijū* and *yokai*) as image-entities that exist primarily in the perceptual and imaginative sphere rather than in material reality: the monster is a soul-form, and the body that contains it is secondary. Using cicada shells — which are literally the evacuated former bodies of living animals — as the material for monster-body sculpture is a direct enactment of this conceptual claim. The cicada exuvia are chosen precisely because they are bodies without souls, vessels that carried a living animal and now retain only its shape. The artist's @ride_hero_ account includes process documentation showing collections of shells and assembly stages.

**Tanikurakai's sculptures (2018)**: The other three images in the collage — the Alien Baltan, the Godzilla, and the "Alien" figure — were created by a Japanese college student identified in Buzzfeed News Japan coverage from 2018 as Tanikurakai. The Alien Baltan piece attracted significant attention in Japanese social media in 2018 after Tanikurakai posted images of it; the Buzzfeed Japan interview provides direct attribution and process detail. Tanikurakai described using approximately 30 shells for the Alien Baltan, drawing from three Japanese cicada species: the large bear cicada (*Graptopsaltria nigrofuscata*, the largest Japanese cicada and the species Tanikurakai noted for providing volume); a slender brown species used for proportional adjustment; and the small and structurally fragile Kempfer cicada (likely *Mogana kempferi*) used as a detail element for facial components. The sculpture was completed in approximately one week after shell collection in the garden of the artist's residence.

The choice of Alien Baltan as a subject for cicada shell sculpture is culturally resonant rather than arbitrary: Alien Baltan is the most recognized villain in the *Ultraman* franchise (Tsuburaya Productions, 1966–present), and its character design is explicitly modeled on crustaceans and arthropods, featuring large lobster-like claws, a segmented body, and a crustacean-inspired head with compound-eye structures. Using cicada shells — the exoskeletons of insects, which share the basic arthropod body plan — to construct an arthropod-themed alien figure enacts a material-concept coherence that is visually and conceptually satisfying. The result, documented in photographs, is an object that appears designed at professional level but was produced by a college student working with collected natural materials.

**Zhang Zhenwu**: ChinaDaily.com is cited in the Snopes fact-check as reporting on Shanghai artist Zhang Zhenwu, who also works with cicada shell sculpture. This case is distinguished from the Japanese examples by professional artist context rather than student work, but the substantive practice — using shed cicada exuviae as a sculptural medium — is the same.

The cicada exuvia itself as a physical material has structural properties worth noting. Insect cuticle is a composite biological material consisting primarily of chitin (a polysaccharide polymer) cross-linked with proteins, which in the hardened (sclerotized) regions of the exoskeleton is further tanned through catecholamine crosslinking reactions that increase rigidity and resistance to degradation significantly (Neville, 1975, *Biology of the Arthropod Cuticle*, Springer). The shed nymph exuvia retains the full surface topography of the instar that produced it — all facets of the compound eyes, all tarsal segmentation, all surface macrotrichiae — in a lightweight, relatively durable form. The material properties of cicada exuvia — rigid but workable, molecularly detailed at the surface, light enough to be assembled into three-dimensional structures without requiring supports — make them technically suitable as a sculptural medium in ways that differ substantially from other natural waste materials.


## 3. Why the Context Matters and Why People Misread It

The circulation of these photographs during the Brood X emergence of 2021 generated a specific contextual misreading that, while not constituting outright misinformation, nonetheless conveyed an inaccurate temporal and geographic impression. Viewers who encountered the collage in the context of Brood X news coverage were likely to infer that the sculptures were made from Brood X shells, that American artists or cicada-emergence enthusiasts were making them in real time, and that this represented a new cultural practice inspired by the specific 2021 emergence event. All three inferences are incorrect: the artworks are Japanese, date from 2017 and 2018, and reflect Japanese artistic traditions rather than an American response to a periodical cicada emergence.

The misattribution dynamic here is subtle and deserves careful characterization. The photographs were not deliberately presented as Brood X art; the Twitter post that Snopes fact-checked simply showed the images with a caption like "cicada sculpture art," without claiming they were new or American. The contextual contamination was provided by the ambient media environment at the time of sharing: May 2021 was saturated with Brood X content, and viewers processed the images within that frame. This is an example of what cognitive psychologists call contextual source monitoring error — the misattribution of the sources of associated content when multiple streams of similar-category information are processed in temporal proximity (Johnson et al., 1993, *Psychological Bulletin*).

**Epistemic Novelty — Brood X as a Viral Context Amplifier and the Temporal Flattening Problem**: The 2021 Brood X emergence was itself a media event of unusual proportions, generating extensive advance coverage beginning months before the emergence commenced, intensive documentation during the emergence (citizen science platforms like iNaturalist recorded millions of observations within weeks), and a wave of cultural production that included journalism, photography, documentary coverage, and social media content from amateur and professional naturalists. This dense information environment created a powerful contextual attractor that pulled proximate content — including the 2017 and 2018 Japanese cicada art — into its frame through temporal and topical proximity.

The broader epistemological phenomenon at work is temporal flattening: social media circulation systematically strips content of its original temporal context. A photograph posted to Twitter or shared in a Reddit thread carries no inherent timestamp visible to casual viewers, and the recirculation of archival images as contextually relevant to present events is a structural feature of social media information ecology rather than an exceptional pathology. The viral dynamics of information recirculation — in which images are shared because they are visually striking and contextually resonant, not because their provenance has been verified — means that content created years or continents away will routinely be recontextualized by ambient media environments in ways that produce inaccurate temporal and geographic impressions.

The Brood X emergence offers a particularly clear case because the cicada emergence event was so precisely timed and spatially bounded (eastern North America, late May 2021) that any image associating cicada content with recent emergence necessarily implied American, 2021 context. The Japanese cicada art had no such specificity in its original posting context but acquired apparent temporal and geographic specificity through its redeployment alongside Brood X news content.

This temporal flattening problem is one of the most significant structural challenges for visual fact-checking. Reverse image search tools (Google Images, TinEye, CrowdTangle) can in principle recover original posting context, but these tools are not integrated into the average user's media consumption workflow, and the proportion of social media users who routinely verify the provenance of viral images is estimated to be quite small. The Snopes fact-check performs exactly this provenance recovery function, but it operates downstream of the viral spread: by the time the fact-check is published, the images have typically already completed most of their circulation cycle.


## 4. Verdict

**Rating: TRUE (with important provenance context)**

The photographs of cicada shell sculptures are genuine and show real artworks made from real cicada exoskeletons. The artworks were created by Japanese artists — @ride_hero_ in 2017 and Tanikurakai in 2018 — using Japanese cicada species, and have no connection to the 2021 Brood X cicada emergence with which they were implicitly associated during their viral circulation period. The photographs themselves are authentic and unmanipulated. The sculptures are extraordinary examples of found-material insect exoskeleton sculpture.

**EARC Mode: E — Empirically Confirmed**

The artworks are documented with direct attribution to identified artists, process photography, and contemporaneous first-person accounts. The biological materials used are accurately characterized; the structural and aesthetic rationale provided by the artists is well-documented in translated accounts.

**Gap Category: Authentic Art, Misattributed Timing**

This entry addresses a category of viral content in which the artifact itself is genuine but the implied context — temporal, geographic, or causal — is inaccurate due to recirculation in an ambient information environment that imposes a new frame on older content.


## 5. The Wider Picture

Cicadas as a biological and cultural phenomenon occupy an unusual position in both entomology and in the anthropology of human-insect relations. The periodical cicadas of the genus *Magicicada* — the North American species responsible for Brood X and twelve other numbered broods — have one of the most distinctive life history patterns of any animal on Earth: prime-number developmental cycles (13 or 17 years) spent underground as root-feeding nymphs, followed by a brief intense adult phase of approximately 4 to 6 weeks focused entirely on reproduction. The prime-number periodicity is hypothesized to be an evolved response to predator saturation: by emerging in prime-year intervals, periodical cicadas reduce the probability of synchronizing with predator population cycles, exceeding the saturation capacity of all present predators and ensuring that a proportion of adults survive to reproduce (Lloyd & Dybas, 1966, *Evolution*). This "predator satiation" strategy is the accepted evolutionary explanation for the periodicity, though additional hypotheses involving competition between broods have also been proposed (Yoshimura, 1997, *American Naturalist*).

The annual cicadas of East Asia — members of genera including *Graptopsaltria*, *Cryptotympana*, *Platypleura*, and many others — do not follow the periodical pattern; they emerge annually with predictable summer timing and are a defining sensory feature of the Japanese summer soundscape, integrated into haiku poetry, art, and seasonal cultural practice for centuries. The cicada (Japanese: *semi*) and its shed exuvia (*nukegara*) carry extensive cultural symbolism in Japanese tradition: the exuvia in particular is associated with the Buddhist concept of *se* (molt/transformation), with transience and impermanence (*mono no aware*), and in some traditions with concepts of resurrection and the soul departing the body. *Nukegara* appear in classical haiku as a kigo (season word), and the image of an empty, abandoned cicada shell is a frequent metaphor in classical Japanese poetry for the emptied vessel of a life spent (Ueda, 1991, *The Path of Flowering Thorn: The Life and Poetry of Yosa Buson*, Stanford University Press).

This cultural tradition provides direct context for the conceptual framing that @ride_hero_ articulated in the artist's statement: the choice to use cicada shells as material for monster-body sculpture is explicitly connected to Japanese thinking about souls and their material residues, a framework with deep roots in classical literature and visual art. The *mottainai* impulse that the artist identified — the feeling that the empty exuvia represents a value that should not simply be wasted but transformed and perpetuated — is itself a recognized Japanese cultural concept (the "3Rs" of Reduce, Reuse, Recycle have been associated with *mottainai* in Japanese environmental campaigns) that operates here at the intersection of ecological and aesthetic sensibility.

The biology of the molt itself is spectacular enough to deserve attention beyond its role as art medium. The cicada nymph, after years underground, tunnels to the surface and climbs the nearest vertical structure. The thoracic cuticle then splits along a pre-formed medial dorsal suture, and the adult cicada emerges from this split, pumps hemolymph into its wings over the course of approximately one to two hours, hardens and darkens its new cuticle through sclerotization, and subsequently abandons the exuvia permanently. The exuvia retains the complete three-dimensional form of the nymph's final instar, including the structural detail of the tracheal system (preserved as cast tubes), the eye facet topography, and all external appendage morphology — a three-dimensional cast of the living animal formed of biological composite material. The material quality that makes exuvia suitable for sculpture — rigidity, surface detail, lightweight — is a direct consequence of the biology of the molt process, in which the cuticle must be both precisely shaped to the previous body form and durable enough to hold that form through handling and physical disruption after abandonment.

The use of multiple cicada species to achieve different sculptural properties in Tanikurakai's work — the large bear cicada for volume, a slender species for proportional refinement, the Kempfer cicada for detail — represents an intuitive material engineering approach that exploits the known morphological differences between Japanese cicada species, which range from roughly 35 mm (*Mogana kempferi*) to over 60 mm (*Graptopsaltria nigrofuscata*) in body length and differ substantially in body proportions, cuticle thickness, and structural rigidity.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

The Snopes entry by Dan Evon is exemplary in its handling of the provenance recovery function. It correctly identifies all visible artworks in the collage, traces each to a specific named artist with supporting documentation (the @ride_hero_ portfolio site, the Buzzfeed Japan interview with Tanikurakai, the ChinaDaily reference to Zhang Zhenwu), and provides translated excerpts from the primary source materials that give direct first-person artist context. The key qualifying statement — that none of the images feature Brood X shells and all predate the 2021 emergence — is clearly articulated.

The translation-derived artist statement from @ride_hero_ is particularly valuable as primary source material, and Evon's decision to include it at length rather than merely summarizing it allows the artist's conceptual framework to speak directly. This is a good instinct in fact-checks where the primary interest is in the authentic provenance and meaning of genuine material: the artist's statement about *mottainai*, souls, and empty vessel-bodies provides more conceptual depth than any paraphrase could achieve, and it transforms the fact-check from a neutral provenance confirmation into a genuine encounter with a specific and thoughtful creative practice.

The broader category lesson this entry illustrates is the value of temporal disambiguation in viral content fact-checking. The standard fact-check verdict categories (True, False, Misleading, Miscaptioned, etc.) do not have a specific category for "authentic content, inaccurate implied temporal context" — which is what the cicada art images represent. The "True" verdict is technically accurate (the sculptures are real) but requires the substantial qualification provided in the fact-check body. The absence of a dedicated verdict category for this type of contextual displacement — content that is authentic in itself but inaccurately framed by its circulation context — reflects a general gap in the categorical architecture of fact-checking that becomes more pressing as the volume of recirculated archival content integrated into contemporary media streams continues to grow.

The cicada shell art case is also a useful positive example for conservation and natural history communication: it demonstrates that viral content about insects can, when handled well, create high-engagement entry points into entomological biology (periodical cicada life cycles, exuvia formation, species morphology), cultural history (Japanese *semi* traditions, *mottainai*, kaijū aesthetics), and material science (insect cuticle composition and structural properties) simultaneously. The challenge for science communicators is to recognize these moments as they pass through high-engagement viral windows and attach substantive educational elaboration before the attention cycle moves on.
