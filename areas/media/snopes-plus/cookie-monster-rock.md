## §1 — Claim and Context

In January 2021, photographs began circulating widely on social media purportedly showing two halves of a rock — cracked open to reveal an interior whose blue crystal formation bore a striking resemblance to Cookie Monster, the beloved Muppet from the American children's television programme *Sesame Street*. The images showed blue amethyst or chalcedony crystals arranged in a pattern mimicking the character's wide, googly eyes and open mouth.

On January 26, 2021, Snopes fact-checker Dan Evon published a verification of the claim. His investigation confirmed that the specimen was genuine: a volcanic rock (agate or geode subtype) discovered by Lucas Fassari in Brazil and brought to wider attention by geologist Mike Bowers, who posted a video of the specimen being split open on his Facebook page. Snopes rated the claim **True**.

The claim requires two distinct components to be true: (1) the photograph is not doctored — the geological specimen actually contains the described formation; and (2) the geological formation described is physically possible — i.e., naturally occurring mineral crystals can arrange themselves into patterns resembling human or humanoid faces. Both components are evaluated below.

The broader context is one of a recurring phenomenon in popular science media: striking geological specimens with anthropomorphic or zoomorphic pareidolic patterns go viral on social media, prompting both genuine wonder and skepticism. Earlier famous examples include the Martian "Face on Mars" (a natural mesa formation photographed by Viking 1 in 1976, later revealed as a natural landform — Carlotto, 1988, *Applied Optics*), various "face" formations in agate slabs sold in rock and mineral shops worldwide, and a 2019 "Owl" agate that preceded the Cookie Monster specimen in viral attention.

## §2 — Verification Methodology

Evon's verification approach is direct and appropriate. The primary evidence chain is:

1. Geologist Mike Bowers posted a video on Facebook showing the specimen being cracked open — the "reveal" format that made the claim credible as a natural specimen. Video documentation of this type is significantly harder to fake than static photography.

2. Bowers stated that the specimen was discovered by Lucas Fassari in Brazil. Brazil is the world's leading source of commercial agate and amethyst geodes, primarily from the state of Rio Grande do Sul; this provenance is geographically consistent.

3. Bowers, as a named geologist, provided expert characterisation of the specimen as an agate with an unusually complete and well-defined pareidolic pattern, noting: "I have seen others but here you have it complete on both sides. This is very unusual." This expert testimony establishes both that the specimen is genuine and that its quality is exceptional within the universe of pareidolic agate specimens.

4. Snopes independently sourced the photograph and traced its viral propagation, finding no evidence the image was digitally altered.

The verification does not constitute laboratory analysis (no mineralogical assay, spectroscopic analysis, or independent geologist examination is cited). For the purposes of the claim's complexity — a photograph of a rock — the video documentation and named expert attestation constitute reasonable evidence. Digital manipulation of video is possible but significantly more resource-intensive than photograph doctoring, and the video format showing the specimen being physically cracked is an implicit anti-fakery measure.

## §3 — Epistemic Novelty

The epistemic novelty of this fact-check is modest at first appearance — it is simply confirming that a photograph is genuine — but several deeper threads warrant unpacking.

**The phenomenology of geological pareidolia.** The Cookie Monster rock is an instance of pareidolia in geological context, and its epistemic significance lies partly in why such instances attract intense public attention. Pareidolia — the perception of meaningful patterns (particularly faces) in random or ambiguous visual stimuli — is not a quirk but a core feature of human visual cognition. The human face-detection system (specifically the fusiform face area in the temporal lobe) operates on a hair trigger, generating face percepts from minimal and ambiguous stimuli (Kanwisher, McDermott, and Chun, 1997, *Journal of Neuroscience*). This cognitive architecture means that any geological specimen with two roughly circular features positioned above a curved opening will activate face recognition in most observers. The unusual element is not that humans see Cookie Monster in the rock; it is that the rock's internal crystallisation happened to produce a configuration that so precisely matches the specific character's canonical visual features — round blue eyes on stalks above a wide open mouth — as to be immediately recognisable rather than merely suggestive.

**The agate as a geological record.** The aesthetic properties that make agates visually interesting are themselves a geological archive: the banding patterns, colour zones, and crystal inclusions record sequential episodes of groundwater intrusion with varying mineral chemistry. The Cookie Monster agate's blue colouration represents copper or iron impurities (or amethyst-grade silicon dioxide under particular oxidation conditions) that entered the cavity at a specific point in the deposition sequence, creating a blue stratum precisely positioned to mimic eyes and mouth. This is a coincidence in the strict scientific sense — neither cause nor design was involved — but the depth of geological time and the vastness of agate specimen numbers worldwide make such coincidences essentially certain to occur many times over.

**Viral mechanics and the "reveal" format.** The Cookie Monster rock's viral propagation was substantially driven by Bowers's choice to present the specimen as a video reveal — showing first an undistinguished exterior, then the dramatic interior. This narrative format activates anticipatory cognition in viewers and creates a satisfying "payoff" that plain static photography cannot replicate. The viral mechanics are themselves epistemically interesting: the same specimen photographed as a flat cross-section would have received a fraction of the attention. The medium shaped the message's reach and, consequently, the scope of the fact-checking demand.

## §4 — Empirical Evidence

**On agate formation:**  
Agates are a microcrystalline variety of silica (SiO₂), belonging to the chalcedony family. They form in the cavities and vesicles of volcanic rocks — typically basalt or andesite — when silica-rich groundwater percolates through porous rock and deposits successive layers of silica gel that subsequently crystallise. The characteristic banding of agates reflects this episodic deposition: each band represents a distinct phase of groundwater infiltration with a specific mineral chemistry (Götze et al., 2020, *Crystal Growth & Design*, 20: 5359–5374). The blue colouration in the Cookie Monster specimen likely reflects amethystine quartz (violet-blue from iron impurities and irradiation) or chalcedony with trace copper and iron inclusions.

Geode subtype specimens — cavities lined with macrocrystalline quartz rather than microcrystalline chalcedony — form when silica concentration during late-stage deposition allows larger crystal growth inward from the cavity walls. The combination of chalcedony banding (producing face-like contours) and drusy crystal interiors (producing sparkle and textural interest) is what makes agate geodes particularly striking as specimens.

**On Brazil as the origin of the specimen:**  
Brazil is the world's dominant commercial source of agate, amethyst, and agate geodes. The state of Rio Grande do Sul contains the Paraná volcanic province, a flood basalt plateau that erupted approximately 130–135 million years ago during the Cretaceous. The scale of this formation — covering approximately one million square kilometres across southern Brazil, Uruguay, and Paraguay — produced an extraordinary number of vesicular cavities subsequently filled by hydrothermal silica, creating the world's largest known agate and amethyst deposit (Hartmann et al., 2019, *Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta*). The statistical probability that unusual pareidolic formations exist within this enormous specimen pool is extremely high.

**On pareidolia and face perception:**  
The fusiform face area (FFA) and the broader face-processing network in Brodmann areas 19 and 37 respond preferentially to face-like stimuli with measurable consistency across individuals. Kanwisher, McDermott, and Chun (1997, *Journal of Neuroscience*, 17: 4302–4311) demonstrated using fMRI that the FFA showed significantly greater activation for faces than for other objects or scrambled faces, establishing the specialised face-detection architecture. Liu, Harris, and Kanwisher (2010, *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience*, 22: 2898–2909) further refined the understanding that schematic faces — arrangements meeting only minimal criteria for two eyes, a nose, and a mouth — reliably activated the face-detection system.

Applied to geological pareidolia: any rock formation presenting two roughly circular structures above a curved or open structure will activate face detection in most human observers. The Cookie Monster agate presented not merely a schematic face but a face with specific iconic character features (large round blue eyes on stalks, wide permanent open mouth), producing not just face detection but character recognition — a cognitively more specific and surprising event that amplifies emotional response and sharing motivation.

**On image authenticity:**  
Digital image forensics methods, including Error Level Analysis (ELA), Metadata Examination, and PRNU-based sensor fingerprinting (Fridrich, 1999, *IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security*), provide systematic tools for detecting photographic manipulation. Snopes's standard verification practice for viral photographs includes reverse image search provenance tracing and examination of image metadata. The pre-existing video documentation of the specimen's opening substantially reduces the prior probability of fabrication.

**On famous pareidolic rocks and cultural precedent:**  
The Martian "Face on Mars" (Viking 1 image, 1976) generated decades of speculation before the Mars Global Surveyor provided high-resolution imagery in 1998 confirming it was a natural mesa formation (Malin and Edgett, 1998, *Science*). This case demonstrates both the power of pareidolia to generate culturally persistent claims and the pathway by which improved evidence resolves them. The Cookie Monster agate differs in that both the pareidolic pattern and its natural geological origin are confirmed by direct physical examination — there is no unresolved ambiguity of the Martian type.

## §5 — Broader Context

The Cookie Monster rock belongs to a category of "wonderful reality" fact-checks — items that go viral precisely because they seem too good to be true (too perfectly formed, too precisely resembling something familiar) to be natural, and that Snopes confirms are exactly what they appear to be. This category includes confirmed items such as the Anglerfish's bioluminescent lure, naturally occurring hexagonal basalt columns (Giant's Causeway, Devil's Postpile), and the geometric precision of certain mineral crystal formations. The epistemic function of these fact-checks is different from those debunking false claims: they affirm the genuine strangeness of the natural world.

The rock and mineral market provides commercial context for understanding why such specimens emerge to public attention with some regularity. Agate and geode specimens are actively mined, sorted, and traded commercially, particularly from Brazil, Uruguay, Mexico, and Madagascar. Miners and gem show vendors who encounter unusual specimens have both economic and social incentives to share them publicly — unusual formations command premium prices, and viral attention is a marketing channel. Bowers, as a geologist-vendor with a Facebook following, represents this commercial-educational hybrid role common in the gem and mineral community.

The Cookie Monster rock joins a short list of specific famous agate specimens whose pareidolic properties have been named, documented, and circulated: the "Owl" agate, the "Scared Face" agate, and others Bowers himself referenced. Each of these represents a notable instance within a broad population of moderately pareidolic specimens that produce more subtle or ambiguous patterns. The Cookie Monster specimen's distinction is the precision and bilaterality of the pattern — the fact that it appears the same way on both cracked faces of the specimen (reflected symmetry of the internal crystallisation) makes the coincidence doubly unlikely and doubly striking.

The viral lifecycle of this claim followed a pattern well-documented in social media diffusion research: an expert (Bowers) posted a video to a specialized community (geological hobbyists and enthusiasts); it was re-shared into general circulation when news aggregators and science-focused social media accounts picked it up; it then triggered fact-check demand because the image looked almost too perfect to be real. The Snopes fact-check functioned as an epistemic resolution point that confirmed the claim for skeptical audience members and provided a shareable confirmation for those who already believed it.

## §6 — Verdict and Knowledge-State Summary

**Verdict:** True — the photograph shows a genuine geological specimen whose formation is physically plausible and confirmed by named expert attestation and video documentation.

**Epistemic standing:** EARC = **E** (Established). The physical mechanism (agate formation in volcanic cavities via silica deposition) is well-understood and extensively documented. The pareidolic visual effect is cognitively well-explained. The specimen's authenticity is supported by video documentation and expert characterisation without credible contrary evidence.

**Key confirmed facts:**
- The agate specimen was discovered by Lucas Fassari in Brazil
- Geologist Mike Bowers confirmed and characterised the specimen via video post
- The blue crystalline pattern on both internal faces of the split rock resembles the Cookie Monster character with unusual precision
- Agate formation by silica deposition in volcanic vesicles fully accounts for the specimen's physical properties
- Brazil's Paraná volcanic province is the world's largest commercial agate/amethyst source, making such specimens statistically plausible

**Gap assessment:**
- No independent laboratory analysis (mineralogy, provenance confirmation, authenticity testing) is cited
- The frequency of equivalent pareidolic agate formations globally is unknown; no systematic survey exists
- The cognitive science of *why* this specific specimen achieved viral status (beyond the "reveal" video format) has not been formally analysed, though pareidolia research provides mechanistic grounding

**Broader significance:** The Cookie Monster rock is a specimen of naturally occurring geological pareidolia — a coincidental alignment of mineral deposition patterns with a culturally recognised visual figure. It demonstrates that geology, given sufficient specimen diversity, will produce formations matching virtually any iconic visual pattern. The specimen is genuine; its significance lies not in being unique but in being an exceptionally precise and visually compelling instance of a widespread geological phenomenon.
