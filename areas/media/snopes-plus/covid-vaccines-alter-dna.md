# COVID-19 mRNA vaccines alter your DNA


## 1. The Claim

From the earliest stages of COVID-19 vaccine rollout, a persistent and widely-shared claim held that the novel mRNA vaccines developed by Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna would alter the DNA of recipients — permanently reprogramming the human genome, making vaccinated individuals genetically modified organisms (GMOs), or inserting viral genetic sequences into the chromosomes of human cells. The claim circulated in several distinct versions, ranging from vague assertions that mRNA "changes your genes" to technically inflected arguments citing reverse transcriptase enzymes, retroviral integration machinery, and specific peer-reviewed studies as supporting evidence.

The most common form of the claim runs as follows: messenger RNA, once injected, enters the nucleus of human cells; the mRNA vaccine sequence is then reverse-transcribed by cellular enzymes into DNA; that DNA is integrated into the recipient's chromosomes; and therefore the recipient's genome is permanently altered to encode foreign genetic information. Variant versions incorporate the further claim that the integrated DNA would be heritable — that vaccinated parents would pass modified genomes to their children, effectively altering the human germline. Some versions specifically invoked the prospect of pharmaceutical companies "patenting" the modified genome of vaccinated individuals, though this legal claim is separately unsupported.

The claim gained particular momentum in two distinct phases. The first, pre-vaccine rollout, was driven primarily by general anxieties about the novelty of mRNA technology and by anti-vaccination networks that framed the vaccines as unprecedented genetic interventions rather than established pharmaceutical products. The second phase was triggered in early 2021 by the circulation of a preprint — subsequently published in a peer-reviewed journal — from researchers at Harvard and MIT, which appeared to show that SARS-CoV-2 RNA sequences could be reverse-transcribed and integrated into human cells under laboratory conditions. This paper was widely cited in anti-vaccination discourse as proof that the underlying DNA-alteration mechanism was scientifically validated.

The claim occupied a distinctive space in COVID-era misinformation: it was technical enough to seem as though it required expert rebuttal, rather than simple common-sense dismissal; it invoked real biological processes (reverse transcription, retroviral integration) that non-specialist audiences could not easily evaluate; and it arrived alongside genuine scientific uncertainty about a novel disease and novel vaccine platform, which made the landscape of "what we know" genuinely harder for the public to navigate. The result was a claim that proved highly resistant to correction precisely because it wore the garb of biological plausibility.


## 2. What's Actually True

**The central dogma and the direction of genetic information flow.** The claim rests on a fundamental misunderstanding of how genetic information moves within cells. Crick (1970, *Nature*) formalised the central dogma of molecular biology: the normal flow of genetic information runs from DNA to RNA to protein, and not in reverse. DNA is transcribed into mRNA; mRNA is translated into protein; information does not ordinarily flow from RNA back into DNA in human cells. This is not a hypothesis but an established principle supported by decades of biochemical investigation.

**What mRNA vaccines actually contain and do.** COVID-19 mRNA vaccines deliver synthetic mRNA molecules encoding the spike protein of SARS-CoV-2. These molecules are enclosed in lipid nanoparticles to protect them during delivery and to facilitate cellular uptake. Once inside cells, the mRNA is read by ribosomes in the cytoplasm and translated into spike protein — the same process by which cells produce all their other proteins. The spike protein is then displayed on the cell surface, where the immune system recognises it and develops antibodies. The mRNA is subsequently degraded by normal cellular mRNA turnover mechanisms within days. It does not enter the cell nucleus; it does not interact with the chromosome; it does not require, and is not processed by, DNA repair machinery (Dolgin, 2021, *Nature*).

**The absence of integration machinery.** For RNA to be converted into DNA and integrated into the genome, two things are required that mRNA vaccines do not provide: reverse transcriptase enzyme and integrase enzyme. Reverse transcriptase converts RNA into complementary DNA (cDNA); integrase catalyses the insertion of that cDNA into chromosomal DNA. These enzymes are produced by retroviruses (such as HIV) and by certain endogenous mobile elements in the genome called retrotransposons. Standard human somatic cells do not constitutively produce active reverse transcriptase at biologically significant levels. Without these enzymatic components, mRNA cannot be reverse-transcribed into DNA and cannot integrate into the genome. Karikó and Weissman's foundational mRNA immunology work — for which they received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2023 — specifically characterised the stability and degradation profiles of synthetic mRNA (Karikó et al., 2005, *Immunity*), establishing that modified mRNA behaves according to known degradation kinetics rather than persisting indefinitely or entering nuclear compartments.

**The Zhang et al. study and its correct interpretation.** The specific paper most frequently cited in support of the DNA-alteration claim was Zhang et al. (2021, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 118[21]): "SARS-CoV-2 RNA reverse-transcribed and integrated into the human genome." This paper — published after peer review in a prestigious journal — reported that, in human cell lines in vitro, SARS-CoV-2 RNA sequences could be detected integrated into the genome. The finding was genuine; the interpretation required by the DNA-alteration claim is not.

Several critical features of the study were systematically omitted in anti-vaccination retellings. First, the integration events were detected only in cell lines that had been transfected with mobile genetic elements (specifically LINE-1 retrotransposons) to artificially elevate reverse transcriptase activity to levels vastly beyond what occurs in normal human somatic cells. This was an experimental system designed to ask whether integration was *possible in principle*, not whether it occurs under normal physiological conditions. Second, the sequences detected were from the SARS-CoV-2 virus itself, not from the vaccine mRNA — the study examined viral infection, not vaccination. Third, subsequent analysis by multiple groups suggested that some of the integration signals detected were likely sequencing artefacts rather than genuine genomic insertions (Smits et al., 2021, *bioRxiv*). Fourth and most fundamentally, nothing in the study design addressed the question of whether mRNA vaccines, delivered as lipid nanoparticles into the cytoplasm of non-transfected cells, produce any reverse-transcription events. The experimental conditions required to produce the reported effect do not exist in vaccine recipients.

**The nuclear entry question.** An additional factual basis for the DNA-alteration claim — that mRNA vaccines enter the cell nucleus — is directly refuted by the route of mRNA processing. Ribosomes, the molecular machines that translate mRNA into protein, operate in the cytoplasm. mRNA does not need to enter the nucleus for translation, and vaccine mRNA does not. Fluorescence localisation studies of lipid nanoparticle-delivered mRNA confirm cytoplasmic distribution without nuclear accumulation (Pardi et al., 2018, *Nature Reviews Drug Discovery*).


## 3. Why People Believe This

The COVID-19 mRNA vaccine DNA-alteration claim is an epistemologically distinctive case because it is not simply an assertion of false information — it is an assertion supported by a selective and technically inflected misreading of real science. Understanding why it was so resistant to correction requires examining the specific epistemic conditions that gave it leverage.

**The novelty asymmetry.** mRNA vaccines were genuinely novel at the time of emergency authorisation. The Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna vaccines were the first mRNA products authorised for large-scale human use, and their approval under emergency use authorisation rather than the standard full approval pathway created a plausible framing in which normal safety and understanding guarantees were suspended. In this context, lay audiences had no experiential basis for distinguishing between "this is genuinely new and unpredictable" and "this violates established molecular biology." The novelty of the platform created epistemic uncertainty that the DNA-alteration claim exploited — claiming, in effect, that because scientists could not predict everything, they could not rule out genetic alteration.

**Scientific self-undermining via preprint weaponisation.** The specific epistemic novelty of the COVID mRNA DNA claim lies in the mechanism by which it gained scientific credibility: a genuine peer-reviewed study in a high-impact journal was excerpted to provide the claim with scientific authority it did not, on full reading, support. This represents a new vector for health misinformation — not the fabrication of fake studies but the selective reading of real studies. The Zhang et al. paper was not fraudulent; it was a legitimate scientific investigation of a genuinely interesting cellular biology question. Its existence in the published literature meant that when health authorities said "there is no evidence mRNA vaccines alter DNA," the response could be "PNAS says otherwise" — an apparently compelling rebuttal that was technically unanswerable in a brief exchange but was scientifically misleading in the specific claim being made. The open science and preprint ecosystem, designed to accelerate scientific communication, was exploited so that a contextual laboratory finding could circulate without its essential methodological context. This mechanism — preprint weaponisation — is likely to recur with future novel medical technologies and represents a underappreciated vulnerability in science communication infrastructure.

**Fear of loss of biological autonomy.** A deeper motivational substrate beneath the technical arguments is the visceral concern about bodily integrity and biological autonomy. The claim that a vaccine could permanently alter one's DNA touches on profound anxieties about irreversibility, about institutional power over bodies, and about the integrity of identity understood as biologically-grounded. Even vaccine-accepting populations show elevated concern about DNA alteration compared with other vaccine side effects, suggesting that the specific framing activates something beyond generalised anti-vaccination sentiment. The GMO parallel was explicitly invoked in much of the discourse — framing vaccinated individuals as genetic modifications analogous to crops modified by agricultural biotechnology, a framing that activuted existing anxieties about corporate ownership of life and naturalness.

**The expertise gap as a structural feature.** The technical content of the claim — involving reverse transcription, LINE-1 retrotransposons, integrase enzymes, and nuclear localisation signals — sits precisely in the zone where specialist knowledge is required to evaluate it and where that knowledge is not widely possessed. A claim that COVID vaccines turn you blue would be immediately dismissible; a claim that invokes the actual molecular biology of reverse transcription with partial accuracy is far harder to dismiss from a lay epistemic position. This is a feature of the claim rather than an accident: the most durable health misinformation in technologically sophisticated populations tends to occupy the zone just beyond general lay comprehension, where authority-granting to partial technical framings is hardest to resist.


## 4. Verdict

**Empirically established as false (EARC: E).** The claim that COVID-19 mRNA vaccines alter human DNA is false on multiple independent grounds: mRNA vaccines are confined to the cytoplasm; they are degraded by normal cellular RNA turnover within days of injection; they do not provide the enzymatic machinery required for reverse transcription; they do not enter the cell nucleus; and no peer-reviewed study conducted under conditions relevant to vaccine recipients has detected vaccine-induced genome alteration. The study most frequently cited in support of the claim (Zhang et al., 2021, PNAS) examined viral infection under artificially elevated reverse-transcriptase conditions and is not evidence for vaccine-induced DNA alteration. Snopes rates the claim False. Major health authorities worldwide — including the CDC, WHO, and EMA — have addressed and refuted the claim on mechanistic grounds.


## 5. The Wider Picture

The COVID mRNA vaccine DNA claim fits into a broader pattern in which successive technological advances in medicine are met with concerns about irreversibility, unnaturalness, and loss of biological integrity. Similar arguments were made about recombinant vaccines, about GMO foods, about gene therapy, and — in older forms — about blood transfusion and germ theory vaccination. The specific technical dressing changes with each generation but the structural concern remains: that medical technology is intervening at a fundamental level in human biology in ways that cannot be undone or fully predicted.

What distinguishes the mRNA case is the extent to which the concern was amplified by conditions specific to the COVID-19 pandemic: emergency authorisation timelines, political polarisation around public health intervention, active disinformation campaigns with identified state and non-state actors, and a media environment in which preprints — designed for rapid scientific communication — were accessible to general audiences without the contextualising apparatus that professional scientific readers bring to preliminary findings.

The response from science communicators to the DNA-alteration claim illustrates the difficulty of correcting technical misinformation in real time. Simple refutations ("mRNA cannot enter the nucleus") are dismissible as oversimplifications; nuanced refutations ("the Zhang study examined artificially elevated LINE-1 conditions irrelevant to vaccination") require background knowledge to follow and are difficult to communicate in the formats where the claim circulates. There is a structural asymmetry between the complexity required to make a sophisticated-seeming false claim and the complexity required to fully rebut it.

Future iterations of this claim structure should be anticipated with novel vaccine platforms. The development of further mRNA therapeutics, mRNA cancer vaccines, and other nucleic-acid delivery systems will create recurring opportunities for technically-inflected DNA-alteration claims to be constructed from partial readings of mechanistic research. Building public familiarity with the central dogma, with the distinction between in-vitro laboratory conditions and physiological conditions, and with the difference between "possible in principle under contrived conditions" and "occurs in practice," would provide more durable immunity to this class of misinformation than case-by-case rebuttal.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

Snopes rates the claim that COVID-19 mRNA vaccines alter DNA as False, citing established molecular biology and the absence of nuclear delivery in mRNA vaccine systems. Their coverage addresses the mechanism in accessible terms and correctly identifies the core error as a misunderstanding of the direction of genetic information flow. The Snopes verdict appropriately directs readers to the central dogma and to the lack of integration machinery in the vaccine delivery system.

The fact-checking gap — where the Observatory adds material — concerns the Zhang et al. study and its handling. Most fact-check treatments of the DNA-alteration claim predate or underhandle this study, which represents the most scientifically sophisticated version of the argument. A fully adequate fact-check needs to: (1) explain what the Zhang study actually showed and under what conditions; (2) explain why those conditions do not apply to vaccine recipients; (3) explain the distinction between in-vitro experimental manipulation and physiological reality; and (4) trace the epistemic pathway by which a legitimate scientific paper with limited findings was transformed into population-level evidence for vaccine-induced DNA alteration.

The broader media literacy lesson here is in the handling of preprints and preliminary findings in public health contexts. Preprint accessibility is a genuine scientific good, but it creates a vulnerability when audiences lack the contextual tools to assess methodological scope. Fact-checkers and science communicators who address real-time medical controversies would benefit from developing standard communicative templates for the "real study, wrong interpretation" class of misinformation — acknowledging the study's existence and legitimacy while clearly explaining the scope limitations that prevent its application to the claim being made.
