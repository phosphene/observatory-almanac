# Did Fracking Cause Volcanic Activity on Hawaii's Big Island?


## 1. The Claim

**Core assertion**: On May 30, 2018 — less than a month after the beginning of an eruption sequence in the lower Puna region of Hawaii's Big Island on May 3, 2018 — conspiracy website YourNewsWire.com published an article claiming that volcanic activity was not driven by natural geological processes but by fracking (hydraulic fracturing) operations at the Puna Geothermal Venture (PGV), a geothermal power plant near the eruption zone. The claim stated: "Scientific data has linked the surge in volcanic activity in Hawaii to increased fracking activity at Puna Geothermal Venture."

The article further alleged that PGV's operations had been "deliberately designed to create a geological process by which lava would drain from Kilauea's summit into the East Rift Zone so as to create large new vents that would destabilize the geology, and trigger a major collapse in the Hilina Fault System." The ultimate purpose of this alleged covert fracking operation, according to the article's primary source (Exopolitics.org), was to produce a massive tsunami through the collapse of the Hilina slump — a geological feature on the ocean-side slope of the Big Island's volcanic complex — thereby causing global economic disruption. Exopolitics linked this conspiracy to the "Rothschild family" on the grounds that the company owning PGV had received the "Rothschild Prize for Innovation" — in 1977, 27 years before acquiring the plant.

**Source genealogy**: Snopes investigative journalist Alex Kasprak, writing on June 22, 2018, traced this claim from YourNewsWire to its primary source, Exopolitics.org. Exopolitics is described by Kasprak as "an alleged branch of political science that deals with extraterrestrial influence on human affairs." The claim thus originates in an anti-semitic conspiracy framework (Rothschild family targeting) combined with catastrophist geology, framed in the language of energy technology and disseminated by YourNewsWire — a site with a documented record of publishing fabricated or heavily distorted content.

**Broader context of the eruption**: The 2018 lower Puna eruption was a genuine and significant geological event. From May 3 to September 4, 2018, eruptions in the lower East Rift Zone of Kilauea produced 24 distinct fissures, destroyed approximately 700 homes, covered 13.7 square miles with lava, and created a new ocean entry point. The event caused the collapse of Kilauea's Halema'uma'u crater, with the crater floor dropping approximately 500 meters. The eruption was entirely consistent with the decades-long ongoing eruptive activity of Kilauea, the world's most continuously active volcano, and with well-understood geological processes involving the movement of magma through the East Rift Zone. Kasprak specifically notes that the 2018 eruption is a continuation of an eruptive cycle that began in 1983.


## 2. What's Actually True

**Fracking does not occur in Hawaii**: The most fundamental refutation of the claim is also the simplest: hydraulic fracturing — the technological process specifically named in the claim — does not occur anywhere in the state of Hawaii. Fracking is a petroleum extraction technique that involves injecting high-pressure fluid (typically water mixed with sand and chemical additives) into hydrocarbon-bearing rock formations to fracture them and increase the flow of oil or natural gas to a well. Its application is contingent on the existence of underground petroleum or natural gas reserves. Hawaii has no petroleum reserves (U.S. Energy Information Administration, Hawaii Energy Profile, accessible at eia.gov/state/analysis.php?sid=HI). There are no petroleum extraction operations anywhere in Hawaii, and consequently no fracking operations. The claim's central factual premise — that fracking was occurring at or near the Puna eruption — fails before any other analysis is required.

**The Puna Geothermal Venture is a geothermal energy plant, not a fracking operation**: The PGV is a geothermal electricity generation facility that has operated in the lower Puna region since 1993. Geothermal energy generation exploits heat stored in the Earth's crust by drilling wells into geothermally active rock, extracting steam and hot water, using the steam to drive turbines that generate electricity, and then reinjecting the cooled water (brine) back into the formation. Hawaiian Electric, which purchased electricity from PGV, described the process as follows: the plant brings "steam and hot liquid up through underground wells"; the steam drives a turbine generator; the condensed steam and the brine (hot liquid) are reinjected into the ground through reinjection wells.

The Exopolitics claim attempts to argue that this reinjection process is "essentially" fracking because both involve injecting fluid into the ground. This argument fails on every technical dimension. The National Academies of Sciences *Induced Seismicity Potential in Energy Technologies* report (2012, National Academies Press) reviewed the seismicity risks of both hydraulic fracturing and geothermal reinjection in detail. The two processes differ critically: the *primary goal* of fracking is to fracture rock (to increase permeability and hydrocarbon flow); the primary goal of geothermal reinjection is to maintain reservoir pressure and prevent land subsidence from the extraction of large volumes of fluid. The fluids differ: fracking typically injects water with proprietary chemical additives and proppants (sand); geothermal reinjection uses water only. The pressures differ: hydraulic fracturing requires pressures high enough to fracture intact rock; geothermal reinjection operates at pressures well below rock fracture thresholds. The geological targets differ: fracking targets tight hydrocarbon formations; geothermal reinjection targets porous volcanic rock already saturated with geothermal fluid. The processes share only the word "inject" and the involvement of fluid.

**The 2018 eruption was a continuation of a 35-year volcanic event**: The 2018 lower Puna eruption did not begin in 2018. Kilauea has been in near-continuous eruptive activity since January 3, 1983, when the current eruptive episode (Episode 61g) began at the Pu'u 'Ō'ō crater on the East Rift Zone. Over 35 years, lava flows from this event covered approximately 48 square miles, reached the ocean at multiple points, and destroyed hundreds of structures. The U.S. Geological Survey Hawaiian Volcano Observatory (USGS HVO) has documented this eruption continuously throughout its duration. The specific 2018 activity in the lower Puna region represented a migration of eruptive fissures down the East Rift Zone — a pattern that had occurred multiple times during the 35-year eruptive cycle — and was forecast by USGS geologists based on seismic activity and ground deformation monitoring. The idea that this eruption, predated by 35 years of continuous geological activity, was triggered by a geothermal plant that began operations a decade after volcanic activity started requires the plant to have retroactively caused the earthquake sequence that preceded it.

**The Hilina Slump**: The claim's catastrophist endpoint — a Kilauea flank collapse causing a trans-Pacific mega-tsunami — references a real geological feature. The Hilina Slump is approximately 5,000 km³ of the Big Island's southern flank that is slowly moving seaward, potentially susceptible to rapid collapse in the event of a sufficiently large earthquake. The catastrophic tsunami potential of a rapid Hilina Slump collapse has been discussed in geological literature (e.g., Ward SN and Day S, 2001, *Geophysical Research Letters*, vol. 28, pp. 379–382), and the 1975 Kalapana earthquake (magnitude 7.2) produced a localized tsunami from partial slump movement. However, geologists' evaluation of collapse scenarios does not implicate human industrial activity as a triggering mechanism, and the scale of geothermal reinjection at PGV (a small commercial plant) is orders of magnitude below any threshold that could meaningfully influence the kiloton-scale forces governing the slump. The invocation of the Hilina Slump in terrorist-conspiracy framing misrepresents legitimate geological inquiry into a rare but real hazard class as evidence of covert weaponization.

**The Rothschild connection**: The claimed connection between PGV and the Rothschild family — used as an anti-semitic conspiracy signal — is that the plant's owner since 2004, Ormat Technologies, was founded by a person who received the "Rothschild Prize for Innovation" in 1977. The prize — awarded by institutions affiliated with the Rothschild family philanthropic trusts — has no operational connection to the Rothschild financial empire, and the 1977 receipt of a prize by a company's founder has no implication for the operations of that company 27 years later. This connection is specifically identified by Kasprak as "generally deployed as an anti-semitic dog whistle" — a framing that is accurate: the Rothschild conspiracy trope is endemic to far-right anti-semitic discourse and has been used for over a century to imply Jewish financial conspiracy, in various contexts, as a causal explanation for harmful events.


## 3. Why People Believe This

**Epistemic novelty: geographical proximity as specious causation, and conspiracy theories about energy infrastructure**

The volcanic eruption-fracking conspiracy claim exploits a specific cognitive vulnerability: the tendency to interpret geographical proximity as evidence of causal relationship, particularly when the claimant provides a causal mechanism — even a false one — that superficially ties them together. The Puna Geothermal Venture was geographically near the eruption zone, and the claim provided a mechanism (the arguable parallel between geothermal reinjection and fracking) that created the appearance of causal connection. For audiences without background in volcanology or geology, assessing the plausibility of this mechanism required knowledge they did not have.

The specific choice of "fracking" as the mechanism is strategically effective for reasons beyond geographical proximity. By 2018, hydraulic fracturing for petroleum extraction had become the subject of substantial public controversy regarding its environmental and seismic impacts. Research had documented that wastewater injection from oil and gas operations — including reinjection of fracking flowback fluids — was associated with induced seismicity in the central United States, particularly in Oklahoma (Ellsworth WL, 2013, *Science*, vol. 341, article 1225942). The claim borrowed the credibility of this legitimate induced seismicity research and transferred it to a context where it did not apply: geothermal reinjection in a volcanically active environment.

The psychology of conspiracy acceptance in this case is well-described by the concept of "proportionality bias" (Leman PJ and Cinnirella M, 2007, *Social Psychology*, vol. 38, pp. 247–256): the intuition that large and significant events should have large and significant causes. The destruction of hundreds of homes, the displacement of thousands of people, and the transformation of coastal geography are events of enormous human significance. Natural volcanic processes driven by plate tectonics and mantle convection — processes that have been active in Hawaii for millions of years, entirely independent of human activity — are a cause that many people perceive as incommensurate with the scale of the event, because natural processes lack intentional agency. A conspiracy involving covert fracking, deliberate tsunami generation, and Rothschild family involvement provides an intentional agent at a scale that seems proportionate to the harm. This psychological fit is independent of any evidential support.

The involvement of an exotic-sounding organization (Exopolitics — dealing with extraterrestrial political influence) as the named primary source might seem to undermine credibility, but for audiences embedded in conspiratorial information networks, the association with extraterrestrial conspiracy adds rather than subtracts plausibility. Within those networks, extraterrestrial involvement in global affairs is taken as background knowledge, and a claim that links volcanic engineering to both the Rothschild family and alien-affiliated organizations fits a comprehensive conspiratorial worldview coherently.


## 4. The Broader Pattern

**Geothermal energy and conspiracy: a specific subgenre**: The Hawaii fracking-volcano claim is part of a broader category of conspiracy claims targeting renewable or low-carbon energy infrastructure. Wind turbines have been accused of causing health impacts through infrasound. Solar farms have been accused of "absorbing" atmospheric heat. High-voltage power lines have been accused of causing cancer. Geothermal plants, by virtue of their physical implantation in seismically and volcanically active terrain, are plausible conspiracy targets because they are visibly connected to geological forces. The causal narrative almost writes itself: drilling into the Earth in a volcanic region → drilling causes eruption. The fact that the Puna Geothermal Venture has operated since 1993 without triggering eruptions, and that the 2018 eruption was a continuation of activity predating PGV by a decade, requires a level of historical context that audiences encountering a single viral article are unlikely to have and unlikely to seek out.

**Induced seismicity: real but different**: The existence of a completely separate and real body of research on human-caused seismicity from fracking and wastewater injection operations creates a plausibility halo for the Hawaii claim among audiences who have heard of induced seismicity. Oklahoma experienced a dramatic increase in earthquake frequency between 2008 and 2015 that was causally linked to saltwater wastewater disposal wells from oil and gas operations — a finding that was eventually acknowledged by the state's own geological survey (Walsh FR III and Zoback MD, 2015, *Nature Geoscience*, vol. 8, pp. 691–693). This real phenomenon — human industrial activity causing seismicity — is different in mechanism, scale, and geological context from the Hawaii claim in every relevant respect, but its existence makes the Hawaii claim more psychologically accessible.

**Kilauea and ongoing volcanic monitoring**: The USGS Hawaiian Volcano Observatory is among the best-resourced and most scientifically rigorous geological monitoring institutions in the world. The observatory has monitored Kilauea comprehensively since the current eruptive episode began in 1983, using seismometer networks, GPS deformation monitoring, gas monitoring, and satellite-based remote sensing. The 2018 lower Puna eruption was anticipated by monitoring data weeks before the surface eruption began, and its subsequent behavior was extensively documented in real time. The complete absence of any evidence for PGV involvement in the eruption in this comprehensive monitoring record is itself significant evidence: a mechanism capable of destabilizing the Kilauea-East Rift Zone system would have produced detectable seismic and deformation signatures traceable to the PGV site, and no such signatures were observed.


## 5. Evidentiary Assessment

**Strength of the refutation**: Very high. The primary claim — that fracking caused the eruption — fails before any geological analysis because fracking does not occur in Hawaii. The backup claim — that geothermal reinjection is equivalent to fracking — fails on every technical dimension: different mechanisms, different pressures, different fluids, opposite geological goals. The geological causation for the 2018 eruption — a continuation of a 35-year volcanic cycle driven by magma movement in the East Rift Zone — is thoroughly documented by independent USGS monitoring predating both PGV's operations and YourNewsWire's existence. The conspiracy framing (Rothschild family connection, deliberate tsunami generation) is based on a transparent misrepresentation of a prize awarded 41 years before the event.

**YourNewsWire credibility**: Kasprak identifies YourNewsWire as "not known for its commitment to facts or accuracy" — an assessment consistent with the organization's track record. The site was banned from Google News in 2018 for repeatedly violating its content policies regarding misinformation and was subsequently rebranded as "News Punch." Exopolitics.org's general framework (extraterrestrial political influence on human affairs) is not a recognized area of legitimate political science.

**Known limitations**: The geological literature on catastrophic volcanic flank collapses (including the Hilina Slump scenario) represents real scientific uncertainty about low-probability/high-consequence events in Hawaii's geological future. This legitimate scientific discussion has no bearing on the false claim that PGV caused the 2018 eruption, but it represents a domain where the boundary between real hazard assessment and catastrophist conspiracy can be confused.


## 6. Verdict and Implications

Snopes rates the fracking-volcano claim **False**. The rating is justified by multiple independent lines of argument, each of which is individually sufficient to falsify the claim. The claim's factual architecture is a cascade of errors: fracking doesn't happen in Hawaii; geothermal reinjection is not fracking; the eruption predated PGV by a decade; the conspiracy genealogy rests on an anti-semitic interpretive framework rather than evidence.

The implications for science communication are instructive in several respects. This claim illustrates how real scientific debates — about induced seismicity, about volcanic hazard assessment, about the Hilina Slump — can be decontextualized and weaponized in conspiracy framing. The presence of real scientific discussion about induced seismicity from fracking provided the claim with scientific-sounding vocabulary and a reference class of real cases; the conspiracy layer then transferred that vocabulary to a context where it didn't apply. Effective fact-checking in this domain requires both the scientific knowledge to identify the technical errors and the conspiracy literacy to recognize the structural features of motivated misrepresentation.

The broader pattern — energy infrastructure as the target of conspiracy claims during environmental disasters — is likely to recur. As renewable energy infrastructure expands into geologically active territories and as climate change increases the frequency and severity of natural disasters, the opportunities for geographic-proximity-based conspiracy claims linking infrastructure to natural events will multiply. The epistemological tools for evaluating these claims are well-established: Does the proposed causal mechanism actually operate in this context? Does the event predate the alleged cause? What does continuous independent monitoring show? Is the conspiracy framework grounded in demonstrable evidence or in motivated pattern-matching? Applying these questions to the Hawaii fracking-volcano claim resolves it unambiguously.
