## §1 — Claim & Verdict Summary

The claim subject to investigation here appeared widely on Facebook in mid-July 2022 under the emphatic header "A LOCAL RESTAURANT POSTED THIS TODAY!!!!" The post described specific dramatic cost increases for frying oil, chicken wings, and other food commodities, framing them as the lived experience of a local food business struggling with inflationary pressures. The verdict from Snopes investigator Bethania Palma (2022) is **Mixture**.

What is true: The post genuinely originated with a real person in the food industry who experienced the cost pressures described, and the underlying economic data on food price inflation was accurate at the macro level. What is false, or at minimum misleading: (1) the post originated from Kwasi Moses, a private chef and food-product entrepreneur based in Oakland, California — not from a restaurant — and was created on October 18, 2021, not "today" in July 2022; (2) the framing as a "local" restaurant made it seem immediately present and geographically proximate to each individual reader, when Moses is "local" only to the San Francisco Bay Area; and (3) the nine-month gap between original creation and viral circulation detached the post from its original temporal and geographic context, allowing it to function as a perpetual-present inflation grievance document deployable by anyone, anywhere.

The mixture verdict reflects the genuine economic accuracy of the underlying data combined with the fabricated provenance, timing, and identity claims introduced through the copypasta recontextualization process.

## §2 — Origin and Spread of the Claim

Copypasta — the internet phenomenon in which text content achieves virality through mass copying and re-sharing rather than original authorship — is a well-documented category of digital information behavior. As Palma (2022) noted in the foundational Snopes investigation, the mechanism systematically strips content of its original context (creator identity, date, geography, intent) as each iteration of sharing removes or reconstructs the framing. The result is that a piece of content, by the time it achieves significant viral scale, may bear virtually no contextual resemblance to its original instantiation.

Moses' original Instagram post was created in an explicit personal/professional voice: he was a Bay Area-based chef sharing his direct experience of cost increases in food-related raw materials and logistics. He reported skyrocketing prices for frying oil, chicken wings, seasonings, plates, forks, cups, eggs, and tamper-proof shipping containers required for product compliance in California. His post also specified the California regulatory environment as a cost amplifier — requirements for specific packaging materials and tamper-proof containers that add to baseline commodity costs already elevated by national supply chain disruptions.

By mid-July 2022, this nine-month-old Instagram post had been stripped of its author, date, and geographic context and reframed as a news item about a local, unnamed restaurant's immediate price emergency. The text of Moses' original post was then shared as though it were a breaking business announcement rather than a personal reflection from a year prior. The header added by subsequent sharers — "A LOCAL RESTAURANT POSTED THIS TODAY!!!!" — introduced three specific false claims simultaneously: the actor's identity (restaurant vs. individual chef), the timing (2022 vs. 2021), and the geographic proximity (the recipient's local area vs. Oakland, California specifically).

Moses, in a direct phone interview with Palma (2022), explained that he recognized the economic content remained relevant even out of its original context: "It's alarming that a lot of consumers don't really understand what it takes to run a business around food... Some people think it's a joke or a scare tactic, but it's real." This acknowledgment — that the decontextualized version served a function that was at least partially aligned with his original communicative intent, even while misrepresenting him — represents a nuanced position that complicates simple true/false binaries.

The National Restaurant Association (NRA) provided Palma (2022) with corroborating data confirming that wholesale food costs nationally had increased more than 13% in the preceding 12 months, described by spokesperson Vanessa Sink as "the largest 12-month increase in nearly 5 decades." Sink further cited the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Producer Price Index data from June 2022 documenting dramatic year-over-year increases across specific commodity categories.

## §3 — Epistemic Analysis and Novel Findings

**Epistemic novelty:** This case illuminates a specific and underexamined dynamic in how personal grievance narratives are transformed into political data through copypasta mechanics. The original Moses post functions, in its authentic form, as a first-person witness statement about microeconomic experience — a genre of content that carries evidential weight in qualitative research on economic hardship but whose value depends entirely on its contextual integrity. The decontextualized copypasta version, by stripping the first-person specificity, converts it into something far more rhetorically powerful and factually hazardous: an anonymous, dateless, geographically universal claim that can be presented as fresh local evidence in any context at any time.

This transformation maps onto what Chadwick (2013, *The Hybrid Media System*) describes as the political logic of "information cycles" — the process by which content created for one media logic is repurposed through the conventions of another. Moses created content under the logic of personal professional testimony on Instagram; it was converted, through copypasta mechanics, into local news-style urgency appropriate for Facebook political discussion. Each stage of this conversion added false metadata (the "local restaurant," the "today") without changing the underlying economic content — a form of semantic laundering that preserves factual kernel while replacing contextual shell.

From a misinformation typology perspective, this case belongs to what Wardle and Derakhshan (2017, *Council of Europe Report*) classify as *misleading content* — content that uses accurate information to frame an issue or individual deceptively. It is distinct from both fabricated content (which is wholly invented) and satire/parody (which signals its non-factual status). The underlying wage/price data in Moses' post was accurate; it was the identity, timing, and proximity framing that created the misleading impression.

The specific inflation statistics cited by the NRA and BLS deserve individual notation because of their magnitude. Sink (cited in Palma, 2022) reported the following year-over-year changes to the BLS Producer Price Index as of June 2022: eggs (+156.1%), butter (+60.5%), fresh vegetables (+34.9%), flour (+38.5%), fats and oils (+29.4%), milk (+22.0%), cheese (+21.7%), processed poultry (+21.6%), and fresh fruits and melons (+13.7%). These figures are extraordinary by historical standards. The overall 13%+ increase in wholesale food costs was, as the NRA characterized it, the largest such increase in nearly fifty years — a reference to the comparable period around the 1973 oil shock and associated commodity inflation. Nakamura and Steinsson (2008, *Quarterly Journal of Economics*) documented the substantial distributional asymmetry in inflation effects on low-income households, noting that food commodities constitute a significantly higher share of expenditure for lower-income deciles, meaning these price increases fell disproportionately on more economically vulnerable consumers and businesses.

**Research gap:** There is no established research program examining how small-business cost-narrative content — first-person accounts of commodity price increases written by individual operators — gets systematically amplified, decontextualized, and politically mobilized through social media sharing mechanics. The specific mechanism of copypasta in economic discourse deserves attention from both media studies and political economy frameworks. The Moses case suggests that authentic individual economic testimony, once extracted from its source context, can function as infinitely renewable political ammunition precisely because: (a) the underlying facts remain broadly accurate at the macro level; (b) the absence of specific attribution makes counter-verification difficult; and (c) the present-tense framing creates perpetual rhetorical urgency regardless of when the original was created.

This dynamic also raises a structural question about the relationship between corporate supply chain disclosures and consumer-facing communication. Moses' post highlighted cost increases for specific items — frying oil, chicken wings — that were simultaneously tracked in BLS and commodity futures data but rarely translated into consumer-accessible framing. The virality of Moses' lay translation of professional cost experience into concrete, named-item terms suggests that accessible economic communication by practitioners fills a genuine epistemic gap that neither corporate PR nor government statistical releases adequately addresses.

## §4 — Evidence Examined

**Original post:** Moses, Kwasi (2021, October 18). Instagram post documenting food input cost increases, accessible via Archive.ph archived version at https://archive.ph/ELDov. Establishes October 2021 creation date, Oakland, California origin, and individual chef (not restaurant) authorship.

**Primary interview:** Phone interview with Kwasi Moses conducted by Bethania Palma, Snopes, July 18–19, 2022. Moses confirmed authenticity of original post, acknowledged decontextualization, and affirmed continued relevance of the underlying economic content.

**Macroeconomic validation:** National Restaurant Association (NRA) / Spokerperson Vanessa Sink (2022), cited in Palma (2022), citing U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Producer Price Index (June 2022). Corroborates the specific cost claims at the categorical level: wholesale food costs up 13%+ over 12 months, eggs +156.1%, butter +60.5%, flour +38.5%, fats/oils +29.4%, milk +22.0%, cheese +21.7%, processed poultry +21.6%, fresh vegetables +34.9%, fresh fruits/melons +13.7%.

**Regional context:** Sanchez, K. (2021, November 15). Eating out? Why Bay Area restaurants continue to raise prices. *NBC Bay Area*. Provides independent regional verification of cost increase pressures facing California food service operators in the same period as Moses' original post.

**NRA industry data:** National Restaurant Association, Food Costs page. https://restaurant.org/research-and-media/research/economists-notebook/economic-indicators/food-costs/. Provides aggregated wholesale food cost tracking data corroborating 5-decade-high increase framing.

## §5 — Counterclaims, Limitations, and Alternative Interpretations

The "Mixture" rating requires careful defense against both directions of interpretive challenge: those who would consider the deceptive framing sufficient for a pure "False" verdict, and those who would consider the accurate underlying economics sufficient for a broader "True" verdict.

**Against the "should be False" argument:** The decontextualization introduced three specific false claims (identity, timing, geography). However, a strict "False" verdict would imply that the underlying economic content is false, when it was in fact broadly corroborated by independent macro data. The Mixture rating correctly separates the truth status of the economic substance from the truth status of the distribution framing. The NRA data shows that the general phenomenon described — rapid cost increases for specific food inputs — was genuinely occurring on the scales Moses described.

**Against the "should be True" argument:** The false framing is not merely incidental. The framing claims — "local restaurant," "today," "in town" — performed specific rhetorical functions: converting macro-economic grievance into immediate local emergency, and creating the false sense that verified local knowledge was being shared. These framing claims are material to why the post was shared at the scale it was. Factual content wrapped in false provenance claims cannot receive a clean "True" rating without endorsing the frame.

**Moses' own ambiguity:** Moses' statement to Palma — essentially, the post remains relevant even if decontextualized — reflects a pragmatic acceptance of copypasta mechanics that did not explicitly condemn the false framing. This does not change the factual verdict but complicates any clean moral assessment of the information ecosystem.

**Limitation:** The Snopes investigation does not quantify the scale of the viral distribution, nor does it establish whether the Facebook posting style (with visible user attribution cropped for privacy) was typical of how the post circulated or whether there were versions with no visible attribution at all. The copypasta phenomenon implies the latter was likely the dominant mode, but this is inference rather than documented evidence.

## §6 — Conclusion and Standing

The verdict of **Mixture** is accurately assigned. The post claimed to be from a "local restaurant" posting "today" about dramatic food cost increases. The economic content of the claims was broadly accurate at the macro and micro level, corroborated by BLS Producer Price Index data and NRA industry tracking. However, the provenance was entirely falsified through copypasta mechanics: the post originated with an individual private chef in Oakland, California, was created in October 2021, and was shared nine months out of context as though fresh and local.

The case documents a specific and important dynamic in political economic discourse: authentic practitioner testimony about real cost pressures, decontextualized and redistributed as anonymous perpetual-present emergency. The inflation data from 2022 — the largest 12-month wholesale food cost increase in nearly five decades — was severe enough that the authentic Moses content required no embellishment to be alarming. The false framing added nothing to its informational value but significantly increased its emotional urgency and geographic ubiquity, enabling it to function as simultaneous evidence of crisis "here" for every reader regardless of location.

Standing as of 2026-07-21: Verdict unchanged. The economic context (2021–2022 food commodity inflation) has evolved since original publication. Whether the specific cost figures Moses cited remain accurate for current market conditions would require fresh verification, but the factual structure of the verdict — accurate core economics, false provenance and dating — remains stable.
