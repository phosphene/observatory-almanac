# "Dolphins Are the Smartest Animals After Humans"


## 1. The Claim

**Core Claim:** "Dolphins are the smartest animals after humans"

This popular assertion positions dolphins as occupying the second-highest rank in a presumed linear hierarchy of animal intelligence, with humans at the apex and all other species arranged in descending order of cognitive capability. The claim suggests that dolphin intelligence surpasses that of all other non-human animals, including great apes, elephants, corvids, and cephalopods, across all relevant cognitive domains.

The claim typically encompasses several related assumptions: that intelligence can be meaningfully ranked across species using universal criteria; that dolphins demonstrate superior performance to other animals in problem-solving, social cognition, communication, and learning; that brain size, complexity, or other neuroanatomical features support dolphins' claimed cognitive superiority; and that scientific research consistently places dolphins in the "second position" across comparative cognition studies.

This belief has become deeply embedded in popular culture through marine park marketing, nature documentaries, science fiction narratives, and educational materials that often present dolphin intelligence as a singular phenomenon approaching human-level cognition in aquatic form.


## 2. What's Actually True

**Assessment:** The claim accurately reflects popular belief but misrepresents the nature of comparative cognition research and evolutionary intelligence.

The phrasing "smartest animals after humans" faithfully captures widespread public understanding and media portrayals of dolphin cognition. However, the claim embeds several problematic assumptions that scientific evaluation exposes as category errors rather than empirical questions:

**Conceptual Issues:**

- **Intelligence Hierarchy Assumption:** The claim presupposes that intelligence can be arranged in a single linear ranking across species, despite cognitive abilities evolving as solutions to different environmental challenges and selective pressures

- **Anthropocentric Intelligence Definition:** Implicit in the claim is that human-type intelligence represents the pinnacle toward which all other cognition should be measured, rather than recognizing intelligence as diverse adaptive specializations

- **Cognitive Domain Generalization:** The claim suggests dolphins excel across all cognitive domains, when research shows species-specific cognitive strengths that don't correlate perfectly across different abilities

- **Evolutionary Misunderstanding:** The claim implies cognitive evolution progresses toward human-like intelligence, rather than recognizing that different lineages develop cognitive solutions optimized for their ecological niches

**Popular Culture Reinforcement:**

The claim's persistence reflects several cultural and commercial factors: marine park industry marketing emphasizing dolphin intelligence to justify captivity programs; science fiction narratives portraying dolphins as nearly human-level communicators; nature documentaries focusing on spectacular dolphin behaviors while underrepresenting comparative cognitive research; and educational simplification that reduces complex comparative cognition findings to easily memorable rankings.


**Scientific Consensus:** Clear consensus among comparative cognition researchers that intelligence cannot be meaningfully ranked linearly across species, and that multiple taxa demonstrate cognitive sophistication in different domains.

Modern comparative cognition explicitly rejects the "scala naturae" (great chain of being) approach that arranges species in hierarchical intelligence rankings. Instead, researchers recognize cognitive evolution as producing diverse adaptive specializations rather than progress toward human-like intelligence.

**Theoretical Framework Shift:** The field has moved from asking "which animal is smartest?" to investigating how different cognitive abilities evolved as solutions to specific ecological challenges. This approach recognizes that cognitive abilities that appear "intelligent" in one context may be irrelevant or maladaptive in another environment.

**Domain-Specific Intelligence:** Research consistently shows that cognitive abilities vary independently—species may excel in some cognitive domains while showing limitations in others. Dolphins demonstrate impressive social cognition and vocal learning but show limitations in some tool use and causal reasoning tasks where other species excel.

**Evolutionary Context:** Cognitive abilities evolve under specific selective pressures related to ecological niches, social structures, and environmental challenges. Dolphin intelligence reflects adaptations to aquatic three-dimensional navigation, complex social dynamics, and echolocation-based sensory processing. These specializations are neither superior nor inferior to cognitive adaptations in other lineages—they represent different evolutionary solutions to different environmental challenges.


## 3. Why People Believe This

**Evidence Base:** Extensive comparative cognition research reveals sophisticated dolphin abilities alongside equally impressive capabilities in other taxa, undermining simple intelligence hierarchies.

The scientific literature on dolphin cognition is substantial and methodologically sophisticated, but research on other species has revealed cognitive abilities that challenge any simple ranking system. Modern comparative cognition approaches species-specific intelligence as adaptive specializations rather than positions on a universal intelligence scale.

**Dolphin Cognitive Capabilities - Established Findings:**

**Mirror Self-Recognition (Reiss & Marino, 2001):** Dolphins demonstrate mirror self-recognition using the mark test—they examine marks placed on their bodies using mirrors, indicating self-awareness. This capability appears around age 7 months in dolphins, earlier than in human children (18-24 months) or great apes (2-4 years). However, mirror self-recognition represents just one aspect of self-awareness, and its ecological relevance for aquatic animals remains debated.

**Signature Whistle Systems (Janik & Sayigh, 2013):** Dolphins develop individually distinctive vocalizations that function similarly to names—other dolphins can produce these "signature whistles" to refer to specific individuals. This represents a form of referential communication rare in non-human animals. However, the complexity and flexibility of these vocal systems remains limited compared to human language or even some bird communication systems.

**Cultural Transmission (Krützen et al., 2005):** Bottlenose dolphins in Shark Bay, Australia, demonstrate tool use that spreads through social learning rather than genetic inheritance. Females teach offspring to use marine sponges as foraging tools, creating cultural lineages of tool users. This cultural transmission spans multiple generations and varies geographically, indicating sophisticated social learning capabilities.

**Cooperative Hunting Strategies (Lewis & Schroeder, 2003):** Dolphins coordinate complex group hunting behaviors requiring individual role specialization, temporal synchronization, and adaptive response to changing conditions. Some populations engage in "strand feeding"—deliberately beaching themselves to catch fish—while others coordinate bubble-net feeding or drive fish into mudbanks.

**Comparative Cognitive Evidence - Other Taxa:**

**Great Ape Tool Use and Planning:** Chimpanzees and orangutans demonstrate tool manufacture, sequential tool use, and planning abilities that often exceed documented dolphin capabilities. Jane Goodall's pioneering research revealed chimpanzees modifying twigs for termite fishing, while more recent studies show tool-dependent hunting strategies and multi-step tool sequences requiring forward planning.

**Corvid Problem-Solving (Taylor et al., 2009):** New Caledonian crows manufacture and use tools with sophistication rivaling great apes, demonstrate causal reasoning about hidden mechanisms, and show planning abilities in laboratory tests. Betty the crow's spontaneous hook-bending for food retrieval and similar corvid innovations suggest problem-solving capabilities that match or exceed documented dolphin abilities in analogous tasks.

**Elephant Social Cognition (Plotnik et al., 2006):** Elephants demonstrate mirror self-recognition, cooperative problem-solving requiring coordination between multiple individuals, and complex social relationships including apparent mourning behaviors and multi-generational knowledge transmission. Their social cognitive abilities in some domains appear to exceed documented dolphin capabilities.

**Octopus Intelligence (Godfrey-Smith, 2016):** Cephalopods demonstrate complex problem-solving, episodic-like memory, and behavioral flexibility despite radically different brain architecture from vertebrates. Their cognitive abilities evolved independently and represent alternative solutions to environmental challenges that often match vertebrate problem-solving performance.

**Methodological Considerations:**

**Cross-Species Testing Challenges:** Comparative cognition research faces fundamental methodological problems when attempting to rank intelligence across species. Tests designed for one species may be inappropriate for others due to sensory differences, motor limitations, motivational factors, or ecological irrelevance.

Dolphins' aquatic environment creates particular testing challenges—many standardized cognitive assessments require terrestrial manipulation or visual discrimination tasks that may not translate meaningfully to aquatic contexts. Conversely, dolphins may possess cognitive abilities adapted to three-dimensional aquatic navigation and echolocation that are difficult to assess using land-based testing paradigms.

**Publication and Research Bias:** Dolphin cognition research receives disproportionate funding and media attention compared to research on less charismatic species. This creates apparent differences in cognitive sophistication that may reflect research investment rather than actual cognitive differences.

Additionally, positive findings about dolphin intelligence receive more publication and publicity than null results or findings showing cognitive limitations, potentially inflating perceptions of dolphin cognitive superiority relative to other species.


## 4. Verdict

**Classification: ACCURATELY STATED BUT EPISTEMOLOGICALLY PROBLEMATIC**

**Gap Category: Epistemologically-Loaded**

The claim "dolphins are the smartest animals after humans" accurately reflects widespread public belief and appears in numerous educational and media contexts. However, the claim embeds epistemological assumptions that comparative cognition research has definitively rejected—specifically, that intelligence can be meaningfully ranked linearly across species.

Dolphins do demonstrate remarkable cognitive abilities: mirror self-recognition, signature whistle communication systems, cultural transmission of tool use, sophisticated social cognition, and complex cooperative behaviors. These abilities are genuinely impressive and scientifically documented. However, great apes, corvids, elephants, and cephalopods demonstrate equally sophisticated cognitive abilities in overlapping and distinct domains.

**Why The Epistemology Matters:**

Modern comparative cognition recognizes that cognitive evolution produces diverse adaptive specializations rather than progress toward human-like intelligence. Different species face different environmental challenges and evolve cognitive solutions optimized for their ecological niches. Attempting to rank these diverse cognitive adaptations on a single intelligence scale misrepresents how cognition evolves and functions.

**The Evidence on Dolphin Cognition:**

Dolphins excel in specific cognitive domains: three-dimensional spatial navigation, vocal learning and production, social recognition and cooperation, and processing echolocation-based sensory information. These abilities reflect adaptations to aquatic environments and complex social structures.

However, dolphins show limitations in other cognitive domains where other species excel: complex tool manufacture and use (great apes and corvids), episodic memory and planning (corvids and great apes), numerical cognition (some primates and birds), and causal reasoning about hidden mechanisms (corvids and primates).

**Comparative Perspective:**

Rather than ranking second in intelligence, dolphins represent one of several independently evolved cognitive sophistication peaks, alongside great apes (adapted for complex tool use and social manipulation), corvids (adapted for flexible problem-solving and planning), elephants (adapted for complex social coordination and environmental knowledge), and cephalopods (adapted for flexible behavioral responses and camouflage-based survival strategies).

**Practical Implications:**

- Recognize dolphin cognitive sophistication without requiring superiority claims over other species
- Evaluate conservation and welfare policies based on cognitive complexity broadly defined rather than anthropocentric intelligence rankings 
- Appreciate cognitive diversity as an evolutionary achievement rather than seeking simple hierarchies
- Support research on cognitive abilities across diverse taxa rather than focusing disproportionately on charismatic megafauna

The claim's persistence reflects cultural values and commercial interests rather than scientific consensus. While dolphins deserve recognition for their remarkable cognitive abilities, these abilities represent evolutionary specialization rather than position in a universal intelligence hierarchy that comparative cognition research shows does not exist.

## 5. The Wider Picture

**Cultural and Scientific Context:**

The persistence of intelligence hierarchy claims reflects several broader phenomena in how societies understand and value animal cognition:

**Charismatic Megafauna Bias:** Large, appealing animals like dolphins receive disproportionate research attention, conservation funding, and media coverage compared to equally cognitively sophisticated but less charismatic species. This bias creates apparent hierarchies that reflect human preferences rather than objective cognitive assessments.

Marine mammals benefit particularly from this bias due to their perceived similarity to humans (large brains, complex social behaviors, apparent emotional expressiveness) and their role in marine park entertainment industries that have commercial incentives to emphasize cognitive sophistication.

**Anthropocentric Intelligence Concepts:** Human societies naturally evaluate animal cognition using human cognitive abilities as reference points, leading to overvaluation of human-like cognitive traits and undervaluation of cognitive specializations that differ from human patterns.

This anthropocentrism appears in the emphasis on mirror self-recognition, tool use, and problem-solving abilities that resemble human cognition, while undervaluing cognitive specializations like echolocation, magnetic navigation, or chemical communication that humans lack.

**Conservation and Ethics Implications:** Intelligence hierarchy claims significantly influence conservation priorities, captivity policies, and animal welfare regulations. Species perceived as more "intelligent" receive greater legal protections and conservation resources, creating ethical frameworks that may not reflect actual cognitive complexity or capacity for suffering.

The dolphin intelligence narrative has been central to anti-captivity campaigns and marine mammal protection legislation, while equally cognitively sophisticated species receive less protection due to lower public recognition of their cognitive abilities.

**Media and Educational Representation:** Nature documentaries and educational materials often present simplified intelligence hierarchies because they are easier to communicate than nuanced discussions of domain-specific cognitive adaptations. These simplifications reinforce public misconceptions that persist despite advancing scientific understanding.

**Commercial Interests:** Marine park industries have historically promoted dolphin intelligence claims to justify captivity programs and interactive experiences. These commercial interests create incentives for emphasizing dolphin cognitive uniqueness while downplaying cognitive abilities in species that are less commercially valuable or charismatic.

**Research Priorities and Funding:** The focus on dolphin intelligence has driven substantial research investment that has advanced understanding of marine mammal cognition. However, this focus may have diverted resources from studying cognitive abilities in less charismatic species, creating knowledge gaps that inadvertently support apparent intelligence hierarchies.

**Cross-Cultural Variation:** Different cultures emphasize different aspects of animal intelligence and may not share Western emphasis on dolphin cognitive superiority. Some indigenous cultures recognize sophisticated cognitive abilities in species that Western science has only recently begun to study intensively.

**Philosophical Implications:** The intelligence hierarchy approach reflects broader philosophical assumptions about consciousness, the nature of mind, and the relationship between humans and other animals. Alternative philosophical frameworks that emphasize cognitive diversity rather than hierarchical ranking may better reflect evolutionary and neurobiological reality.

**Future Research Directions:**

Emerging approaches in comparative cognition focus on understanding cognitive abilities within evolutionary and ecological contexts rather than attempting cross-species rankings. New methodologies allow assessment of cognitive abilities using species-appropriate testing paradigms rather than imposing standardized tests across diverse taxa.

Technology advances enable study of cognitive abilities in natural environments rather than artificial laboratory settings, revealing cognitive sophistication that may be invisible in traditional testing contexts. These developments consistently reveal cognitive complexity across diverse species rather than supporting simple intelligence hierarchies.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

**Status:** Not directly addressed by Snopes

Snopes has not produced a comprehensive fact-check examining the specific claim that dolphins rank second in animal intelligence hierarchies. This represents a notable gap in mainstream fact-checking, particularly given the claim's prominence in educational contexts and its influence on marine mammal conservation messaging and captivity policies.

The absence likely reflects the complex, technical nature of comparative cognition research, which requires engagement with specialized literature across multiple subdisciplines—ethology, cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and evolutionary biology—that extends beyond Snopes' typical focus on discrete factual claims with clear true/false determinations.
