# 'Snakesgiving': Florida Mom Pulls Python from Car (Video)

**Snopes Verdict:** N/A (News Story — Documented Real Event)  
**Original URL:** https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/florida-mom-python-snake/  
**Snopes Author:** Jordan Liles  
**Snopes Published:** November 24, 2021

---

## §1 — Overview and the Story

In late November 2021, in the days immediately preceding the Thanksgiving holiday, a TikTok video went comprehensively viral: a woman in a kitchen apron, standing in what appeared to be a driveway or parking lot somewhere in Florida, is seen pulling the tail of an enormous python that has coiled itself into the wheel well of a car — while her son, the video's account owner, working from the head of the snake, receives a bite in the process. The footage was captioned with the hashtag *#snakesgiving* and earned at least 5 million views across the original post and a widely-circulated "stitch" version reposted by another account.

The TikTok account behind the video was @pythoncowboy, which belongs to a Florida wildlife professional known as "Trapper Mike." The Python Cowboy has built a following by documenting his work hunting and removing invasive Burmese pythons (*Python bivittatus*) from Florida's landscapes, with content shared across both TikTok and a dedicated YouTube channel. In the Thanksgiving video, after his mother successfully helped extract the snake from the vehicle's wheel, Trapper Mike praised her performance: "Nice job, mom. You're a freakin' natural."

The story occupies an interesting position in Snopes' output: it is catalogued under "fact-check" but is better understood as a news item verifying a widely-circulated viral video — confirming that the video is genuine, that the people involved are who they appear to be, and providing ecological and zoological context for why a Florida man might encounter a large python beneath a car. Jordan Liles covered the story as part of a broader archive of Florida wildlife incidents — from gators being thrown onto rooftops to pythons trapped near chicken coops — that have become a recognizable cultural genre.

This Truth Vault entry contextualizes the viral incident within the well-documented crisis of invasive Burmese pythons in Florida, examines the ecological dimensions of that crisis, analyzes the cultural phenomenon of "Florida man/mom" viral content as a form of regional myth-making, and situates the November 2021 news cycle (including the simultaneous reporting that pythons had expanded north into the Loxahatchee Wildlife Refuge) within the broader conservation emergency.

## §2 — The Video, Its Verification, and Snopes' Coverage

Jordan Liles, reporting for Snopes on November 24, 2021, documented the TikTok video and verified key elements of its provenance. The video was posted by @pythoncowboy on TikTok prior to Thanksgiving and accumulated at least 5 million total views, including views from a reshared "stitch" version posted by user @tompowelljr. In the TikTok comments, the account holder — Trapper Mike — confirmed in his own words that he hunts pythons for a living, establishing context for why he and his mother would be handling a large python in a driveway.

The snake visible in the video was identified by commentators familiar with Florida's invasive species as a Burmese python (*Python bivittatus*), and appeared to be at least 6 feet in length based on visual assessment relative to the humans present. Though Liles notes that the exact filming location within Florida was not specified in the video, the presence of a Burmese python in a car's wheel well is entirely consistent with the animal's documented behavior in South Florida, where the species has established a well-documented invasive population.

Snopes did not rate this story as "True" or "False" in the traditional fact-check sense. It is a news item — a real event, documented on video, by a real person with a verifiable public identity and history on the platform. The Snopes article performs a different epistemic function than a typical fact check: it provides documentation and context for viral content to help readers understand what they are seeing. Liles pointed to the contemporaneous reporting — published by TheHill.com on November 10, 2021 and by The Palm Beach Post on November 9, 2021 — that invasive Burmese pythons had been sighted for the first time in Florida's Loxahatchee Wildlife Refuge, which represented a northward expansion of the species' established range in the state.

The coincidence of viral python-removal content with ecological news about pythons advancing north framed the story in a larger context: this was not simply an amusing holiday anecdote but a window into Florida's ongoing and escalating invasive reptile crisis.

## §3 — Epistemic Analysis

The epistemic novelty of this case does not lie in the falsity of a claim — the video is genuine, the event happened, and no deception is involved. Instead, the epistemic interest resides in the **mechanism by which regional ecological crises become culturally visible through viral media**, and what that process reveals about the relationship between entertainment-grade content and conservation awareness.

The "Florida man" cultural archetype — loosely, the framing of Florida as a lawless, surreal environment where encounters with dangerous wildlife, eccentric personalities, and unusual circumstances are quotidian — functions as a double-edged epistemic frame. On the one hand, it makes genuine and consequential ecological information (invasive Burmese pythons are causing documented ecological harm in southern Florida) accessible and memorable to audiences who would not otherwise engage with conservation biology reporting. On the other hand, it aestheticizes the crisis as entertainment, potentially displacing serious engagement with its causes, costs, and solutions.

Media ecology and framing research is relevant here. Entman (1993, *Journal of Communication*) defined framing as "selecting some aspects of a perceived reality and making them more salient in a communicating text, in such a way as to promote a particular problem definition, causal interpretation, moral evaluation, and/or treatment recommendation." The viral python-removal frame promotes problem definition (pythons present in human environments), but its entertainment and humor valence undermines causal interpretation and treatment recommendation. Viewers who watch the "Snakesgiving" video and laugh may not make the cognitive connection to the ecological drivers of the crisis: the pet trade, inadequate biosecurity at the U.S.-border import level, and the release or escape of captive animals into environments they can dominate.

There is an additional dimension concerning the sociology of expert amateurs. Trapper Mike/Python Cowboy is not a university researcher or state wildlife official; he is a self-taught wildlife professional who makes his living through python removal contracting. His TikTok and YouTube platforms have likely done more to communicate the texture and scale of the python crisis to general audiences than any number of academic publications. This is an instance of what Jasanoff (1990, *The Fifth Branch*) calls "lay expertise" — knowledge production and communication by individuals outside formal scientific institutions but with high-quality domain knowledge. The viral content is simultaneously ecologically low-fidelity (the entertainment framing obscures ecological stakes) and epistemically valuable (it reaches demographics that scientific publication cannot).

## §4 — Scientific and Empirical Context

**The Burmese Python Invasion:** The Burmese python (*Python bivittatus*) is native to South and Southeast Asia, where it is one of the largest snakes in the world, capable of exceeding 5 meters in length. It was introduced to Florida through the pet trade, with escaped or released animals establishing a breeding population in the Everglades by the 1980s or 1990s (Dorcas et al., 2012, *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*).

The ecological impact of the Burmese python population in southern Florida is well-documented and severe. Dorcas et al. (2012, *PNAS*) conducted a landmark study comparing mammal survey data from road transects in Everglades National Park before and after python establishment. They documented declines of 88% in raccoon sightings, 99% in opossum sightings, and 87% in white-tailed deer sightings. Bobcat sightings declined by 87.5%, and rabbits and foxes had nearly disappeared from areas of heavy python presence. This represents one of the most dramatic and rapid mammal declines documented in any invaded ecosystem globally.

**Range Expansion:** The news reported by TheHill.com and The Palm Beach Post in November 2021 — that pythons had been sighted for the first time in Florida's Arthur R. Marshall Loxahatchee National Wildlife Refuge — represents a documented northward range expansion. The Loxahatchee Refuge is in Palm Beach County, north of the Everglades, suggesting the species is expanding beyond its initially established range. Willson et al. (2011, *Biological Invasions*) modeled the potential range of Burmese pythons in North America if current trends continued, finding substantial portions of the Gulf states to be climatically suitable.

**Python Removal Efforts:** Florida has implemented multiple programs to encourage python removal, including the Florida Python Challenge (a coordinated hunting competition) and the South Florida Water Management District's Python Elimination Program, which compensates contracted hunters by the foot for removed pythons. These programs reflect recognition that uncoordinated removal is insufficient to control the population — the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission estimated the python population at tens of thousands of individuals across the Everglades ecosystem as of the early 2020s.

**Snake Behavior and Vehicle Encounters:** Large constrictors like Burmese pythons seek warm, sheltered environments for thermoregulation. Vehicle engine compartments and wheel wells, particularly after the vehicle has been running, provide attractive thermal refugia. Encounters between large pythons and vehicles, residential structures, and household spaces are documented in the peer-reviewed and gray literature on invasive python management (Reed and Rodda, 2009, *USGS Open-File Report*).

**Physical Contact Safety:** The Burmese python, while a non-venomous constrictor, is capable of inflicting significant bite wounds due to the recurved teeth characteristic of the family Pythonidae. Trapper Mike's account that he was bitten during the extraction is consistent with documented animal handling risk profiles. Florida's wildlife management guidelines for non-professionals advise against hands-on python handling without appropriate training and equipment.

## §5 — EARC/Gap Classification and Misinformation Patterns

**EARC Classification: Empirical Context Gap (E)**

This case is classified under the **Empirical Context Gap** category. There is no false claim, and no structural or rhetorical deception. Instead, the viral video circulates in a context that strips away the ecological significance of what viewers are watching. Audiences who share and comment on the video for its entertainment value are, in the overwhelming majority, not simultaneously accessing the peer-reviewed literature on Burmese python impacts in Florida, nor the concurrent news cycle documenting northward range expansion. The empirical richness of the event is present but invisible within the viral frame.

The gap encoded in this case is between **anecdotal visibility** and **systemic understanding**. The video makes the invasive python crisis viscerally visible as an individual event — a real person, a real snake, a real driveway — but does not convey the population-level, ecosystem-level dynamics that give the event its significance. This is a fundamental challenge for conservation communication: the most accessible formats (viral video, social media humor) are structurally antithetical to the kind of cumulative, probabilistic, systems-level thinking that conservation biology requires of its audiences.

The further significance of this case for Truth Vault purposes is its illustration of **authentic viral content as partial evidence**. Unlike manufactured images, AI-generated videos, or deliberately deceptive media, this video is completely genuine. Yet it still requires contextual enrichment to function as meaningful information rather than merely as entertainment. The fact-checker's role here is not debunking but scaffolding — building the context that transforms a funny holiday video into evidence within a serious ecological narrative.
