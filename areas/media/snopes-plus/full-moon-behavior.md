# "Full Moons Cause Odd Behavior and More Crime"


## 1. The Claim

The belief that full moons trigger increased crime, psychiatric episodes, emergency room visits, and general "odd behavior" remains remarkably persistent across cultures and professions. This claim appears regularly in police officer accounts, emergency medical personnel reports, psychiatric nursing observations, and popular media coverage of unusual incidents. The narrative typically suggests that lunar gravitational forces affect human behavior similarly to their effect on ocean tides, creating predictable patterns of increased social disruption during full moon periods.

Professional versions of this claim often cite experiential evidence from law enforcement officers who report busier nights during full moons, emergency room staff who observe increased psychiatric presentations, or school teachers who note more disruptive student behavior. These accounts frequently include specific percentages or dramatic increases allegedly correlated with lunar phases, lending apparent empirical weight to the broader claim.

The astronomical mechanism commonly proposed involves lunar gravitational effects on human bodily fluids, particularly brain cerebrospinal fluid or blood, supposedly creating hormonal or neurological changes that manifest as behavioral alterations. This explanation draws apparent scientific credibility from the undeniable fact that lunar gravity affects ocean tides, extending this physical phenomenon to human physiology and psychology.


## 2. What's Actually True

The "lunar effect" claim demonstrates how phenomenologically real experiences can create persistent beliefs despite contrary scientific evidence. People genuinely experience unusual events coinciding with full moons, creating powerful personal testimony that feels more convincing than statistical abstractions. This experiential evidence forms the basis for professional folklore that spreads through institutional cultures.

The temporal framing of lunar effect claims typically involves selective attention to 24-48 hour windows around full moon peaks while ignoring behavioral patterns during other lunar phases. This creates artificial periodicity that appears to support the claim while failing to account for natural variation in human behavior across all time periods.

The gravitational mechanism proposed for lunar effects demonstrates scientifically plausible reasoning applied incorrectly. While lunar gravity does affect large bodies of water, the gravitational differential across a human body is negligible compared to everyday gravitational influences. A mosquito landing on a person's arm exerts greater gravitational force differential than the moon creates between head and feet.


The persistence of lunar effect beliefs despite contrary evidence illustrates several well-documented cognitive biases operating in professional and popular contexts. Confirmation bias leads observers to notice and remember unusual events coinciding with full moons while forgetting similar events occurring during other lunar phases. This selective attention creates apparent patterns that feel statistically significant to observers.

**Illusory Correlation Formation:**

Psychologists have extensively studied how people perceive correlations between unrelated events, particularly when both events are salient or emotionally significant. Full moons are visually dramatic and culturally loaded with meaning, making them memorable anchors for unusual experiences. When bizarre crimes, psychiatric episodes, or disruptive behavior occur during full moon periods, the temporal coincidence becomes psychologically meaningful regardless of actual statistical relationship.

David Hamilton and Robert Gifford's research on illusory correlations demonstrates that people systematically overestimate the frequency of co-occurring distinctive events. Full moons and unusual behavior both represent distinctive, memorable occurrences, creating optimal conditions for illusory correlation formation. Once formed, these correlations resist correction through statistical disconfirmation.

**Professional Culture Transmission:**

The lunar effect belief demonstrates how professional folklore spreads through institutional cultures despite contradictory evidence. Senior officers train junior personnel with experiential accounts that feel authoritative, creating institutional knowledge that persists across generations. Hospital staff pass down lunar effect beliefs through shift reports and informal mentoring, establishing cultural expectations that shape perception of subsequent events.

This cultural transmission occurs independently of formal training or scientific education. Medical professionals who understand lunar gravity physics may simultaneously accept lunar behavioral effects based on experiential evidence from trusted colleagues. The social authority of professional experience often overrides abstract statistical knowledge in workplace cultures.

**Media Amplification and Selection Bias:**

News media contribute to lunar effect persistence through selective reporting of unusual incidents during full moon periods. "Full moon crime spree" stories receive disproportionate coverage compared to identical incidents during other lunar phases, creating public impression of lunar crime correlation. This media selection bias reinforces existing beliefs while providing apparent empirical support.

The entertainment industry further embeds lunar effect beliefs through fictional portrayals that treat them as established fact. Horror films, crime dramas, and supernatural thrillers consistently use full moons as plot devices for increased violence and bizarre behavior, culturally normalizing these associations across multiple generations.


## 3. Why People Believe This

The most comprehensive scientific analysis of lunar effect claims appears in James Rotton and Ivan Kelly's 1985 meta-analysis published in *Psychological Bulletin*. This landmark study examined 37 empirical investigations of purported lunar effects on human behavior, including crime rates, psychiatric admissions, emergency room visits, and various behavioral measures. The meta-analysis found no consistent relationship between lunar phases and any measured behavioral outcomes.

Rotton and Kelly's methodology involved statistical aggregation across studies with varying methodologies, sample sizes, and geographic locations. By pooling results from independent investigations conducted over several decades, their analysis achieved statistical power sufficient to detect small but consistent lunar effects if they existed. The absence of significant findings across this diverse sample strongly challenges the lunar effect hypothesis.

Subsequent research has reinforced these null findings. Raison, Klein, and Steckler (1999) conducted an updated review focusing specifically on lunar effects and mental illness, examining studies published after the original meta-analysis. Their review found continued absence of evidence for lunar influences on psychiatric presentations, suicide rates, or behavioral disturbances in clinical populations.

Modern statistical analysis reveals that many early studies reporting lunar effects suffered from methodological problems including small sample sizes, selective reporting of significant findings, and failure to control for confounding variables. When these methodological issues are corrected, apparent lunar correlations typically disappear or reverse direction across different studies.

The National Institute of Mental Health has never endorsed lunar effect claims despite extensive research into environmental factors affecting mental health. Major psychiatric and psychological professional organizations similarly find insufficient evidence to support clinical consideration of lunar phases in treatment planning or risk assessment.

**Astronomical Context and Physical Mechanisms:**

Gravitational analysis reveals the physical implausibility of lunar behavioral effects. The moon's gravitational force follows an inverse square law, creating differential pull between the near and far sides of objects. For Earth's oceans, this differential operates across thousands of kilometers, generating significant tidal forces. For human bodies, the same differential operates across approximately two meters (head to toe), creating forces measured in nanometers per second squared—effectively zero.

Ocean tides result from gravitational differential across Earth's diameter (approximately 12,800 kilometers) combined with Earth's rotation and the orbital mechanics of the Earth-moon system. Human bodies lack the spatial scale, fluid dynamics, and mechanical freedom necessary for analogous tidal effects. The comparison between ocean tides and human "biological tides" represents a false analogy based on superficial similarity rather than physical mechanism.

The temporal patterns of actual tides also contradict lunar effect claims. Tides cycle twice daily with varying amplitude based on lunar phase, geographic location, and seasonal factors. If human behavior followed similar patterns, effects should appear twice daily with predictable variation throughout lunar cycles, not just during full moons. No such patterns appear in behavioral data.


## 4. Verdict

**CONTRAST: Scientifically Unfounded Despite Phenomenological Reality**

The claim that full moons cause increased crime, odd behavior, or psychiatric episodes lacks scientific support despite widespread professional and popular belief. This assessment requires contrasting the robust empirical evidence against lunar effects with the phenomenologically real experiences that sustain these beliefs across cultures and professions.

**Scientific Consensus Against Lunar Effects:**

1. **Meta-Analytic Evidence**: Rotton and Kelly's 1985 analysis of 37 studies found no consistent lunar behavioral effects across diverse methodologies and populations.

2. **Gravitational Physics**: Lunar gravitational forces on human bodies are negligible compared to everyday influences, making proposed tidal mechanisms physically implausible.

3. **Professional Organization Positions**: Major medical, psychiatric, and psychological organizations find insufficient evidence for clinical consideration of lunar phases.

4. **Large-Scale Data Analysis**: Modern statistical analysis of crime databases, hospital records, and emergency service logs consistently fails to find significant lunar correlations.

**Cognitive and Cultural Mechanisms Explaining Persistence:**

1. **Confirmation Bias**: Selective attention to unusual events during full moons while ignoring similar events during other phases creates apparent patterns.

2. **Illusory Correlation**: Full moons and bizarre behavior are both distinctive, memorable events that become psychologically linked despite statistical independence.

3. **Professional Folklore**: Institutional cultures transmit lunar effect beliefs through experiential accounts that feel more authoritative than statistical abstractions.

4. **Media Selection Bias**: Disproportionate coverage of full moon incidents reinforces public perception of lunar behavioral correlation.

**Practical Implications:**

Rather than supernatural influence, apparent lunar behavioral patterns reflect normal statistical variation, cognitive biases in pattern perception, and cultural expectations that shape both behavior and observation. Emergency services may experience busy periods during full moons due to increased outdoor activity from enhanced visibility, but this operates through illumination rather than mysterious forces.

**Educational Priority**: Correcting lunar effect misconceptions requires addressing both statistical evidence and the psychological mechanisms that sustain false beliefs despite contrary data. Professional training should include specific instruction on cognitive biases affecting pattern perception, particularly in high-stress occupations where anecdotal evidence feels compelling.

The lunar effect represents a classic example of how phenomenologically real experiences can create persistent beliefs that contradict systematic evidence. Understanding this phenomenon helps explain broader patterns of myth persistence in professional cultures and highlights the importance of evidence-based practice in fields affecting public safety and health care.

## 5. The Wider Picture

The lunar effect myth exemplifies broader patterns in how humans process statistical relationships and causal attribution. Similar beliefs persist regarding seasonal affective patterns, holiday crime rates, and various environmental influences on behavior. In each case, selective attention, confirmation bias, and cultural transmission create apparent patterns that feel empirically valid despite lacking statistical support.

**Cross-Cultural Persistence:**

Lunar behavioral beliefs appear across diverse cultures with varying astronomical knowledge and mythological traditions. While specific manifestations differ (werewolf transformations in European folklore, general madness in English "lunacy" concepts, spiritual possession in various traditions), the core assumption of lunar behavioral influence shows remarkable cross-cultural stability. This universality suggests deep-seated cognitive or cultural mechanisms rather than empirical observation.

Anthropological research reveals that lunar behavioral beliefs often correlate with cultures' reliance on lunar calendars for agricultural, religious, or social organization. Societies that structure important activities around lunar cycles naturally develop beliefs about lunar influence on human affairs. These cultural frameworks then shape perception of behavioral patterns, creating self-reinforcing belief systems.

**Legal and Professional Implications:**

Despite lack of scientific support, lunar effect beliefs continue influencing professional practices in law enforcement, healthcare, and education. Some police departments still schedule additional personnel during full moon periods, emergency rooms prepare for increased psychiatric presentations, and schools brace for disruptive behavior. These practices persist through institutional momentum rather than evidence-based policy.

Legal implications occasionally arise when lunar timing factors into criminal cases or professional malpractice claims. While courts generally reject lunar influence as mitigating factors, the beliefs' persistence in professional cultures can affect witness testimony, expert opinions, and jury perceptions. Educational efforts within professional communities remain necessary to counter these influences.

**Technology and Modern Investigation:**

Digital data collection and analysis capabilities now allow unprecedented statistical investigation of lunar effect claims. Hospital admission databases, police incident reports, and emergency service logs can be analyzed across years or decades to detect subtle patterns that individual observation might miss. Consistently, such analyses fail to find significant lunar correlations when proper statistical controls are applied.

The availability of large datasets also reveals previously hidden patterns that explain some lunar effect observations. For example, full moons provide increased ambient light that may affect outdoor criminal activity in areas with limited artificial lighting. However, this effect operates through illumination rather than gravitational or mysterious influences, and disappears in well-lit urban environments where most lunar effect claims originate.

**Evolutionary and Historical Perspectives:**

Some researchers have investigated whether lunar behavioral sensitivity might have provided evolutionary advantages to human ancestors, creating vestigial behavioral responses that persist despite modern irrelevance. However, no plausible evolutionary mechanism has been identified for lunar behavioral sensitivity in terrestrial mammals, and comparative studies of related primates show no lunar behavioral patterns.

Historical analysis reveals that lunar behavioral beliefs intensified during certain periods, particularly the 18th and 19th centuries when "lunacy" became legally and medically codified. These historical fluctuations in belief intensity suggest cultural rather than biological origins for lunar effect concepts.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

Snopes has not systematically addressed the lunar effect claim in a comprehensive fact-check, despite its widespread acceptance in popular culture and professional folklore. The platform's coverage of moon-related claims tends to focus on specific viral incidents allegedly occurring during full moons rather than examining the systematic evidence for or against lunar behavioral influence.

This gap in coverage allows the claim to persist in professional circles where empirical evidence might be expected to govern belief. Police departments, hospitals, and schools continue operating under assumptions about lunar effects without systematic fact-checking scrutiny of the underlying scientific literature.
