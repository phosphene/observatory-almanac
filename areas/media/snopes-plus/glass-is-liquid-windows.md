# "Glass Is Actually a Liquid, Which Is Why Old Windows Are Thicker at the Bottom"


## 1. The Claim

The persistent claim that "glass is actually a liquid" and that this explains why old windows appear thicker at the bottom has captivated popular imagination for generations. This assertion combines two related propositions: first, that glass should be classified as a liquid rather than a solid, and second, that observable thickness variations in historical windows result from centuries of gravitational flow.

The claim's appeal lies in its apparent explanation of a seemingly mysterious phenomenon using counterintuitive physics. It suggests that matter behaves in ways that contradict everyday experience—that something appearing solid can actually be flowing imperceptibly around us. This narrative satisfies our fascination with hidden processes and provides a concrete example of physics operating at timescales beyond human perception.

The myth has proven remarkably durable, appearing in educational contexts, museum displays, and casual conversation. Its persistence reflects both genuine confusion about glass's unusual properties and the compelling nature of explanations that reveal supposedly secret truths about familiar objects.


## 2. What's Actually True

### The Physics of Glass Structure

Glass occupies a unique position in materials science that makes the "liquid" classification superficially plausible. Unlike typical solids, glass lacks long-range crystalline order. Its molecular structure resembles that of a liquid frozen in place—a configuration that has led to the description of glass as having "liquid-like structure with solid-like properties."

**Amorphous vs. Crystalline Structure:** When most materials solidify, their atoms arrange in regular, repeating patterns called crystals. Glass formation bypasses this crystallization process through rapid cooling that "freezes" the random molecular arrangement of the liquid state. This creates an amorphous (non-crystalline) solid with properties distinct from both typical solids and liquids.

**The Glass Transition:** Glass formation involves a glass transition rather than a sharp melting point. As temperature decreases, the material's viscosity increases gradually until molecular motion becomes so restricted that the material behaves as a solid. This transition occurs over a temperature range, not at a specific point, contributing to confusion about glass's fundamental state.

**Viscosity and Flow:** The key physical property distinguishing solids from liquids is viscosity—resistance to flow. Liquids have relatively low viscosity and conform to container shapes over short timescales. Solids have effectively infinite viscosity under normal conditions, maintaining their shape indefinitely.

### Quantitative Analysis of Glass Flow

The most decisive refutation of the glass flow hypothesis comes from quantitative analysis of the timescales involved. Edgar Zanotto's landmark 1998 study in the American Journal of Physics calculated the relaxation time for medieval glass at room temperature.

**Zanotto's Calculations:** Using established theories of glass physics, Zanotto determined that the structural relaxation time for typical soda-lime glass at 20°C (68°F) is approximately 10^32 years. measurable changes in glass shape due to flow would require time periods vastly exceeding the age of the universe (approximately 1.4 × 10^10 years).

**Comparative Analysis:** To put this in perspective, if a medieval glass window had been flowing continuously since the construction of Notre Dame Cathedral (begun 1163 CE), the total deformation after 860 years would be immeasurably small—less than the precision of any available measurement technique.

**Temperature Dependence:** Glass viscosity decreases dramatically with temperature. At glass-working temperatures (around 1000°C), glass flows readily. However, the exponential relationship between temperature and viscosity means that even modest cooling produces enormous increases in flow resistance.

### Historical Manufacturing Evidence

The thickness variations in old windows correlate precisely with known historical manufacturing processes, providing alternative explanations that require no appeal to flow over time.

**Crown Glass Process:** Medieval and early modern window glass was typically produced using the crown glass method. Glassblowers would spin molten glass into large, flat discs up to four feet in diameter. These discs, called "crowns," were naturally thicker at the center (where the pontil rod attached) and thinner toward the edges.

**Installation Practices:** When crown glass discs were cut into panes, glaziers typically installed pieces with the thicker edge at the bottom for structural stability. This practice was especially important for larger panes that needed to support their own weight within the window frame.

**Cylinder Glass:** An alternative historical method involved blowing glass into cylindrical shapes, then cutting and flattening them into sheets. This process also produced thickness variations due to the inherent difficulties of achieving uniform wall thickness in hand-blown glass.

**Regional Variations:** Different regions developed distinct glassmaking traditions that left characteristic signatures in window thickness patterns. These variations correspond to documented historical practices rather than uniform gravitational effects.


The Snopes "False" verdict accurately reflects scientific consensus, but the complexity underlying this assessment reveals important epistemological considerations about materials classification and the nature of scientific explanation.

**Definitional Precision:** The claim highlights the importance of precise scientific definitions. Glass is neither a simple solid nor a simple liquid but an amorphous solid with unique properties that don't fit neatly into everyday categories. The myth arises partly from attempts to force glass into familiar classifications rather than accepting its distinctive nature.

**Observational vs. Explanatory Evidence:** The existence of thick-bottomed old windows is observational fact, but the flow explanation is theoretical inference. The myth demonstrates how observations can be correctly identified while explanations remain flawed.

**Scale and Perception:** Human perception operates on timescales that make certain physical processes effectively invisible. The glass flow myth exploits this perceptual limitation by proposing changes so slow they cannot be directly observed but seemingly fast enough to produce visible effects over historical periods.


## 3. Why People Believe This

### The Appeal of Hidden Processes

The glass flow myth exemplifies several cognitive patterns that make certain types of misinformation particularly compelling:

**Temporal Transcendence:** The claim suggests processes operating on timescales beyond human perception, appealing to our fascination with phenomena that unfold across centuries or millennia. This temporal scale makes the claim difficult to test through direct observation while lending it an air of profound scientific insight.

**Counterintuitive Physics:** The assertion that solid-appearing objects are actually flowing contradicts everyday experience in ways that feel revelatory. People enjoy learning that their basic assumptions about reality are incorrect, especially when the revelation involves sophisticated scientific concepts.

**Observable Evidence:** Old windows do show thickness variations that appear to support the flow hypothesis. This correlation between prediction and observation provides superficial validation that makes the claim feel scientifically grounded.

**Authority Reinforcement:** The myth has been repeated by teachers, tour guides, and even some scientists, creating a sense of expert consensus that discourages critical examination.

### Educational System Vulnerabilities

The persistence of this myth reveals specific vulnerabilities in science education:

**Materials Science Gaps:** Most general science education provides limited coverage of materials science concepts like amorphous solids, glass transitions, and viscosity. These knowledge gaps make students vulnerable to oversimplified explanations that conflate structural properties with behavioral classifications.

**Quantitative Literacy:** The myth persists partly because few people perform the calculations needed to evaluate the timescales involved. Even basic order-of-magnitude estimations would reveal the implausibility of measurable flow over historical periods.

**Historical Context Neglect:** Science education often presents modern understandings without adequate historical context. Students learning about glass properties may not encounter information about historical manufacturing methods that provide alternative explanations for observed phenomena.

### Peer Review and Correction Mechanisms

The glass flow myth demonstrates how even false claims can achieve widespread acceptance when they fill explanatory gaps in popular understanding:

**Scientific Communication Delays:** While materials scientists have understood glass physics for decades, this knowledge has been slow to penetrate general education and popular culture. The lag between expert consensus and public understanding creates opportunities for misinformation to persist.

**Correction Resistance:** Once established, false explanations become self-reinforcing. People who have shared the glass flow explanation may resist correction to avoid admitting error, while those who hear corrections may doubt them if the false explanation came from trusted sources.

**Institutional Inertia:** Museums, textbooks, and educational materials may perpetuate outdated information long after scientific understanding has evolved. Correcting these institutional sources requires coordinated effort across multiple organizations.


## 4. Verdict

**Classification:** REJECTED on both theoretical and empirical grounds.

The claim fails fundamental tests of physics and materials science. Glass is properly classified as an amorphous solid with viscosity so high that flow over historical timescales is physically impossible. Observable window thickness variations have well-documented explanations in historical manufacturing processes that require no appeal to temporal flow.

**Epistemological Assessment:** This myth demonstrates how genuine scientific complexity (glass's unusual properties) can be oversimplified into appealing but incorrect explanations. It highlights the importance of quantitative reasoning and historical context in evaluating scientific claims.

**Educational Value:** The glass case provides excellent opportunities for teaching materials science concepts, quantitative reasoning, and historical technology. It illustrates how correlation (old windows are thicker at bottom) does not imply causation (flow over time) and demonstrates the importance of considering alternative explanations.

**Recommendation:** Educational materials should explicitly address this myth while teaching correct glass physics and historical manufacturing methods. The case can serve as a model for developing critical thinking skills about materials properties and temporal processes.

The claim's persistence reflects the need for improved science communication that acknowledges the genuine complexity of materials while providing accurate explanations accessible to general audiences. In the broader context of scientific misinformation, the glass myth represents a relatively benign example that nonetheless illustrates important principles about how false explanations can achieve widespread acceptance when they fill gaps in popular understanding.

## 5. The Wider Picture

### Comparative Materials Myths

The glass flow claim belongs to a broader category of myths about materials behaving in unexpected ways:

- **Metal fatigue misconceptions:** Claims that metal objects "get tired" often misunderstand the physical processes underlying material failure.
- **Plastic degradation myths:** Exaggerated claims about plastic persistence sometimes misrepresent the complexity of environmental breakdown processes.
- **Concrete aging folklore:** Various myths about concrete behavior over time often ignore the chemistry of cement hydration and carbonation.

These myths share common patterns: they identify genuine material properties but provide oversimplified or incorrect explanations for observable phenomena.

### Historical Technology Understanding

The glass case illustrates broader challenges in understanding historical technology:

**Manufacturing Process Knowledge:** Modern people often lack familiarity with pre-industrial manufacturing techniques, creating knowledge gaps that folklore can fill.

**Material Property Evolution:** Contemporary materials often behave differently than their historical counterparts, making direct analogies problematic.

**Documentation Gaps:** Historical records of manufacturing techniques may be incomplete or technical details may have been lost, requiring inference from physical evidence.

### Scientific Literacy and Quantitative Reasoning

The persistence of the glass flow myth reflects broader issues in scientific literacy:

**Order-of-Magnitude Thinking:** Many people lack the quantitative reasoning skills needed to evaluate claims involving large numbers, long timescales, or exponential relationships.

**Physical Intuition:** General education often fails to develop robust physical intuition about material properties, rates of change, and scale relationships.

**Source Evaluation:** People may accept scientific-sounding claims without checking authoritative sources or understanding the difference between speculation and established fact.

### Contemporary Relevance

Understanding glass physics has practical importance for:

**Conservation Science:** Proper preservation of historical windows requires accurate understanding of their material properties and aging mechanisms.

**Materials Engineering:** Advanced glass technologies for electronics, optics, and construction depend on precise control of glass properties that requires accurate theoretical understanding.

**Science Communication:** The glass myth serves as a case study in how scientific misinformation spreads and how accurate information can be effectively communicated to general audiences.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

Snopes addresses this claim with a clear "False" rating, correctly identifying it as a conflation of glass's unusual structural properties with liquid behavior. Their analysis notes that while glass lacks the crystalline structure typical of solids, it is properly classified as an amorphous solid rather than a liquid.

The Snopes treatment explains that old window thickness variations result from historical manufacturing techniques, particularly the crown glass process used in medieval and early modern periods. They note that the timescales required for any theoretical glass flow would exceed the age of historical buildings by many orders of magnitude.

However, the Snopes analysis, while accurate, does not fully explore the scientific complexity that makes this myth particularly instructive. The claim touches on fundamental questions in materials science about the nature of the glass transition and the definition of solid versus liquid states that remain areas of active research.
