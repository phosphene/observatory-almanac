## The Claim

On April 18, 2024, an account on X (formerly Twitter), @AlwaysFrosted2, posted a striking close-up image of what appeared to be Jupiter's atmospheric band structure — swirling, multicoloured regions of banded turbulence resembling the visual signature of the gas giant's cloud layers — with the caption: "Using my Nikon Z 8, I captured this amazing shot of Jupiter." The implication was that the poster had obtained, with a consumer prosumer mirrorless camera released the previous year, close-up planetary imagery competitive with space agency photographs. "Looks like your coffee table, but it's as good as anything NASA puts out," one commenter responded, capturing the mix of impressed credulity and gentle scepticism the image provoked.

The image was immediately arresting. Jupiter's visible surface — the top of its outer cloud layer — is genuinely one of the most visually spectacular phenomena in the solar system: a dynamic, structurally complex band system driven by powerful convective circulation and differential rotation at different Jovian latitudes. The Great Red Spot (a persistent anticyclonic storm, continuous for at least 350 years according to Simon, A.A. et al., 2018, *The Astronomical Journal*), the folded filamentary structures of the North Equatorial Belt, and the multiple jet streams that generate complex turbulent boundaries between adjacent bands produce an aesthetic of organised yet wildly dynamic complexity. Photographs of Jupiter — particularly high-resolution close-ups from space missions — are among the most visually striking images produced by planetary science.

The problem was not that the image was unattractive or implausible as planetary imagery. It was that it had been misidentified. It showed a stone slab.

## What's Actually True

Snopes fact-checker Aleksandra Wrona applied a Google reverse-image search to the photograph and found that it had been shared on Reddit in 2018 — six years before its April 2024 posting. The 2018 Reddit post was captioned "a stone slab that looks like Jupiter's storms" and was shared on r/mildlyinteresting. The original poster specified that the stone in the photograph was "Brentwood" — a quartzite sandstone found in Tennessee, known for its distinctive visual resemblance to petrified wood and, apparently, to the cloud banding structures of Jupiter (Reddit, r/mildlyinteresting, September 2018).

Two pieces of additional evidence confirm the misattribution. First, the Nikon Z 8 — the camera credited in the April 2024 post — was not released until May 10, 2023, according to Nikon's official press release covering the camera's launch (Nikon Corporation, 2023, Product Announcement). This makes the stated provenance technically impossible as stated: the image that appeared on Reddit in 2018 could not have been taken with hardware that did not exist until 2023. This is not conclusive on its own — it is possible (if implausible) that the original 2018 image was re-used by someone who then falsely attributed it to their Z 8 — but it substantially undermines the credibility of the claim.

Second, the visual match between the stone slab and Jovian cloud structures, while genuinely impressive, is explicable through well-understood geological mechanisms. Brentwood quartzite sandstone from Tennessee forms under conditions of sedimentary lamination followed by diagenetic recrystallisation, producing a banded and folded mineral microstructure in which iron oxide impurities generate rich orange-brown colouration in patterns that mimic, at macroscopic scale, the fluid dynamic patterns of atmospheric banding. The resemblance is not coincidental in the aesthetic sense — both structures arise from fluid or semi-fluid material undergoing layered deposition or flow under differential conditions — but it does not require any connection between the geological and astronomical phenomena.

As corroborating astronomical context, Snopes located an authentic close-up of Jupiter's Great Red Spot region captured by the Voyager 1 spacecraft in March 1979, available through Getty Images (Oxford Science Archive/Print Collector/Getty Images). The Voyager image, captured at a distance of 278,000 kilometres during the spacecraft's Jupiter flyby, shows the turbulent boundary region surrounding the Great Red Spot with a resolution and dynamism that no Earth-based consumer camera could replicate — let alone surpass.

## Why Stone Looks Like Jupiter: Pareidolia, Geology, and Planetary Science

The core epistemic interest of this case is not the misattribution — that is a standard miscaptioning — but the underlying phenomenon that makes the misattribution visually compelling: a piece of sedimentary rock from Tennessee is genuinely, strikingly reminiscent of the cloud structures of the solar system's largest planet. Understanding why this is so illuminates both geological and planetary atmospheric processes.

**The physics of banding in fluids and sediments.** Jupiter's distinctive atmospheric bands — the alternating zones (lighter, upwelling) and belts (darker, sinking) — arise from a combination of differential rotation (Jupiter's equatorial regions rotate faster than polar regions), deep convective heat transport from the interior, and the Coriolis effect of the rapid Jovian day (approximately 10 hours). These forces create a quasi-two-dimensional turbulent flow regime constrained into latitudinal bands. Theoretical and observational work by Ingersoll, A.P. (1990, *Science*) established that Jupiter's bands are the result of deep atmospheric dynamics rather than solely surface-layer meteorology, and that the band widths scale with the Rossby deformation radius — the characteristic length scale at which Coriolis effects become important.

Sedimentary banding in quartzite sandstones arises from episodic deposition: alternating cycles of sediment influx, mineral composition variation, and diagenetic mineral migration produce alternating bands of differing colour and texture in the rock record. When the rock is subsequently subjected to tectonic deformation — folding, shearing, metamorphic recrystallisation — these original sedimentary bands develop folded, convoluted geometries that visually recall fluid flow patterns. Treagus, J.E. (2003, *Journal of Structural Geology*) documented the mathematical relationship between buckle-fold geometry in metamorphic rocks and fluid flow equations, noting that the same mathematical frameworks (particularly those derived from Rayleigh–Taylor instability theory) describe both geological folding and fluid interface instabilities.

The epistemic novelty here is structural: geological pareidolia of the Jovian type is not random or accidental. It is a symptom of deep mathematical commonality between geological and atmospheric dynamic processes. The same equations that govern viscous flow instabilities appear in both domains, producing similar visual signatures at vastly different scales. A visually literate observer who has seen both sets of phenomena might find the resemblance *less* surprising than a naive viewer, because they would recognise both as instances of the same general class of layered-instability pattern.

**Pareidolia as a cross-domain recognition mechanism.** The human visual system's tendency to find patterns — to see faces in clouds, animals in rock formations, familiar objects in random textures — is a well-documented cognitive tendency with deep evolutionary roots. Ramachandran, V.S. & Hirstein, W. (1997, *Journal of Consciousness Studies*) discussed the neural basis of object recognition, arguing that pattern-finding is a fundamental cognitive strategy that evolved in contexts where false positives (seeing a predator that isn't there) are less costly than false negatives (missing a predator that is). Applied to the Jupiter-stone comparison, "geological pareidolia" is not a failure of reasoning but an accurate recognition of genuine visual similarity — the error is in identifying the source.

The case falls into a specific category that might be called *accurate visual recognition with false source attribution*: the viewer correctly identifies that the image resembles Jupiter's cloud bands; the error is in concluding that the resemblance indicates the image *is* Jupiter's cloud bands rather than something that *looks like* them. This source-misattribution error is distinct from fully delusional pattern recognition (seeing Jupiter bands where there is no relevant visual structure) and deserves its own analytical category.

## Why People Believe This

The miscaptioning of this image was rapidly and widely accepted for reasons that go beyond inattention or credulity.

**The prosumer astronomy context.** Consumer astrophotography has undergone a genuine revolution since the early 2010s. The combination of affordable CMOS sensors with high quantum efficiency, computerised equatorial tracking mounts, and computational post-processing approaches such as lucky imaging and atmospheric dispersion correction has made it genuinely possible for amateur astronomers to produce Jupiter images of remarkable quality. Damian Peach, an amateur astrophotographer in the United Kingdom, produced Jupiter images from Earth-based amateur equipment in the 2000s that were compared favourably by professional planetary scientists to space-probe imagery of equivalent resolution (Peach, D., 2012, presentations at Association of Lunar & Planetary Observers). A viewer who is even broadly aware of the amateur astrophotography revolution might find the claim "I took this with my Nikon Z 8" somewhat surprising but not categorically implausible — a slight misunderstanding of the equipment's capabilities rather than a wholesale delusion.

**The NASA comparison frame.** The commenter's observation that the image looked like "anything NASA puts out" functioned as a credibility anchor. If viewers have seen NASA's high-resolution inner solar system images — particularly the dramatic outputs from the Juno mission, which began producing unprecedented close-up Jupiter imagery from 2016 onwards — they may have a visual model of "what good Jupiter photography looks like" that the stone slab genuinely resembles. The Juno JunoCam instrument produces richly colourful, turbulence-dominated images of Jovian cloud structures (Orton, G.S. et al., 2017, *Geophysical Research Letters*) that do share visual characteristics with the Brentwood quartzite image. The visual similarity that drove the initial misattribution is not illusory.

**The six-year delay.** The image appeared on Reddit in 2018 without significant viral spread. By 2024, its Reddit provenance was unknown to the overwhelming majority of viewers who encountered it. Images do not carry their histories with them across platforms. Six years of platform migration had effectively erased the original context for any viewer approaching the image for the first time in April 2024.

**Equipment specificity as credibility cue.** The specific mention of the "Nikon Z 8" functions as a precision-of-detail credibility cue. False claims that include specific, checkable details tend to be read as more credible because they appear to commit the claimant to verifiable facts. The irony in this case is that the very specificity of the equipment claim — the Nikon Z 8 — was what allowed the claim to be definitively refuted, since the camera was not released in time to have been used for the 2018 image. Precision-of-detail is a credibility cue that functions bidirectionally: when the detail is consistent with the claim, it adds credibility; when the detail is inconsistent, it provides a falsification edge.

## Verdict

**Miscaptioned.** The image shows a stone slab — Brentwood quartzite sandstone from Tennessee — that bears a striking visual resemblance to Jupiter's cloud banding structures. It was posted to Reddit in 2018 as "a stone slab that looks like Jupiter's storms." The April 2024 claim that the image was a photograph of Jupiter taken with a Nikon Z 8 is false: the Nikon Z 8 did not exist in 2018, and no consumer mirrorless camera can produce close-up planetary imagery resembling space mission photography. The resemblance between the stone slab and Jovian cloud structures is genuine and explicable through the mathematical commonality between geological folding dynamics and fluid atmospheric instability — both processes generate similar visual signatures through related physics.

## The Wider Picture

The Jupiter stone case illuminates several intersecting areas of geoscience, planetary science, cognitive psychology, and media forensics that are individually well-studied but rarely brought together.

**The geological record of fluid dynamics.** The study of geological structures as frozen records of ancient fluid dynamics is a cornerstone of structural geology and metamorphic petrology. Treagus, J.E. (2003, *Journal of Structural Geology*) demonstrated that fold geometries in metamorphic rocks provide quantitative evidence about the rheological properties of rocks during deformation — effectively reading the past viscosity and strain rate of rock masses from their current fold geometry. The Brentwood quartzite's visual character is thus not just aesthetically interesting: it encodes quantitative information about the conditions under which it formed.

**Planetary atmosphere dynamics: the Juno era.** Since its arrival in Jupiter's orbit in July 2016, the Juno spacecraft has transformed understanding of Jovian atmospheric structure. Bolton, S.J. et al. (2017, *Science*) reported the first gravity field measurements constraining the depth of Jupiter's zonal jets to at least 3,000 kilometres below the cloud tops — far deeper than previously estimated. This has implications for the mechanisms that generate the visual banding: the jets are not shallow meteorological features but extend deep into the hydrogen envelope. The visual structures that make Jupiter photographs so striking are surface expressions of a dynamical system that penetrates a significant fraction of the planet's radius.

**Reverse image search methodology.** The straightforward application of Google reverse-image search in this fact-check is worth noting as a methodological exemplar. For claims involving supposedly novel or uniquely captured photographs, reverse-image search is typically the first and most productive forensic tool. It requires no specialist software, is freely accessible, and in this case produced the definitive evidence (the 2018 Reddit post) in a single step. The fact that this relatively simple tool was sufficient to debunk the claim underscores both the effectiveness of the technique and the ease with which it is neglected by casual sharers of viral photographic content.

**Astrophotography and public perception.** The claim that a consumer camera could capture close-up Jupiter imagery "as good as anything NASA puts out" significantly misrepresents both what consumer cameras can achieve and what NASA's planetary science cameras do. Even the best Earth-based amateur astrophotography of Jupiter — which has indeed become extraordinary in recent decades — produces full-disk or wide-format images in which details are resolved at scales of hundreds of kilometres (atmospheric features visible to the naked eye through backyard telescopes). NASA's Juno JunoCam and Voyager era systems produce close-up images at scales of tens to hundreds of kilometres at the cloud-top level, from orbital proximity. These are categorically different imaging achievements and require categorically different hardware. Consumer astronomy education benefits from accurate calibration of what different instrumentation can and cannot achieve.

## How Fact-Checkers Handle It

Snopes' approach to the Jupiter-stone claim is clean and efficient, with several notable methodological strengths.

**The equipment falsification approach.** Identifying the Nikon Z 8's May 2023 release date as a falsifying constraint is an elegant and decisive verification step. It does not require expert planetary science opinion, advanced forensic tools, or deep investigation: a public product release date is verifiable in seconds. The elegance of this approach lies in the fact that the claimant's choice of a specific, recently released camera model — presumably intended as a realism-enhancing detail — instead provided the clearest evidence of the claim's falsity. The more specific the false claim, the more falsifiable it is.

**Providing authentic context.** The inclusion of an authentic Voyager 1 image of Jupiter via Getty Images provides readers with a direct visual comparison between genuine NASA planetary photography and the claimed consumer camera image, allowing them to calibrate the significant difference in resolution, dynamic range, and visual character. This contextual anchoring is valuable beyond the specific debunking: it educates readers about what authentic planetary imagery looks like.

**The pareidolia explanation gap.** What the Snopes fact-check does not provide is an explanation for *why* the stone looks like Jupiter — why the resemblance is so compelling and what it tells us about underlying physical processes. This is understandable given the format constraints of a short fact-check, but it is the genuinely novel and interesting scientific content in the story. The miscaptioning is the error to correct; the geological-astronomical visual convergence is the phenomenon worth understanding. A fuller treatment would explain the mathematical kinship between rock-folding dynamics and atmospheric band instabilities, turning a debunking into an education about fluid dynamics operating at radically different scales.

**Reader calibration on amateur astrophotography.** The fact-check does not provide guidance on what consumer cameras can and cannot achieve in astrophotography — an omission that leaves open the implicit false impression that "of course you can't photograph Jupiter like that" without explaining *how* you actually can photograph Jupiter with amateur equipment (within resolution limits), and what hardware and techniques genuine planetary astrophotography requires. This calibration gap invites a residual misunderstanding: that the claim was wrong because consumer cameras are useless for planetary imaging, rather than because the specific image was a stone slab misidentified.

The Jupiter stone is ultimately a case about the limits of visual inference from appearance alone. The stone looks like Jupiter. That it looks like Jupiter is genuinely interesting, scientifically. That it *is* Jupiter is false. The gap between "looks like" and "is" is the fundamental epistemological problem that photographic fact-checking navigates every time it opens a new case.
