# Liberal College Millennials Are Less Biologically Fit?

## 1. The Claim

Five days after the 2016 United States presidential election, Mike Adams — the self-styled "Health Ranger" and founder of the conspiracy-oriented Natural News network — published an article titled "TRIGGER WARNING: Why precious college snowflakes will eliminate themselves from the human gene pool at the next natural selection event." The timing was not incidental. The article appeared on November 12, 2016, while college campuses across the country were organizing vigils, protests, and counseling sessions in response to Donald Trump's victory. Adams seized on the emotional tenor of these responses to advance a sweeping biological claim: that the characteristics of stereotypical millennial liberals — their emotional sensitivity, their preference for cooperation over competition, their rejection of traditional gender hierarchies — render them less competitive organisms who are fundamentally "in discord with the natural world."

Adams structured his argument around what he called three "universal traits" of successful biological organisms. First, **competition**: winners survive, losers perish, and only winners pass on their genes. Second, **adaptation**: successful organisms are those that display high adaptive flexibility, able to shift strategies in response to environmental change. Third, **strong males produce successful offspring**: Adams claimed that patriarchal social structures are a universal feature of the natural world, that dominant males monopolize reproductive access, and that any social arrangement deviating from this template is biologically dysfunctional.

From these premises, Adams concluded that liberal millennials — whom he characterized as emotionally fragile, competition-averse, and hostile to masculine dominance — would "eliminate themselves from the human gene pool" at the next "natural selection event." The rhetorical architecture is worth noting. Adams did not merely argue that liberal political positions are wrong; he argued that holding them is literally lethal in evolutionary terms. The claim transforms a political disagreement into a biological prophecy: nature itself, Adams asserted, will punish progressivism with extinction.

The rhetorical purpose is transparent. By cloaking political contempt in the language of evolutionary biology, Adams accomplished several things simultaneously: he delegitimized emotional responses to political events as signs of biological weakness, he naturalized conservative social hierarchies as the only viable biological template, and he implied that the political opposition was not merely incorrect but doomed by the implacable machinery of natural selection. This is not a scientific argument dressed up for popular consumption. It is a political argument dressed up as science, exploiting the cultural authority of evolutionary biology to score points in a partisan conflict five days old.

## 2. What's Actually True

Snopes rated this claim **False**, and the scientific case for that verdict is overwhelming across every axis Adams attempted to use.

**Competition is not the only driver of evolutionary success.** Adams' first "universal trait" — that winners survive by outcompeting losers — reflects a cartoon version of Darwinian theory that professional evolutionary biologists abandoned generations ago. Cooperation is not merely present in nature; it is a powerful engine of fitness in its own right. The most striking illustration comes from vampire bats (*Desmodus rotundus*). Carter and Wilkinson (2013, *Proceedings of the Royal Society B*) demonstrated that vampire bats share regurgitated blood meals with roostmates who have failed to feed — and critically, this food-sharing occurs across kinship boundaries and without strict reciprocity. Bats donated food to unrelated individuals who had not previously shared with them, a pattern that cannot be explained by kin selection or tit-for-tat reciprocity alone. The bats who shared food were not losing the evolutionary game; their cooperative behavior was itself a fitness strategy, building social bonds that increased their own survival probability in a species where a single missed feeding night can be lethal. Cooperation, in evolutionary terms, is not the opposite of competition — it is frequently the mechanism by which organisms compete most effectively.

**Darwin's definition of adaptation is not what Adams thinks it is.** Adams' second claim — that successful organisms are "highly adaptive" — commits a fundamental confusion between phenotypic plasticity and genetic adaptation. Darwin himself defined adaptation as a genetically determined, heritable trait that confers a fitness advantage in a particular environment. An adaptation is not behavioral flexibility or emotional resilience; it is a specific morphological, physiological, or behavioral feature encoded in the genome and shaped by selection over generations. When Adams claims that millennials who need "safe spaces" are failing to "adapt," he is using the word in its colloquial sense — adjusting to circumstances — while pretending to invoke its technical evolutionary meaning. These are entirely different concepts. A polar bear's dense fur is an adaptation. A college student seeking counseling after a distressing political event is engaging in phenotypic plasticity — a flexible behavioral response to environmental conditions — which is itself, ironically, one of the hallmarks of a highly evolved cognitive system. Adams' confusion of these categories is not a minor error; it is the foundational mistake upon which his entire argument collapses.

**Patriarchal dominance is not a biological universal.** Adams' third claim — that "strong males" producing successful offspring through patriarchal social structures is a universal law of nature — is empirically false. Female dominance has been documented across numerous mammalian species. Clutton-Brock and Huchard (2013, *Animal Behaviour*) and earlier work by Clutton-Brock and colleagues (2008, *Animal Behaviour*) identified female dominance in spotted hyenas (*Crocuta crocuta*), hyraxes, velvet-furred swamp rats, brush-tailed possums, and nutrias, among others. In spotted hyenas, females are larger than males, socially dominant, and control access to food and mating opportunities — a complete inversion of Adams' supposed universal law. The diversity of mammalian social structures — from the matrilineal hierarchies of elephants and orcas to the cooperative breeding of meerkats and naked mole-rats — demonstrates that no single template of male dominance governs the natural world. Adams selected one pattern, ignored the others, and declared his selective sample a universal law.

**A "natural selection event" is a fabrication.** Adams structured his argument around the idea of a coming "natural selection event" — some catastrophic occurrence that would winnow the gene pool and eliminate the unfit. This is not a recognized concept in evolutionary biology. Darwin described natural selection as the process by which organisms with heritable traits better suited to their environment leave more offspring, and he emphasized that this process is not catastrophic but continuous — "daily and hourly scrutinising, throughout the world, every variation, even the slightest" (*On the Origin of Species*, 1859). Natural selection is not an event. It is an ongoing, diffuse, multigenerational process. The fantasy of a single apocalyptic test that separates the biologically worthy from the unworthy has more in common with millenarian religious prophecy than with evolutionary biology.

**The heritability requirement demolishes the argument entirely.** For natural selection to act on any trait, that trait must be heritable — it must be transmitted from parent to offspring through genetic mechanisms. Political orientation, emotional sensitivity, and attitudes toward competition are complex behavioral phenotypes shaped by vast networks of genes, epigenetic factors, developmental environments, cultural transmission, education, and individual experience. Even if liberal political attitudes were somehow disadvantageous (a claim for which Adams provides no evidence), natural selection could only eliminate them if they were primarily genetically determined and transmitted. They are not, at least not in the straightforward way Adams requires. His argument demands that "being liberal" functions like a Mendelian trait subject to simple selective pressure. The actual biology of political behavior is incomparably more complex.

## 3. Why People Believe This

The appeal of Adams' argument is not scientific but psychological, cultural, and historical. Understanding why such claims spread requires examining the deep history of evolutionary rhetoric in political discourse, the genuine (but limited) science of political orientation heritability, and the cognitive biases that make naturalistic arguments viscerally persuasive.

### Social Darwinism: Spencer to Hitler

Adams' article is not original. It belongs to a tradition stretching back to Herbert Spencer, who coined the phrase "survival of the fittest" in 1864 — a phrase Darwin reluctantly adopted but that Spencer used to justify laissez-faire capitalism, colonialism, and the abandonment of social welfare programs. Spencer argued that aiding the poor and weak interfered with nature's winnowing process, and that civilization progressed only when the unfit were allowed to perish. This "Social Darwinism" — which Darwin himself never endorsed — was enthusiastically adopted by industrialists, racial theorists, and eventually by the architects of Nazi eugenic policy. The intellectual trajectory from Spencer's *Principles of Biology* (1864) to the Nuremberg Laws (1935) is not a slippery slope fallacy; it is a documented historical pathway in which evolutionary language was systematically weaponized to naturalize existing power hierarchies and to argue that social inequality reflects biological inevitability.

Adams' article operates in exactly this tradition. By claiming that liberal values are biologically dysfunctional, he naturalizes conservative values as the only viable template and transforms political opponents into evolutionary dead ends. The rhetorical move is ancient: declare your preferred social arrangement "natural," declare alternatives "unnatural," and invoke the authority of science to settle what is actually a political dispute.

### The Naturalistic Fallacy

The philosophical error at the heart of Adams' argument is the naturalistic fallacy — the inference from "is" to "ought." Even if competition, rigid adaptation requirements, and male dominance were universal features of the natural world (they are not, as demonstrated above), it would not follow that human societies *should* be organized along these lines. Cancer is natural. Infanticide occurs in numerous species. Parasitic wasps lay eggs inside living caterpillars. The natural world is not a moral template. David Hume identified this error in 1739 (*A Treatise of Human Nature*); G.E. Moore named it in 1903 (*Principia Ethica*). Adams commits it with abandon, treating observed (and frequently misreported) patterns in nature as prescriptions for human social organization.

### Phenotypic Plasticity vs. Genetic Adaptation: The Core Confusion

Adams' argument depends on a category error so fundamental that it would fail a first-year biology exam. He treats behavioral and cultural traits — political preferences, emotional responses, attitudes toward authority — as though they were fixed genetic phenotypes subject to simple selective pressure. In reality, behavioral flexibility in humans is itself a product of natural selection. Our species' extraordinary phenotypic plasticity — our ability to modify behavior, adopt new cultural practices, and adjust to radically different social environments — is arguably *the* defining adaptation of *Homo sapiens*. The capacity to create "safe spaces," organize protests, develop counseling infrastructure, and process collective grief is not a sign of evolutionary weakness. It is a manifestation of the cooperative, culturally transmitted behavioral repertoire that allowed humans to colonize every terrestrial biome on the planet.

### What Twin Studies Actually Show

There is genuine science on the heritability of political orientation, but it says far less than partisans on either side wish. Alford, Funk, and Hibbing (2005, *American Political Science Review*) analyzed data from twin registries and found that additive genetic factors accounted for roughly 40–60% of the variance in political attitudes on a liberal-conservative spectrum, with shared environment contributing relatively little and non-shared environment accounting for the remainder. Bouchard and McGue (2003, *Science*) similarly found moderate heritability for a range of personality traits and social attitudes.

However — and this is the critical caveat that Adams and his readers systematically ignore — heritability estimates describe the proportion of *variation* in a trait attributable to genetic differences within a specific population at a specific time. They do not tell us that "liberalism is genetic" in any deterministic sense. They do not identify specific genes. They do not predict individual outcomes. And they emphatically do not tell us that liberal political attitudes reduce biological fitness. Heritability is a population-level statistical concept, not a diagnostic tool for identifying evolutionary losers. The existence of moderate heritability for political attitudes is scientifically interesting; it is also completely irrelevant to Adams' claim that liberals will be eliminated by natural selection.

### Why Evopsych Claims Go Viral on the Political Internet

Adams' article exploits a well-documented pattern in online information ecosystems. Evolutionary psychology claims — especially those that appear to naturalize existing social hierarchies around gender, race, or political orientation — spread with extraordinary velocity on partisan media platforms. This is because they satisfy simultaneously the desire for scientific authority and the desire for ideological confirmation. A claim framed as "science proves conservatives are biologically superior" does not need to be scientifically valid to be persuasive; it needs only to *feel* authoritative while confirming pre-existing beliefs. The language of evolution — fitness, selection, adaptation, survival — carries enormous cultural prestige, and its misapplication is difficult for non-specialists to detect. Adams is not a scientist writing for a scientific audience. He is a political commentator borrowing scientific vocabulary to grant his political conclusions a veneer of inevitability.

## 4. Verdict

**Propositional status: False.**

The claim that liberal college millennials are less biologically fit than "real Americans" is false across every dimension in which it can be evaluated. Adams' three "universal traits" are not universal. His definition of adaptation confuses colloquial and technical meanings. His claim of patriarchal universality is contradicted by extensive comparative zoological evidence. His concept of a "natural selection event" has no basis in evolutionary biology. And the fundamental requirement for natural selection — that the trait under selection be heritable in a straightforward genetic sense — is not met by political orientation, which is a complex phenotype shaped by genes, environment, culture, and experience in ways that defy the simple selective model Adams proposes.

Multiple category errors are identifiable: conflation of phenotypic plasticity with genetic adaptation, confusion of behavioral flexibility with biological weakness, equation of normative political preferences with selectable biological traits, and the naturalistic fallacy itself — the illegitimate inference from perceived natural patterns to prescriptions for human social organization.

**Confidence: High.** The relevant evolutionary biology, behavioral ecology, and population genetics are well established and not subject to significant scholarly dispute. The errors in Adams' argument are not borderline or debatable; they are foundational and disqualifying.

## 5. The Wider Picture

Adams' article is a single specimen of a much larger phenomenon: the political weaponization of evolutionary rhetoric. Understanding the wider context requires engaging with what evolutionary anthropology actually says about cooperation, competition, and fitness — and with the historical pattern of misuse that connects Social Darwinism to contemporary online pseudoscience.

### Cooperative Fitness and Multilevel Selection

Modern evolutionary theory has moved far beyond the "nature red in tooth and claw" caricature that Adams relies upon. Multilevel selection theory, as articulated by Wilson and Wilson (2007, *Quarterly Review of Biology*), provides a rigorous framework for understanding how natural selection can operate at multiple levels simultaneously — on individuals within groups, but also on groups competing with other groups. In this framework, traits that reduce individual fitness but increase group fitness (altruism, cooperation, resource-sharing) can be favored by selection when between-group competition is strong enough to offset within-group costs. Human societies, with their elaborate systems of norms, punishments, and cooperative institutions, are the most dramatic example of multilevel selection in the natural world. The capacity for large-scale cooperation — including the kind of collective emotional processing that Adams mocks as "snowflake" behavior — is not a deviation from evolutionary success. It is arguably the *mechanism* of human evolutionary success.

### WEIRD Bias in Evolutionary Psychology

A persistent criticism of evolutionary psychology is its reliance on data from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) societies, a bias documented by Henrich, Heine, and Norenzayan (2010, *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*). Claims about "universal" human nature — including claims about universal male dominance, universal competitive hierarchies, and universal patterns of mate selection — are frequently derived from studies of North American and European undergraduates and then projected onto all of humanity. When these claims are tested against the ethnographic and cross-cultural record, they frequently fail. Patriarchal dominance hierarchies are not universal across human cultures; matrilineal and matrilocal societies exist and have existed throughout human history. The "universals" that Adams invokes are often parochial features of the particular societies that evolutionary psychologists happen to study.

### What Makes a Trait Actually Heritable and Selectable

For Adams' argument to work, political orientation would need to be: (1) primarily genetically determined, (2) reliably transmitted from parent to offspring, (3) consistently associated with reduced reproductive success, and (4) subject to selection pressure strong enough to change allele frequencies over generational timescales. None of these conditions is met. Political orientation is moderately heritable (Alford et al., 2005, *American Political Science Review*), but moderate heritability in a complex behavioral trait does not imply genetic determinism. The genetic architecture of political attitudes likely involves thousands of genes of tiny individual effect, interacting with developmental environments, cultural contexts, and individual life experiences in ways that make simple selective models inapplicable. And crucially, there is no evidence that liberal political attitudes are associated with reduced reproductive output. If anything, the declining abortion rate since the Reagan era — a trend Snopes noted — suggests that mate selectivity among young women (a core component of sexual selection) has been *increasing*, not decreasing. Adams' evolutionary prophecy is not merely unsupported; it requires conditions that observational data actively contradict.

### The Historical Misuse of "Fitness"

The word "fitness" in evolutionary biology has a precise technical meaning: the relative reproductive success of a genotype in a given environment. It does not mean strength, health, intelligence, emotional resilience, or any of the colloquial connotations the word carries. When Adams declares that liberal millennials are less "fit," he is exploiting the gap between the technical and colloquial meanings — implying biological doom while actually describing traits he personally finds contemptible. This equivocation has a long and dark history. The eugenics movement of the early twentieth century — which led to forced sterilization programs in the United States, Canada, and Scandinavia and provided intellectual scaffolding for Nazi racial policy — was built on exactly this kind of conflation between biological fitness and social desirability. Declaring that certain people are "less fit" is never a neutral scientific observation when it is deployed in political rhetoric. It is an assertion of biological hierarchy with consequences that history has thoroughly documented.

### Differential Fitness Requires Generations, Not Events

A final conceptual error deserves emphasis. Biological fitness is measured in differential reproductive success across generations. It is not determined by survival of a single catastrophic event. Adams' vision of a "natural selection event" that eliminates liberals in one dramatic sweep has no analogue in evolutionary biology. Even genuine mass extinction events — the end-Permian, the end-Cretaceous — do not operate by testing individual organisms against a checklist of desirable traits. They alter selection pressures across entire ecological communities over geological timescales. The fantasy of a single test that separates the worthy from the unworthy is drawn from apocalyptic mythology, not from any understanding of how evolution actually works.

## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

Snopes' treatment of this claim, authored by Alex Kasprak, is competent and well-sourced. The article correctly identifies the core scientific errors in Adams' argument: the mischaracterization of competition as the sole driver of evolutionary success, the false claim of universal patriarchy in nature, the confusion between colloquial and technical definitions of adaptation, and the fabricated concept of a "natural selection event." Kasprak cites relevant primary literature — Carter and Wilkinson on vampire bat food-sharing, Clutton-Brock and colleagues on female dominance in mammals — and correctly notes that any trait subject to natural selection must be heritable.

### EARC Assessment: C (Challenge)

This entry receives an EARC mode of **C** because the appropriate response to Adams' article is not merely to correct individual factual errors but to **challenge the entire conceptual framework** within which those errors are situated. Adams' argument is not wrong because he got a few details incorrect about vampire bats or hyenas. It is wrong because the entire enterprise — using evolutionary biology to prophesy the extinction of political opponents — is built on category errors so fundamental that correcting them one-by-one is inadequate. The framework itself — the inference from biological patterns to political prescriptions, the conflation of behavioral traits with heritable genetic fitness, the apocalyptic model of selection as a single dramatic event — must be rejected wholesale. A fact-check that merely says "False, because vampire bats share food" has answered the question correctly but has not addressed the deeper problem: that the question itself rests on a conceptual architecture that is rotten from the foundations upward.

### Strengths and Limitations of the Snopes Approach

Snopes' strength is accessibility. Kasprak's article presents the scientific rebuttal in clear, readable prose and provides enough primary sources for a motivated reader to verify the claims independently. Its limitation is the structural constraint of the fact-checking format itself: a True/False verdict with supporting evidence. This format handles empirical claims well — did a politician say X? does the data support Y? — but it handles conceptual category errors less well. Adams' claim is not a single falsifiable proposition; it is a *framework* of interlocking assumptions, each of which is independently wrong and which collectively produce a conclusion that is not merely false but incoherent. A more comprehensive treatment would require:

1. Explicit identification of the naturalistic fallacy as the organizing logical error.
2. A clearer distinction between phenotypic plasticity and genetic adaptation, explaining why the entire premise of the argument depends on confusing them.
3. Engagement with the history of Social Darwinism to show that Adams' argument is not novel but belongs to a well-documented tradition of political misuse of evolutionary language.
4. A more thorough treatment of what population-genetic research on political orientation actually shows (and does not show).

Snopes answered the question correctly. The Truth Vault aims to illuminate why the question was malformed, why it was asked in the form it took, and why the rhetorical pattern it represents — evolutionary prophecy as political weapon — recurs with such regularity in moments of political upheaval. Adams published his article five days after an election. The timing tells you everything about the purpose: this was never about biology. It was about contempt, seeking the most authoritative language available to express it.
