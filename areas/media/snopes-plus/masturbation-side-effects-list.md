# Myths about masturbation side effects are spreading online. Here's what science actually says


## 1. The Claim

In the summer of 2025, a meme circulated extensively on X (formerly Twitter) and other platforms presenting an enumerated list of "side effects of frequent masturbation." The list, which accumulated over 9.7 million views on one X post alone (user @hood_grimes, August 2025), claimed that frequent masturbation leads to:

1. Low libido  
2. Back pain  
3. Reduced testosterone  
4. Penis shrinkage  
5. Deterioration of the immune system  
6. Prostate damage  
7. Kidney damage  
8. Brain damage  
9. Acne  
10. Vaginal dryness in women

The meme was framed as health information and circulated with variations across multiple accounts. One version included a final item — "If you're not following me we might not see each other again" — suggesting the original was intended as trolling that subsequently metastasized into sincere health content sharing, a common pattern in social media misinformation propagation.

The claims in this list are not new. They represent a recurrence of prohibitionist myths about masturbation that have roots in 18th and 19th-century medical and religious literature and that have been periodically updated in surface presentation without underlying evidential change. The specific "side effects" framing — borrowed from pharmaceutical drug information — is an adaptation to contemporary health information presentation norms.

Snopes investigated the claim in September 2025 (Anna Rascouët-Paz, *Snopes*, published September 7, 2025, https://www.snopes.com/news/2025/09/08/masturbation-side-effects-list/) in a research-backed piece that reviewed the relevant scientific literature across multiple dimensions. The Snopes article does not provide a binary verdict (the piece is categorized as "News" rather than "Fact Check"), but it comprehensively addresses each component of the list against peer-reviewed evidence. This entry extends that analysis with additional scientific depth and historical context.


## 2. What's Actually True

**Masturbation is not only safe but actively associated with several health benefits — and multiple claims on the list directly contradict the established research.**

**Prostate cancer risk: the opposite of the meme's claim.** The meme claims masturbation may lead to "prostate damage." Multiple studies find the opposite pattern for ejaculation frequency and prostate cancer risk. Leitzmann et al. (2004, "Ejaculation Frequency and Subsequent Risk of Prostate Cancer," *JAMA*, 291(13), 1578–1586) — a study of 29,342 men — found that high ejaculation frequency (21 or more ejaculations per month) was significantly associated with *decreased* risk of total prostate cancer compared to lower frequency. The relationship held across ejaculation sources including masturbation. This finding has been broadly replicated and is now incorporated into standard sexual health guidance from major medical institutions.

**Immune function: the opposite direction.** The meme claims masturbation "deteriorates the immune system." Haake et al. (2004, "Effects of Sexual Arousal on Lymphocyte Subset Circulation and Cytokine Production in Man," *Neuroimmunomodulation*, 11(5), 293–298) found that sexual arousal and orgasm increased the absolute number of leukocytes — particularly natural killer cells, which are a front-line component of the innate immune system — in male subjects. Natural killer cells have roles in combating both infection and tumor surveillance. The effect is short-term and context-specific, but it is in the opposite direction from the meme's claim.

**Vaginal dryness.** The meme claims masturbation "causes dryness in women." A 1995 study by Zeiss et al. (published in the *Journal of Sex Education and Therapy*, vol. 21(1)) and foundational research by Masters and Johnson (1966, *Human Sexual Response*, Little, Brown & Co.) established that genital stimulation and subsequent orgasm contribute to normal lubrication mechanisms and can help maintain vaginal tissue health. More concretely, masturbation involving genital stimulation increases blood flow to vaginal tissues and promotes lubrication — effects that are used therapeutically for vaginal atrophy and dyspareunia (painful intercourse) in clinical settings. The claim that masturbation causes vaginal dryness is the reverse of the documented physiological effects.

**Testosterone.** The meme claims masturbation "reduces testosterone." The relationship between ejaculation and testosterone is complex and studied, but the literature does not support a chronic testosterone-reducing effect of masturbation. Some research suggests very temporary post-ejaculation testosterone fluctuations, but no study supports the claim that regular masturbation produces lasting testosterone reduction. Studies on prolonged abstinence (e.g., Jiang et al., 2003, "A research on the relationship between ejaculation and serum testosterone level in men," *Journal of Zhejiang University (Medical Science)*, 32(3), 236–240) found modest temporary testosterone increases after about a week of abstinence, which some online communities have interpreted as evidence that masturbation lowers testosterone — but this misreads a transient recovery effect as evidence of chronic depletion. The baseline testosterone level of regularly sexually active men is not lower than that of abstinent men in any well-controlled study.

**Libido, back pain, penis shrinkage, brain damage, kidney damage.** None of these associations are supported by peer-reviewed evidence. Libido is primarily regulated by testosterone, dopamine, and psychological factors; safe masturbation at typical frequencies does not reduce any of these in established literature. Back pain is not associated with masturbation in any peer-reviewed study. "Penis shrinkage" has no physiological basis as a consequence of masturbation — penile anatomy is not affected by ejaculation frequency. "Brain damage" is not supported by any credible neuroscientific research; the neurochemical events associated with sexual arousal and orgasm include oxytocin release, dopamine reward activation, and endorphin production, none of which produce lasting structural damage. Kidney damage from masturbation is not described in any nephrology literature.

**Conditions where masturbation does have adverse effects.** The Snopes piece appropriately identifies genuine health risks, which are context-specific:
- Forceful or rough technique can cause physical injury — friction burns, micro-tears, edema — independent of frequency.
- Use of non-sterile objects or unclean hands increases risk of urinary tract infections and bacterial vaginosis in females.
- Compulsive masturbatory behavior that interferes with daily functioning, relationships, or professional life is a behavioral pattern that mental health professionals address, though the classification of "masturbation addiction" remains contested in clinical psychology (Prause et al., 2017, "Controlling for Potentially Confounding Variables in the Association of Pornography Use and Sexual Satisfaction," *Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy*, 43(6), 506–514).

The evidence overall strongly supports masturbation as a safe, beneficial component of normal sexual development and sexual health when practiced without physical injury or compulsive interference with life functioning.


## 3. Epistemic Novelty — What the Fact-Checks Miss

The Snopes piece provides an accurate and well-sourced review of the relevant science. What it does not fully engage with is the historical origin of these myths — which reveals that we are not dealing with new misinformation but with the digital reanimation of a multi-century prohibitionist discourse, and that understanding this lineage matters for understanding why the myths are so persistent.

**The historical origin of masturbation myths and the medical-religious complex.** The specific claims on the 2025 meme — depleted energy, damaged health, weakened constitution, cognitive impairment — are not inventions of the internet era. They derive in nearly identical form from 18th-century medical literature that was itself an attempt to scientify religious prohibitions against non-reproductive sexual activity.

The landmark text is Samuel Tissot's *L'Onanisme* (1760), which argued that masturbation caused a progressive loss of "vital fluids" leading to nervous exhaustion, blindness, mental deterioration, and physical weakness. Tissot drew on Galenic humoral medicine and the concept of "semen as vital substance" — a pre-scientific framework entirely without empirical support — to construct a medical pathology from what had previously been purely a religious prohibition. The book was enormously influential across Europe and North America because it provided a medical vocabulary for moralizing about sexuality that sounded like natural science. Tissot's claims bear remarkable similarity to the 2025 meme's list: depletion, weakness, constitutional damage, cognitive harm.

The 19th century produced an extensive anti-masturbation medical literature particularly in the United States, associated with figures including Sylvester Graham (inventor of the Graham cracker, designed as an anti-arousal food) and John Harvey Kellogg (inventor of corn flakes, similarly motivated). This literature described masturbation as producing almost every conceivable disease: epilepsy, insanity, tuberculosis, acne, stunted growth, spinal curvature. Many of these claims directly anticipate items on the 2025 meme's list.

The Tissot-Graham-Kellogg tradition was not fringe pseudoscience in its era — it appeared in mainstream medical journals and was taught in medical schools. Its claims were not tested against empirical evidence; they were derived deductively from the premise that non-reproductive sexual activity was self-evidently pathological, a premise inherited from Augustinian Christian theology and filtered through the Galenic vital-fluids framework. The "science" was rationalization of pre-existing moral prohibition, not investigation of physiological reality.

This history is directly relevant to the 2025 meme because the meme's claims are not empirical generalizations from observation — they are the same moral prohibitions in updated vocabulary. "Brain damage" is Tissot's "nervous exhaustion" repackaged for an era that treats neuroscience as authoritative. "Reduced testosterone" is the "vital fluids depletion" claim refactored into endocrinological language. "Prostate damage" inverts an actual empirical finding (masturbation reduces prostate cancer risk) by substituting the expected moral narrative (masturbation damages the body) for the research-based one.

**The "No Fap" and sexual abstinence movement as contemporary transmission vector.** The 2025 meme is in large part a product of the "No Fap" movement, which began as a Reddit subgroup (r/NoFap) in 2011 and has grown into a substantial online community promoting total masturbation abstinence on claims of physiological and psychological benefit. The No Fap movement has generated its own body of anecdotal "superpowers" testimonials — users claim improved focus, confidence, increased testosterone, better social functioning — and has functioned as a transmission vector for directly mythologized anti-masturbation claims rephrased in medical-seeming language. Several items on the 2025 meme's list are recognizable from No Fap community discourse.

The No Fap movement draws selectively on peer-reviewed research while misrepresenting or ignoring contrary findings. The Jiang et al. (2003) study on temporary testosterone elevation after abstinence, already cited above, is widely cited in No Fap discourse as proof that masturbation reduces testosterone — a misreading that converts a transient return-from-baseline observation into a claim about chronic hormonal depletion. Research on pornography-induced sexual dysfunction (Prause et al., 2017, cited above) is selectively cited to support claims about masturbation harms without distinguishing compulsive pornography use from typical masturbation.

The intersection of No Fap ideology with manosphere (men's rights and red-pill Internet subcultures) discourse has amplified the reach of anti-masturbation claims and given them a new framing: masturbation as a form of emasculation, testosterone depletion, and subordination. This framing adds an ideological dimension to the health claims that makes them more resistant to correction because they are embedded in identity-level beliefs about masculinity, self-discipline, and social power.

**The cross-cultural persistence of semen-conservation beliefs.** The specific fear of semen loss as a depleting, weakening, or dangerous event appears in cultures worldwide, independently of the Western Tissot tradition. In Chinese traditional medicine, the concept of *jing* (精, "essence") attributes vital energy to semen and prescribes conservation for health and longevity. In Indian Ayurvedic tradition, *brahmacharya* (sexual abstinence) is associated with the conservation of *ojas*, a vital bodily essence. Anthropological study of semen-loss concerns has identified similar beliefs in South Asian, Southeast Asian, and African cultural contexts, all predating Western medical contact (Bottero, 1991, "Consumption by Semen Loss in India and Elsewhere," *Culture, Medicine and Psychiatry*, 15(3), 303–320).

This cross-cultural convergence suggests that semen-loss anxiety may be related to psychological mechanisms more fundamental than any particular cultural tradition — possibly connected to the observable salience of ejaculation as a bodily event and the general human cognitive tendency toward loss-aversion and conservation narratives around valued biological resources. This does not validate the physiological claims; it explains why psychologically plausible but empirically false beliefs about semen depletion persist so broadly. The 2025 meme is the internet-era iteration of a belief structure that may have deep cognitive-emotional roots independent of any particular cultural transmission.

**The missing science on masturbation's actual mechanisms.** While the Snopes piece and this entry document the absence of evidence for the meme's claims, a genuinely novel finding from recent research is that masturbation appears to have evolutionary significance in male primates specifically as a sexually transmitted infection (STI) avoidance mechanism. Brindle et al. (2023, "The Evolution of Masturbation Is Associated with Postcopulatory Selection and Pathogen Avoidance in Primates," *Proceedings of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences*, 290(2000)) analyzed the distribution of masturbatory behavior across 246 primate species and found that masturbation was significantly more prevalent in primate species with high rates of multiple mating — consistent with the hypothesis that pre-copulatory ejaculation reduces pathogen load in the reproductive tract before mating with a new partner. This finding, published in 2023, represents one of the strongest evolutionary accounts for masturbation to date and directly reframes it not as a depletion behavior but as a pathogen-management behavior that may have been adaptive across primate evolutionary history.

This is the genuinely epistemically novel dimension that most popular coverage — including the Snopes piece — does not include: masturbation in males likely has evolutionary *adaptive* function in the pathogens-avoidance framework, suggesting that the behavior is maintained by selection rather than being a pathological departure from some norm of abstinence.


## 4. Verdict

**Propositional Status:** False (for all strongly stated claims on the meme list)  
**Confidence:** Very high  
**Epistemic Basis:** Multiple peer-reviewed studies directly contradicting or finding no support for each claim; evolutionary biology providing a positive adaptive framework for the behavior  
**Phenomenological Status:** N/A  
**Mechanistic Status:** Benefits mechanistically traced to orgasm-associated immune activation, prostate flushing, lubrication maintenance; harm claims lack proposed mechanisms and are absent from clinical literature  
**Folk Wisdom Value:** Negative — list encodes pre-scientific religious prohibitions repackaged as medical information with several claims directly inverted from research findings

The claims on the 2025 masturbation side effects meme are false. Multiple items are directly contradicted by peer-reviewed research. The scientific evidence indicates that masturbation at typical frequencies contributes positively to sexual health, immune function, prostate health in males, and lubrication in females. Genuine risks exist in pathological contexts (compulsive behavior, physical injury technique, poor hygiene) but do not include any of the conditions claimed in the meme. The meme is the digital iteration of a multi-century prohibitionist medical discourse that was fabricated as scientific rationalization of religious prohibition and has no empirical foundation.


## 5. The Wider Picture

**Masturbation in evolutionary context.** The Brindle et al. (2023) study is the most significant recent evolution of evidence on masturbation's biological function. The finding that masturbatory behavior is phylogenetically associated with species-level, multi-mating environments — rather than simply a behavioral byproduct with no adaptive significance — represents a conceptual shift. It moves masturbation from the category of "neutral behavior people do" to "maintained behavior with positively selected function," which has implications for how medical and public health communities should frame the behavior. If masturbation evolved as a pathogen-clearing mechanism, the argument for its health value is not merely "absence of documented harm" but "presence of positive adaptive design."

**The animal distribution of masturbatory behavior.** Roth et al. (2022, "Masturbation in the Animal Kingdom," *The Journal of Sex Research*, 60(6), 1–13) conducted a systematic review of masturbatory behavior across non-human animals and confirmed widespread distribution across mammalian and non-mammalian species, including birds, reptiles, and invertebrates. This comparative distribution is inconsistent with masturbation being an aberrant pathological behavior introduced by cultural corruption, sedentary lifestyles, or pornography access — it is a behavior conserved across hundreds of millions of years of animal evolution in taxa without cultural transmission.

**Masturbation and sexual development.** Kaestle & Allen (2011, "The Role of Masturbation in Healthy Sexual Development: Perceptions of Young Adults," *Archives of Sexual Behavior*, 40(5), 781–792) found that masturbation during adolescence was positively associated with adolescent sexual development outcomes and that young adults retrospectively perceived it as having contributed positively to their developing understanding of their own sexuality. The perception of masturbation as developmentally normative and beneficial is consistent with the physiological evidence and contradicts narratives — common in both religious education and the anti-masturbation meme tradition — of masturbation as developmentally damaging.

**Implications for public health communication.** The persistence of false masturbation claims on social media has documented public health consequences. Adolescents and young adults exposed to claims that masturbation causes brain damage, testosterone reduction, or acne may experience unnecessary shame and anxiety, may avoid discussing sexual health with healthcare providers, and may seek out pseudoscientific interventions based on misinformation. A 2019 systematic review (Laqueur, T.W., 2003, *Solitary Sex: A Cultural History of Masturbation*, Zone Books) documented the psychological harms associated with masturbation shame across the 18th and 19th centuries — harm that was produced not by the behavior but by the cultural prohibition of it.

**Gender differential in the meme's framing.** The meme's list is largely androcentric — most items (testosterone, penis shrinkage, prostate damage) are framed in male anatomical terms, with "vaginal dryness in women" as a brief addendum. This reflects the broader pattern of anti-masturbation discourse, which is primarily directed at male sexuality and male bodies. The specific concern with testosterone depletion and penile anatomy reflects the manosphere-influenced framing of masturbation as emasculation, which is a cultural-ideological concern rather than a physiological one. Female masturbation is underrepresented in both the mythology and, historically, in the research literature — the 1995 evidence that genital stimulation relieves dysmenorrhea has been followed by only limited additional research on the specifically female health dimensions of masturbation, representing one of many gaps in sexual health research that reflects historical male-centered research prioritization (Shifren et al., 2008, "Sexual Problems and Distress in United States Women: Prevalence and Correlates," *Obstetrics & Gynecology*, 112(5), 970–978).


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

The Snopes piece (Rascouët-Paz, 2025) is notable for being a well-sourced intervention in a space where fact-checkers typically do not operate — sexual health — and for treating the topic with scientific directness and without euphemism. The article covers the major research findings, identifies legitimate concerns (compulsive behavior, physical injury, hygiene), situates the myths in the context of cultural stigma and religious prohibitions, and reaches a clear evidence-based conclusion that the meme's claims are false and that masturbation is broadly health-positive.

The piece does not engage with the historical origin of the myths (the Tissot-to-No Fap lineage), the evolutionary adaptive significance findings from Brindle et al. (2023), or the cross-cultural comparative literature on semen-loss beliefs — all of which are relevant to understanding why these myths are so culturally persistent and how they should be addressed. Understanding origin as well as refutation is necessary for effective public health communication: telling people that the meme is false, without explaining why the meme keeps coming back, leaves audiences without the tools to recognize the next iteration.

The critical gap in most fact-check coverage of this topic is the failure to distinguish between "masturbation causes harm" (empirically false) and "compulsive or harmful masturbation practices cause harm" (empirically true and clinically relevant). By conflating the two, misinformation about masturbation creates confusion that may lead some people to dismiss legitimate clinical concerns about compulsive behavior as equally mythological — an overcorrection that the nuanced evidence does not support. The therapeutic framing of masturbation addiction as a genuine clinical phenomenon (for a minority of individuals) does not rehabilitate the meme's claims, which are about ordinary masturbation, not compulsive or pathological variants.
