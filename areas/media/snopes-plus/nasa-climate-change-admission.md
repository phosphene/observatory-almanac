# NASA admitted that climate change is caused by Earth's orbital changes, not humans


## 1. The Claim

A claim that has circulated with extraordinary persistence across climate-sceptic websites, social media posts, and email forwarding chains since approximately 2016 asserts that NASA itself has officially documented and in some versions "admitted" that climate change is driven by Earth's orbital and axial variations, not by human greenhouse gas emissions, and that this finding has been suppressed, ignored, or buried by the mainstream scientific establishment and government bodies with an interest in maintaining the climate consensus.

The claim typically links to or paraphrases a NASA educational content page, hosted at one point on the Climate.NASA.gov website, that describes the Milankovitch cycles — periodic variations in Earth's eccentricity, axial tilt, and the precession of the equinoxes — and explains their role in driving the glacial-interglacial cycles of the past several hundred thousand years. Some versions of the claim circulate a screenshot of text from a NASA page alongside headlines such as "NASA Confirms Earth Is Not Warming Due to Man-Made Activities," "NASA Admits Orbital Changes Drive Climate Cycles," or "The Truth NASA Doesn't Want You to Know About Climate Change."

The more sophisticated versions of the claim attempt to construct a logical sequence: NASA's own documentation confirms that the most dramatic climate changes in Earth's history — the ice ages — were driven not by CO2 or human activity but by astronomical factors entirely outside human control; therefore, if powerful natural forces can drive major climate change, the current warming period must be natural rather than anthropogenic; and if NASA has documented this in its own educational materials, then its simultaneous attribution of current warming to human activity represents either a logical contradiction or deliberate institutional dishonesty.

This claim has been shared millions of times across platforms, appears regularly in comment sections on climate news articles, and has been cited in the public statements of elected officials in multiple countries as evidence against climate legislation.


## 2. What's Actually True

### The NASA page is real and accurately describes a real science

NASA has indeed published educational content describing the Milankovitch cycles and their role in Earth's palaeoclimate. This is entirely non-controversial, well-established astronomical physics. Milutin Milankovitch, a Serbian mathematician and astronomer, developed the mathematical framework for calculating how periodic variations in three components of Earth's orbital geometry — the eccentricity of Earth's orbit around the Sun (cycles of approximately 100,000 years), the obliquity or tilt of Earth's rotational axis relative to its orbital plane (cycles of approximately 41,000 years), and the precession of the equinoxes (cycles of approximately 23,000 years) — alter the seasonal and geographic distribution of solar radiation received at Earth's surface. Milankovitch (1941, *Kanon der Erdbestrahlung und seine Anwendung auf das Eiszeitenproblem*, Royal Serbian Academy) published the foundational mathematical treatment.

The empirical validation of Milankovitch's theory came decisively from deep-sea sediment cores in the 1970s. Hays, Imbrie & Shackleton (1976, *Science*, 194(4270), pp. 1121–1132) — now one of the most cited papers in Earth science — analysed the oxygen isotope and foraminiferal assemblage records in two Southern Ocean sediment cores, found periodic signals at the 100,000-year, 41,000-year, and 23,000-year frequencies predicted by Milankovitch theory, and concluded that these orbital cycles were "pacemakers of the ice ages." This finding is accepted across virtually all of climate science and has been corroborated by dozens of independent palaeoclimate studies from ice cores, speleothems, and lake sediments. The NASA educational content describing this is accurate, well-sourced scientific communication.

### The claim's misrepresentation

The misrepresentation is in the implication drawn from this real science. NASA's documentation of Milankovitch cycles as the driver of glacial-interglacial cycles over the past 800,000 years is not a statement about current warming. It is a statement about palaeoclimate on timescales of tens of thousands of years. Using it as a "NASA admission" about present-day warming involves a fundamental category error: it applies a mechanism documented to operate on orbital timescales of 10,000 to 100,000 years to events unfolding over decades. Orbital parameters do not change on decadal timescales. They cannot explain climate change events that have unfolded in 50–100 years.

Berger (1978, *Journal of the Atmospheric Sciences*, 35(12), pp. 2362–2367) calculated the current state of all three Milankovitch parameters in precise detail. Earth is currently in a period of relatively low eccentricity, and based on the orbital geometry we are actually in a phase where Milankovitch forcing predicts gradual cooling from its optimum, not warming. Berger & Loutre (2002, *Science*, 297(5585), pp. 1287–1288) published projections extending 100,000 years forward and found that current CO2 concentrations are so elevated relative to natural background that they are likely to suppress the next glacial cycle that would otherwise be predicted by the orbital geometry. Using the Milankovitch mechanism to explain current warming requires it to drive warming at a rate and in a direction contrary to what that same mechanism mathematically predicts for the current and near-future orbital configuration. The claim is not merely unsupported — it contradicts the mechanism it invokes.

### The observable fingerprint of orbital versus greenhouse forcing

Climate science depends not only on explaining the total magnitude of temperature change but on matching the physical patterns — the "fingerprint" — of warming to candidate forcing mechanisms. Different causes of warming produce distinguishably different spatial and temporal signatures.

Orbital forcing is fundamentally asymmetric between hemispheres because the distribution of solar radiation by latitude changes when axial tilt varies and the timing of seasons changes when precession shifts. Glacial-interglacial transitions show pronounced inter-hemispheric asynchrony consistent with this orbital geometry. The current warming pattern is the opposite: both hemispheres are warming, the Arctic is warming 2–4 times faster than the global average (Arctic amplification), and the stratosphere is simultaneously cooling while the troposphere warms — a pattern that is the precise thermodynamic signature of an enhanced greenhouse effect and is inconsistent with increased solar or orbital forcing, which would warm the stratosphere and troposphere together.

Santer, Taylor, Wigley, Johns, Jones, Karoly, Mitchell, Oort, Penner, Ramaswamy, Schwarzkopf, Stouffer & Tett (1996, *Nature*, 382, pp. 39–46) published one of the foundational "fingerprinting" analyses demonstrating that the observed spatial pattern of late twentieth century warming matched climate model predictions for greenhouse forcing and did not match predictions for solar or orbital forcing. This fingerprinting approach has been refined and replicated many times since.

Fu, Johanson, Wallace & Reichler (2004, *Science*, 303(5656), pp. 350–353) examined tropical tropospheric temperature trends and found amplification consistent with greenhouse forcing predictions, distinguishable from solar forcing predictions. The stratospheric cooling trend has been replicated across independent datasets and is now regarded as a definitive fingerprint of greenhouse-gas-driven warming rather than solar or orbital changes.

### NASA's actual institutional position

The claim that NASA has "admitted" orbital cycles are responsible for current warming is additionally refuted by simply reading what NASA says about current warming. NASA's Goddard Institute for Space Studies (GISS) maintains the GISS Surface Temperature Analysis (GISTEMP), one of the four primary global surface temperature datasets. NASA's Earth observing satellite fleet — including GRACE, CloudSat, CALIPSO, ICESat-2, and the Landsat series — provides continuous global observations of ice sheet mass loss, sea level rise, ocean heat content, and surface temperature. NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory contribute to measurements of ocean heat uptake and sea level change.

Hansen, Ruedy, Sato & Lo (2010, *Reviews of Geophysics*, 48(4), RG4004) documented the long-term global surface temperature record and attributed the warming explicitly to greenhouse gas forcing. Velicogna & Wahr (2006, *Nature*, 443, pp. 329–331) used GRACE satellite gravity data to measure ice sheet mass loss, finding Greenland losing approximately 248 Gt/year in 2006 — a finding fully consistent with greenhouse-driven polar warming and inconsistent with orbital-cycle timescales.

NASA maintains publicly accessible explanations of attribution science at climate.nasa.gov stating directly that "the current warming trend is of particular significance because it is unequivocally the result of human activity since the mid-20th century." No ambiguity, no buried admission — the institutional position is explicit, continuously updated, and based on the agency's own measurements.


## 3. Why People Believe This

**The appeal of citing authority against itself.** The specific rhetorical structure of this claim — using NASA's own materials to "refute" the climate consensus, rather than citing unknown contrarian sources — makes it unusually persuasive to audiences who are generally inclined to respect scientific-institutional authority. If it is not a disreputable alternative-media source making the claim but NASA itself, the argument gains the credibility of scientific legitimacy. This is a well-documented rhetorical strategy in science denial: citing established institutions in ways that misrepresent their actual positions to suggest the institution itself has doubts it is not communicating directly.

Schmitt & Ioannidis (2021, *Misinformation Review*, 2(3)) analysed the information-content structure of viral science misrepresentation and found that posts incorporating genuine scientific sources — even when those sources were selectively quoted or contextually stripped — achieved higher credibility ratings and wider sharing than posts citing disreputable sources making equivalent claims. The NASA-framing of the Milankovitch argument achieves exactly this credibility borrowing.

**Genuine complexity in palaeoclimate science.** CO2-temperature relationships in the palaeoclimate record are genuinely complex, as discussed in the companion Truth Vault entry on ice-core lag arguments. Audiences who have encountered scientific debates about the relative roles of orbital forcing and greenhouse gas feedbacks in palaeoclimate are correctly aware that the science involves real complexity. The claim exploits this accurate awareness to suggest that the complexity extends to current warming attribution when it does not, because the causal structure of current anthropogenic forcing differs categorically from the orbital-plus-feedback dynamics of ice-age cycles.

**Scale mismatch as a cognitive challenge.** Human intuition is poorly calibrated for reasoning about processes operating over tens of thousands of years. The temporal gap between orbital timescales — cycles of 23,000, 41,000, and 100,000 years — and the human perception of "recent climate history" is so vast that many people cannot intuitively grasp why a mechanism documented for ancient ice ages would not also operate on the decades-long timescale of observed warming. The claim exploits this difficulty by simply not explaining the timescale conditions that make orbital forcing irrelevant to decadal temperature change.

Sterman & Booth Sweeney (2007, *Climatic Change*, 80(1–2), pp. 213–238) studied public mental models of climate change dynamics and found systematic difficulty with concepts involving rate-of-change and the gap between emissions and their temperature effects. This revealed difficulty in reasoning about climate dynamics on multiple timescales provides the cognitive conditions in which the Milankovitch timescale confusion thrives.


## 4. Verdict

**False.** The claim that NASA has admitted that climate change is caused by Earth's orbital cycles rather than by human activity is false on every component.

NASA has not issued any such admission. The NASA educational content describing Milankovitch cycles is a description of palaeoclimate science explaining glacial-interglacial cycles over hundreds of thousands of years, not a statement about the drivers of current warming. NASA's actual institutional position, supported by its own measurements from surface temperature records, satellite observations, and ice sheet mass balance, is that current warming is unequivocally attributable to human greenhouse gas emissions.

Milankovitch cycles cannot explain current warming because: (a) they operate on timescales four to five orders of magnitude longer than the decades over which current warming has been observed; (b) the current orbital configuration predicts gradual cooling, not the observed warming; (c) the spatial fingerprint of current warming — simultaneous stratospheric cooling and tropospheric warming, Arctic amplification, symmetric heating in both hemispheres — matches greenhouse forcing and contradicts the asymmetric, slow patterns expected from orbital forcing; and (d) atmospheric CO2 concentrations are being increased by human emissions at more than 100 times the rate of any natural geological variation, providing a direct physical mechanism for warming that requires no appeal to orbital dynamics.


## 5. The Wider Picture

The "NASA-admitted-Milankovitch" claim exemplifies a specific tactic in science denial that might be called authority inversion — appropriating the credibility of a scientific institution by misrepresenting documents produced by that institution. The tactic is particularly potent because it is essentially unfalsifiable through citation-checking alone: the cited document is real, the science it describes is legitimate, and the misrepresentation resides entirely in the contextual shift from what the document says about the past to what the claim implies about the present.

Lewandowsky, Ecker, Seifert, Schwarz & Cook (2012, *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 13(3), pp. 106–131) categorised authority inversion as a misinformation technique in their comprehensive review of myth debunking research. They found that corrections of misrepresented-authority claims are particularly difficult to achieve via simple factual rebuttal because audiences need to be equipped not just with the correct fact but with an explanation of the misrepresentation technique itself — a more cognitively demanding task. Knowing that a source has been misrepresented requires holding in mind both what the source actually says and what the misrepresentation claims it says.

The claim has also functioned as a mobilising argument in policy contexts. Documented examples of elected representatives in the United States, Australia, and Canada citing variants of the "orbital forcing" argument in debates over climate legislation illustrate that viral misinformation with plausible scientific-sounding framing can migrate from social media into formal deliberative processes. Boykoff & Boykoff (2004, *Global Environmental Change*, 14(2), pp. 125–136) documented earlier phases of this process, in which journalistic balance norms that treated sceptical and consensus positions as equally weighted produced distorted public perceptions of the actual state of scientific agreement. The Milankovitch claim represents a more technically sophisticated evolution of the same public confusion production.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

The specific claim regarding NASA and Milankovitch cycles has been addressed by fact-checking organisations multiple times in response to viral circulation events, particularly in 2016, 2019, and 2022, which represented major peaks in the claim's spread.

Climate Feedback assembled NASA climate scientists and palaeoclimatologists to directly review articles and social media posts making this claim, producing verdicts of "inaccurate" with detailed explanations of both the misrepresentation of NASA's position and the timescale mismatch that makes Milankovitch cycles irrelevant to current warming attribution. NASA itself issued clarifying statements on its climate.nasa.gov platform in response to high-circulation versions of the claim, explicitly stating that orbital cycles are not drivers of current warming and directing readers to its attribution research.

PolitiFact and Lead Stories have addressed the claim in response to viral social media posts, finding "false" in each case. Their analyses consistently identify the same two errors: the misrepresentation of what NASA's educational content discusses (past ice ages, not current warming), and the physical impossibility of orbital cycles driving temperature change on decadal timescales. The fact-checking record on this claim is unusually consistent, with no reputable fact-checking organisation finding the claim credible in any version, reflecting the severity of the logical errors the claim embeds rather than a contested empirical question.

For science educators, the claim is a useful teaching case for the logical fallacy of composition — assuming that because orbits drove major past climate changes, orbits must drive all climate changes — and for the critical reading skill of checking not merely whether a cited source is real but whether the claim accurately represents what that source says. The full NASA climate.nasa.gov attribution page, accessible without paywall, directly addresses each component of the claim.
