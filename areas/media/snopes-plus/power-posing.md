# "Power Posing Changes Your Hormones and Makes You More Confident"


## 1. The Claim

The power posing claim asserts that holding expansive body postures—such as standing with feet apart and hands on hips (the "Wonder Woman" pose) or raising arms in victory—for two minutes produces measurable hormonal changes and enhanced psychological states. The specific claims include:

**Hormonal Effects:**
- **Testosterone increase:** 20% average elevation after two minutes of high-power posing
- **Cortisol decrease:** 25% reduction in stress hormone levels compared to baseline
- **Sustained duration:** Hormonal changes lasting 15-20 minutes after posing ends
- **Dose-response relationship:** More expansive poses producing larger hormonal effects

**Psychological and Behavioral Effects:** 
- **Enhanced confidence:** Increased self-reported feelings of power and dominance
- **Risk tolerance:** Greater willingness to take chances in gambling and decision-making tasks
- **Performance improvement:** Better outcomes in job interviews, presentations, and competitive situations
- **Stress reduction:** Decreased anxiety and improved stress management in challenging situations

**Mechanistic Claims:**
The theory proposes that expansive postures trigger evolutionary dominance signals, activating the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis and testosterone production pathways. This represents an application of **embodied cognition theory**—the idea that physical states directly influence psychological states through bidirectional feedback mechanisms.

**Cultural Penetration and Applications:**
This claim gained extraordinary cultural influence through Amy Cuddy's 2012 TED talk "Your Body Language May Shape Who You Are," which became the second most-viewed TED presentation in history with over 70 million views. The content was subsequently:
- **Integrated into corporate training programs** across Fortune 500 companies
- **Incorporated into business school curricula** at Harvard, Wharton, and other elite institutions 
- **Adopted by athletic coaches** for pre-competition psychological preparation
- **Promoted in self-help and personal development contexts** as accessible confidence-building technique
- **Implemented in educational settings** to help students with test anxiety and presentation skills

The appeal lay in its simplicity and empowerment message—anyone could access increased confidence and hormonal benefits through brief, private physical practices requiring no special training, equipment, or ongoing commitment.


## 2. What's Actually True

The power posing claims demonstrate a complex trajectory from initial apparent scientific support through systematic replication failures, revealing multiple layers of methodological and interpretive problems:

**Original Research Foundation:**
Carney, Cuddy, and Yap's 2010 study in *Psychological Science* reported that participants who held high-power poses for two minutes showed:
- **Testosterone increases:** 20% average elevation (8.8% increase in high-power vs. 10.7% decrease in low-power conditions)
- **Cortisol decreases:** 25% reduction (15.4% decrease in high-power vs. 17.9% increase in low-power conditions)
- **Risk tolerance enhancement:** 86% of high-power posers chose risky gamble vs. 60% of low-power posers
- **Self-reported power feelings:** Significant increases on validated psychological scales

The study appeared methodologically sound, using a between-subjects design with 42 participants, validated hormone assays, and established psychological measures.

**Theoretical Framework Grounding:**
The research emerged from established embodied cognition literature demonstrating that physical states can influence psychological states through various mechanisms:
- **Facial feedback effects:** Smiling improving mood (Strack, Martin, & Stepper, 1988)
- **Postural influences:** Upright vs. slouched posture affecting confidence (Briñol et al., 2009)
- **Motor actions:** Arm flexion/extension influencing approach/avoidance motivation (Cacioppo et al., 1993)

This theoretical foundation provided biological plausibility for power posing effects within broader embodied cognition frameworks.

**Replication Crisis Emergence:**
**Ranehill et al. (2015):** The first major replication attempt used a larger sample (200 participants) and more rigorous methodology, including:
- **Pre-registered protocol:** Study design specified before data collection began
- **Larger sample size:** Nearly 5x larger than original study for increased statistical power
- **Improved hormone assays:** More precise measurement techniques with better reliability
- **Blind data collection:** Researchers unaware of participant condition assignments

Results showed **complete failure** to replicate hormonal effects:
- **Testosterone:** No significant differences between high-power and low-power conditions
- **Cortisol:** No significant differences in stress hormone response
- **Risk tolerance:** Only marginal effects that didn't reach statistical significance
- **Self-reported feelings:** Small effects potentially attributable to demand characteristics

**Subsequent Replication Attempts:**
Multiple follow-up studies using increasingly rigorous methodologies consistently failed to find the originally reported effects:
- **Bombari et al. (2017):** Swiss study with 247 participants found no hormone effects
- **Jackson et al. (2017):** Found small subjective effects but no physiological changes
- **Klaschinski et al. (2017):** German replication found no effects on any measured outcomes

**Statistical Re-analysis:**
**Simmons and Simonsohn (2017):** P-curve analysis of the power posing literature examined the distribution of p-values in published studies to detect signs of:
- **P-hacking:** Selective analysis and reporting to achieve statistical significance
- **Publication bias:** Preferential publication of positive over null results
- **Questionable research practices:** Data manipulation or selective inclusion/exclusion

The analysis found evidence suggesting that original positive findings likely resulted from methodological artifacts rather than genuine biological effects.

**Lead Author Retraction:**
In an unprecedented move, Dana Carney—the first author of the original 2010 study—published a statement in 2016 declaring: "I do not believe that 'power pose' effects are real." Carney cited:
- **Consistent replication failures** across multiple laboratories
- **Her own unsuccessful attempts** to reproduce the original findings
- **Recognition of methodological limitations** in the original research
- **Concerns about the study's impact** on public understanding of science


The scientific consensus has clearly shifted away from supporting the original power posing claims, particularly regarding hormonal effects. However, the epistemological landscape remains complex regarding subjective psychological effects:

**Hormonal Claims: REFUTED**
The evidence overwhelmingly contradicts claims about testosterone and cortisol changes:
- **Multiple failed replications** with larger samples and better methodology
- **Meta-analytic evidence** showing no reliable hormonal effects (Jonas et al., 2017)
- **Biological implausibility** of rapid hormonal responses to brief postural changes
- **Original author acknowledgment** that the effects are not real

**Subjective Effects: CONTESTED**
Some evidence suggests small effects on self-reported feelings of power, but these findings face several interpretive challenges:
- **Demand characteristics:** Participants may report expected effects rather than genuine experiences
- **Placebo mechanisms:** Subjective improvements may result from belief in the intervention rather than specific biological pathways
- **Context dependency:** Effects may depend heavily on situational factors, individual differences, and measurement specifics
- **Clinical significance:** Even if statistically reliable, the effect sizes may be too small for practical importance

**Methodological Lessons:**
The power posing case has generated important insights about research practices:
- **Sample size requirements:** Psychological effects need much larger samples than historically used
- **Pre-registration necessity:** Study protocols must be specified before data collection to prevent bias
- **Replication imperative:** Findings should not be applied until independently replicated
- **Open science benefits:** Transparent practices enable better error detection and correction

**Meta-Scientific Implications:**
The case highlights systematic issues in how psychological research is conducted, published, and disseminated:
- **Publication bias:** Positive findings are more likely to be published than null results
- **Media translation:** Scientific findings often get oversimplified or misrepresented in popular communication
- **Institutional momentum:** Once findings become integrated into training programs and curricula, they resist updating
- **Economic entrenchment:** Commercial interests can create resistance to scientific correction


## 3. Why People Believe This

The power posing case illuminates several important epistemological issues about how scientific knowledge develops, spreads, and gets validated or refuted:

**Initial Plausibility vs. Rigorous Testing:**
The original power posing study achieved publication in a prestigious journal (*Psychological Science*) and appeared methodologically sound according to contemporary standards. However, the research community's understanding of appropriate sample sizes, pre-registration requirements, and replication protocols has evolved significantly since 2010.

**Effect Size Plausibility:**
Modern endocrinological knowledge suggests that the originally reported hormonal changes were implausibly large for such brief interventions:
- **Testosterone responses:** Typically require sustained physical or psychological challenges over hours or days
- **Cortisol regulation:** Responds to chronic stress or significant acute stressors, not brief postural changes
- **Magnitude inconsistency:** The 20% testosterone increase exceeds changes from intense exercise or competitive victories
- **Timeline implausibility:** Significant hormonal shifts within minutes contradicts known HPA axis dynamics

**Methodological Evolution:**
The power posing controversy occurred during a period of rapid methodological advancement in psychology:
- **Pre-registration protocols:** Study designs specified before data collection to prevent post-hoc analysis manipulation
- **Larger sample requirements:** Recognition that psychological effects require hundreds rather than dozens of participants for reliable detection
- **Open science practices:** Data and analysis code sharing to enable independent verification
- **Replication emphasis:** Systematic attempts to reproduce findings before accepting them as established

**Publication System Dynamics:**
The case reveals how academic publication systems can amplify preliminary findings before validation:
- **Novelty bias:** Journals preferentially publish surprising or counterintuitive findings
- **Media amplification:** Press coverage and TED talks can spread findings faster than replication processes
- **Citation momentum:** Highly cited papers become "established" facts before replication confirms reliability
- **Career incentives:** Researchers benefit from novel, high-impact findings regardless of subsequent replication success


## 4. Verdict

**EARC Classification: CONTRAST (C)**

**Gap Category: Contested-Consensus**

The power posing claims represent a compelling case of initially promising research that systematically failed rigorous replication attempts, revealing the complex dynamics between scientific discovery, popular dissemination, and institutional correction mechanisms.

**Core Scientific Assessment:**
The original power posing claims—particularly regarding hormonal effects—lack credible scientific support after extensive testing with improved methodologies:
- **Hormonal effects:** Multiple large-scale replications have failed to find testosterone or cortisol changes
- **Behavioral effects:** Risk tolerance and performance improvements have not reliably replicated 
- **Subjective effects:** Small self-reported confidence increases may reflect placebo or demand characteristics rather than specific biological mechanisms

**Methodological Contrast:**
The case illuminates the contrast between initial research practices (small samples, post-hoc analysis, publication bias) and contemporary standards (large samples, pre-registration, open science, replication requirements). This methodological evolution explains how initially credible findings can be subsequently refuted without implying fraud or incompetence in the original research.

**Cultural vs. Scientific Trajectories:**
Power posing demonstrates how compelling narratives can achieve cultural entrenchment independently of scientific validation:
- **TED talk amplification:** 70+ million views created global awareness before replication was attempted
- **Institutional adoption:** Corporate training and educational integration occurred faster than scientific validation
- **Economic momentum:** Commercial applications created resistance to scientific updating
- **Narrative appeal:** The empowerment message addressed genuine psychological needs regardless of mechanism accuracy

**Broader Implications:**
The case provides valuable lessons about:
- **Research dissemination:** The need for better systems connecting scientific validation with popular application
- **Media responsibility:** How platforms like TED talks can influence public understanding of preliminary science
- **Institutional adaptation:** How organizations should respond when adopted practices face scientific refutation
- **Scientific literacy:** The importance of understanding research limitations and replication requirements

**Practical Recommendations:**
1. **Avoid hormonal claims:** No evidence supports testosterone or cortisol effects from brief postural changes
2. **Consider placebo effects:** Any subjective benefits likely reflect expectation and ritual rather than specific biological mechanisms
3. **Seek evidence-based alternatives:** Confidence-building interventions with stronger empirical support (preparation, skill development, social support)
4. **Understand research limitations:** Appreciate the difference between preliminary findings and validated practices

**Meta-Scientific Value:**
Beyond the specific claims, power posing serves as an important case study in science communication, research methodology, and the relationship between scientific knowledge and practical application. Understanding this case enhances scientific literacy and critical evaluation of research-based claims in popular contexts.

## 5. The Wider Picture

The power posing phenomenon operates within several broader contexts that illuminate its significance beyond the specific scientific claims:

**Replication Crisis in Psychology:**
Power posing became a prominent case study in psychology's broader replication crisis, joining other famous failures:
- **Social priming studies:** Claims that subtle environmental cues dramatically influence behavior
- **Ego depletion research:** The idea that willpower operates like a muscle that gets fatigued
- **Facial feedback effects:** Questions about whether facial expressions directly influence emotions
- **Stereotype threat:** Debates about the magnitude and universality of performance effects

This broader crisis led to systematic reforms in research practices, journal policies, and graduate training programs.

**Embodied Cognition Research:**
The power posing failure doesn't invalidate all embodied cognition research but highlights the need for more precise theories about mind-body interactions:
- **Validated effects:** Some embodied phenomena have stronger empirical support (exercise and cognition, postural influences on certain tasks)
- **Mechanism specificity:** Different embodied effects likely operate through different biological and psychological pathways
- **Boundary conditions:** Understanding when and why physical states influence psychological states requires more sophisticated theorizing
- **Individual differences:** People may vary significantly in their responsiveness to embodied interventions

**Corporate Training and Business Education:**
The integration of power posing into business contexts created complex institutional challenges when the science was debunked:
- **Curriculum updating:** Business schools faced decisions about removing content based on debunked research
- **Training program adaptation:** Corporate trainers needed to modify or replace power posing components
- **Economic considerations:** Consulting practices built around power posing had financial incentives to resist scientific updating
- **Alternative approaches:** Institutions sought evidence-based alternatives for confidence and performance enhancement

**Media Science Communication:**
The case highlighted challenges in translating scientific findings for popular audiences:
- **Oversimplification pressures:** Complex research gets reduced to memorable sound bites
- **Correction coverage:** Debunking studies receive less media attention than original exciting findings
- **Platform responsibilities:** Questions about TED talks' role in disseminating unvalidated scientific claims
- **Expert communication:** Scientists need better training in public communication and media interaction

**Gender and Empowerment Narratives:**
Power posing gained particular traction as advice for women entering male-dominated environments, creating tension when the science was refuted:
- **Empowerment messaging:** The technique offered accessible confidence-building for underrepresented groups
- **Scientific accuracy vs. support:** Balancing factual correctness with supportive messaging for marginalized populations
- **Alternative approaches:** Developing evidence-based alternatives for addressing confidence and imposter syndrome
- **Cultural adaptation:** Understanding how gendered advice spreads and persists in professional contexts

**Digital Integration and Technology:**
Power posing became embedded in various technological platforms and applications:
- **Mobile apps:** Smartphone applications providing power posing reminders and guided sessions
- **Wearable integration:** Fitness trackers and smartwatches incorporating posture monitoring and power posing prompts
- **Virtual reality applications:** VR environments designed to facilitate power posing practice
- **AI coaching systems:** Automated programs using power posing as part of confidence-building protocols

**Academic Celebrity and Public Intellectuals:**
Amy Cuddy's rise and fall as a popular psychology figure illustrates broader patterns in academic celebrity culture:
- **TED talk influence:** How individual presentations can transform academic careers and public influence
- **Media amplification:** The role of book deals, speaking tours, and media appearances in spreading scientific ideas
- **Reputation management:** How academics navigate public prominence when their research faces scientific criticism
- **Institution responsibility:** Universities' roles in supporting or distancing themselves from controversial faculty research

**Research Funding and Policy Implications:**
The power posing controversy influenced research funding priorities and policies:
- **Replication funding:** Increased support for studies attempting to reproduce previous findings
- **Methodology training:** Enhanced emphasis on statistical training and research design in graduate programs
- **Open science incentives:** Funding agencies requiring data sharing and pre-registration
- **Applied research standards:** Questions about evidence thresholds for interventions promoted to the public


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

Snopes has not systematically addressed the power posing claims despite their enormous cultural impact and subsequent scientific controversy. This represents a significant gap -checking coverage, as the claims involve specific, testable assertions about biological mechanisms that have been extensively studied and largely refuted by subsequent research.

**Coverage Gap Analysis:**
Several factors may explain the absence of Snopes coverage:
- **Academic vs. Viral Distinction:** The claims originated in peer-reviewed research rather than social media or partisan sources, potentially falling outside Snopes' typical purview
- **Complexity Barriers:** Evaluating hormone research requires technical expertise in endocrinology and research methodology that exceeds typical fact-checking capabilities
- **Temporal Dynamics:** The replication crisis unfolded gradually over several years, making it difficult to identify a specific moment for definitive fact-checking
- **Institutional Authority:** The research emerged from Harvard Business School with prestigious journal publication, lending credibility that might discourage fact-checking scrutiny

**Alternative Information Landscape:**
Without systematic fact-checking, public understanding has been shaped by:
- **Academic debates** published in specialized journals with limited public accessibility
- **Popular psychology coverage** that often lacks technical depth to evaluate methodology
- **Corporate training materials** that may have economic incentives to maintain power posing efficacy claims
- **Fragmented social media discussions** mixing scientific critiques with personal testimonials

This fragmented landscape allows contradictory interpretations to persist simultaneously, with different audiences receiving different messages about power posing's validity based on their information sources.
