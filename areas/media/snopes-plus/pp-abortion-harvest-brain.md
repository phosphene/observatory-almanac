# Planned Parenthood Harvests Fetal Brains During Abortions


## 1. The Claim

The claim that Planned Parenthood "harvests fetal brains" during or immediately after abortions has circulated in anti-abortion political media since at least 2015. In its most sensationalized form, the claim alleges that Planned Parenthood staff perform abortions with the deliberate goal of extracting fetal brain tissue for sale to biomedical researchers — altering surgical procedures, keeping fetuses alive longer than medically necessary, and treating the abortion itself as incidental to a tissue procurement operation conducted for commercial gain.

The precise claim comes in several variants. The least extreme version says that Planned Parenthood provides fetal tissue — including brain tissue — to research organizations and accepts payment for doing so. The middle version claims that this constitutes illegal traffic in human tissue for profit. The most extreme version alleges that fetuses were kept alive so their brains could be extracted while the heart was still beating — framing the procedure as a form of infanticide conducted for biological commerce.

All of these versions derive from a series of secretly recorded undercover videos produced by the Center for Medical Progress (CMP), an anti-abortion activist organization, and released beginning in July 2015. The videos became one of the most significant political controversies of that year, producing congressional investigations, state-level investigations, defunding attempts, and an enduring narrative in conservative media about Planned Parenthood's alleged participation in an organ trafficking scheme.

The gravity of the specific claim — that healthcare providers were extracting brain tissue from living fetuses in a covert commercial operation — compels careful examination of what the videos actually showed, what context was removed, and what investigations subsequently found.


## 2. What's Actually True

Fetal tissue donation for medical research is legal under United States federal law, specifically under the National Institutes of Health Revitalization Act of 1993. The law explicitly prohibits profit from the transfer of human fetal tissue; it allows only "reasonable payments associated with the transportation, implantation, processing, preservation, quality control, or storage" of donated tissue. Informed consent from the patient is mandatory, and crucially, the decision to terminate a pregnancy must be made independently of and prior to any discussion of tissue donation — a provision designed to prevent providers from influencing abortion decisions to serve tissue acquisition goals.

Some Planned Parenthood affiliates, at the time the videos were made, participated in fetal tissue donation programs. The organization's representatives, in the undercover conversations filmed by CMP operatives posing as tissue procurement company representatives, discussed the logistics and costs associated with tissue donation. They discussed which fetal tissue types were most useful for research, how procurement costs were structured, and in some cases used casual or clinical language about tissue preparation that, in edited form, appeared to suggest profit motive and callousness.

The critical finding across all subsequent investigations is that the videos were deceptively edited to remove context that exculpated the Planned Parenthood staff. Forensic analysis of the recordings — conducted by Fusion GPS and confirmed by multiple independent reviewers — identified numerous instances where conversations were spliced, statements were rearranged, and key contextual sentences were deleted. In the most significant case involving a specific "brain harvesting" claim, the edited video showed a fetal heart briefly restarting after an abortion was performed, with a statement about brain tissue appearing to follow. In the unedited footage, a portion of the conversation had been cut that explicitly clarified the fetus was deceased at the time neural tissue was examined.

The specific claim about extracting brains from "living" fetuses was further addressed through scrutiny of another viral element: allegations about "beating hearts snatched out of live babies." This claim derived from a misrepresentation of the Langendorff perfusion technique, a standard laboratory method used to study cardiac tissue function in isolation. The heart tissue was not extracted from a living fetus; it was preparation from deceased fetal tissue maintained under laboratory conditions to enable study of cardiac physiology. Conflating a lab tissue-preparation technique with live infant organ harvesting is a category error of significant magnitude.

Twelve states conducted investigations and found no evidence that Planned Parenthood affiliates sold fetal tissue for profit or engaged in other alleged wrongdoing. The U.S. House Oversight and Government Reform Committee similarly found no evidence of illegal conduct. In November 2019, a federal jury found CMP, its founder David Daleiden, and co-defendant Sandra Merritt liable for violations of RICO (the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act), fraud, trespass, breach of contract, and illegal secret recording. The jury awarded Planned Parenthood more than two million dollars in damages.

In California, Daleiden and Merritt faced criminal charges including felony invasion of privacy. A plea to a felony charge was entered in 2025. These legal outcomes — spanning civil and criminal proceedings across multiple jurisdictions — constitute the judicial record on the veracity of the videos and their production methods.

In October 2015, Planned Parenthood announced that its clinics would no longer seek any reimbursement for expenses related to tissue donation, even though accepting reimbursement for procurement costs was and is legal. The organization characterized this change as a means of removing any basis for continued political attacks. Currently, no Planned Parenthood affiliate facilitates fetal tissue donation for research.


## 3. Why People Believe This

The CMP videos were sophisticated influence operations. They were not home videos or accidental recordings; they were produced with editorial resources, framing choices, and psychological architecture designed to produce a specific emotional and moral response. The production team understood that visual evidence — particularly footage of clinical procedures and frank clinical discussion — would carry far more persuasive weight with audiences than written claims.

The moral architecture of the claim maps onto deep and pre-existing cultural fears. Organ harvesting is a recurring nightmare in modern cultural imagination, connected to historical anxieties about body snatching, exploitation of the vulnerable, and the commodification of human life. The specific claim that babies were kept alive to have their brains removed resonated because it touched on the most primal category of atrocity. Audiences experiencing that emotional response were not inclined to scrutinize methodology.

The videos were released strategically, in sequence, over weeks — each release designed to renew the news cycle and allow the cumulative impression of wrongdoing to solidify before the next revelation. By the time forensic analysis began circulating, the underlying emotional conclusion had already been formed for many viewers. Corrections and context struggled to overcome a fully formed belief.

Anti-abortion politics provided a vast pre-existing distribution network. Evangelical and Catholic media outlets, right-leaning political commentary, and prominent elected officials amplified the videos without waiting for independent verification. The political incentive structure rewarded amplification: the videos provided fresh, vivid evidence for positions that were already held. There was no institutional mechanism for pausing circulation pending fact review.

It is also worth noting that the existence of legal fetal tissue donation functions, for some audiences, as confirmation of the broader claim. If Planned Parenthood affiliates were involved in fetal tissue programs of any kind, the distinction between legal reimbursement-for-costs and illegal profit-for-sale is one that requires comprehension of statutory nuance. For audiences who believe any fetal tissue transaction is inherently wrong — a position many hold sincerely on moral grounds — the legal distinction is unpersuasive, and the "harvest" claim becomes plausible because any tissue procurement seems equivalent to it.

The ongoing political salience of abortion as a cultural and legislative battleground means the claim has continued to circulate in modified forms long after its evidentiary basis was dismantled. Electoral cycles, Supreme Court proceedings, and state-level abortion legislation consistently prompted renewed interest in the videos and the underlying narrative. Each reactivation introduced the claim to audiences who had not encountered the original context or the subsequent investigations.


## 4. Verdict

**False.** The specific claim that Planned Parenthood "harvested" fetal brains during abortions — with the connotations of illegal commercial organ trafficking, alteration of abortion procedures to obtain that tissue, and extraction from living fetuses — was false as stated and was built on deceptively edited video footage. Multiple investigations conducted by state attorneys general, federal oversight committees, and independent forensic analysts found no evidence of illegal fetal tissue trafficking by Planned Parenthood.

The foundation of the claim, the Center for Medical Progress videos, was found by a federal jury to have been produced through fraud, RICO violations, illegal recording, and breach of contract. Their creator entered a felony plea in 2025.

The factual kernel within the claim is that fetal tissue donation programs, including some that involved neural tissue, operated legally through some Planned Parenthood affiliates. That activity was legal, was subject to federal consent and prohibition-on-profit requirements, and was not the same as the "brain harvesting" claim made by CMP. The edited videos deliberately structured viewer understanding to conflate the two.

Snopes rates this claim as "False," consistent with the findings of every independent investigation that examined the underlying evidence.


## 5. The Wider Picture

The CMP video operation is now a documented case study in the construction of viral misinformation through selective editing. The technique used — record extensive footage, identify segments where clinical or in-context language can be isolated from explanatory framing to produce a misleading impression, release in edited form — is applicable across many domains and is not unique to the abortion debate. The forensic methodology used to analyze the CMP videos subsequently informed frameworks for detecting selectively edited footage in other political contexts.

The medical and scientific community's response illuminated the genuine importance of fetal tissue research. As noted in peer-reviewed literature and statements from the National Institutes of Health, fetal tissue has been instrumental in the development of vaccines for polio, rubella, measles, chickenpox, hepatitis A, and shingles — vaccines whose existence depends on research conducted using the same biological material that the CMP videos portrayed as a site of criminal exploitation. COVID-19 vaccine development also involved fetal cell lines. This is not a fringe or marginal scientific activity; it is foundational to modern vaccinology and is why major health institutions resisted calls to ban fetal tissue research even during the height of the political controversy.

The political consequences of the videos were substantial regardless of their veracity. Congressional defunding votes against Planned Parenthood followed the video releases. State investigations were launched. Some states passed legislation restricting fetal tissue research. The political infrastructure that the videos activated remained operational long after their evidentiary claims were dismantled — a demonstration of how false claims that generate sufficient initial momentum can produce durable policy effects without requiring sustained accuracy.

The CMP case also raises important structural questions about investigative journalism and activist media. The CMP operated under the cover of journalistic investigation, recorded conversations without consistent consent, and deployed journalistic conventions — the undercover investigation, the document release, the whistleblower-adjacent framing — as tools of political activism rather than truth-seeking. The legal distinction between journalism and fraud in undercover operations is not a settled bright line in US law, and the CMP prosecution turned in significant part on the fraudulent misrepresentation used to gain access rather than on the act of recording itself. This has implications for how courts and regulators assess activist operations that adopt journalistic form.

The ongoing criminal proceedings against Daleiden and Merritt extended through the mid-2020s, and their outcomes continued to be covered primarily by media aligned with the organizational interests involved. The general public's awareness of the legal dispositions — including the federal civil verdict and the state criminal plea — remained substantially lower than their awareness of the original video claims, consistent with the documented asymmetry in initial claim virality versus correction visibility.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

Snopes addressed this claim directly and rated it False, examining both the specific "harvesting" claim and the broader tissue trafficking allegations. FactCheck.org produced detailed analysis of the first CMP video shortly after its release in 2015, identifying the misleading editing and providing the missing context around clinical language that had been stripped from the tape. Both outlets followed the story as additional videos were released and as investigations proceeded.

The case represents a relatively high-confidence fact-check in the classic form: a specific empirical claim that was tested against documentary evidence, investigative findings, and judicial outcomes, and was found to be false on each dimension. The core facts — what the videos actually showed in unedited form, what the investigations found, what the courts determined — are not genuinely contested in the evidentiary record. The persistence of the underlying belief is sustained by ideological commitment and selective media consumption rather than by any evidentiary ambiguity.

Fact-checkers covering this topic face two recurring challenges. The first is the "firehose" problem: the CMP released ten videos over the course of the campaign, each requiring individual analysis, and the resources required to fact-check each claim exceeded what most outlets could deploy ahead of the initial news cycle. By the time thorough analysis was available, the claim had already been institutionalized in the political discourse of a large segment of the population.

The second challenge is the legitimate-kernel problem. Because legal fetal tissue donation exists and existed, and because some Planned Parenthood affiliates participated in it, fact-checkers must acknowledge that kernel of factual truth while accurately characterizing the gap between what was legal and what was claimed. This requires more epistemic precision than "true or false" — it requires a structured account of which elements are accurate, which are distorted, and why the distortion matters. The Truth Vault format is particularly well-suited to this kind of claim, where the verdict requires layered rather than binary assessment.

The claim also illustrates the importance of tracking claims over time. The original fact-check coverage from 2015 accurately characterized the videos as deceptively edited and found the specific claims unsupported. The subsequent decade produced significant additional evidence: the federal civil verdict, the state criminal proceedings, and ultimately Daleiden's 2025 felony plea. An archived fact-check from 2015 that accurately called the claim false became even more strongly supported by subsequent events — a pattern that vindicates the initial assessment and reinforces the value of ongoing investigation rather than single-point fact-checking.
