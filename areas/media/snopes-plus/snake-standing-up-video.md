# Does Video Show a 'Snake Standing Up'?


## 1. The Claim

In early August 2022, a video spread rapidly across Facebook, Reddit, and other social media platforms showing a large snake raising a substantial portion of its body vertically, with its head elevated high in the air. The video was captioned with descriptions ranging from the relatively accurate ("King Cobra") to the dramatic and somewhat anthropomorphizing ("snake standing up"). Social media commenters debated the identity of the species — the primary candidates mentioned were king cobra and black mamba — and the geographic origin of the recording, with suggestions including Malaysia, India, Zimbabwe, and other countries.

The oldest version of the video identified by Snopes was posted by a Facebook page called "We are Malaysians" on August 1, 2022, with the caption "King Cobra." Within less than two weeks, that single Facebook post had accumulated nearly 5 million views. A slightly extended version of the same video later appeared on a Zimbabwe-based Facebook page operated by Sly Media Productions, suggesting the video had been circulating in multiple regional networks before achieving viral status via the Malaysian page.

The core claim that needed verification was simple: is the video real, and what species of snake does it show? These questions are separable but related. The visual implausibility of a snake raising itself so high prompted some viewers to suspect the video might be computer-generated or otherwise fabricated. The identity question was primarily a matter of taxonomy and geographic distribution.

This entry examines the video's authenticity, the identification of the species, the biomechanics that allow certain snakes to perform this behavior, and the broader context of how large venomous snakes are encountered and documented in Southeast and South Asia.


## 2. What's Actually True

The video is authentic. John Kleopfer, state herpetologist with the Virginia Department of Wildlife Resources, confirmed by email to Snopes that the snake depicted is a king cobra (*Ophiophagus hannah*). A state herpetologist is a professional wildlife biologist responsible for monitoring and managing reptile and amphibian populations within a state; Kleopfer's identification carries the weight of trained professional judgment rather than amateur online identification.

King cobras are fully capable of raising their heads high into the air — a behavior that is well-documented and biologically well-understood. The king cobra is the world's longest venomous snake, with a typical adult length of 10 to 12 feet (3 to 3.6 meters) according to the Smithsonian's National Zoo and Conservation Biology Institute, with exceptional individuals reaching up to 18 feet (5.4 meters). At this size, a king cobra that raises one-third of its body length off the ground can elevate its head to a height of three to six feet — well above human waist level and approaching eye-level for many adults.

The king cobra's ability to "stand up" is a function of its defensive and threat-display behavior. When threatened, territorial, or engaged in combat with other cobras, king cobras raise the anterior portion of the body by pushing against the ground with the ventral surface of their lower body and engaging the muscular posterior while allowing the raised anterior to remain unsupported. The raised portion is typically one-quarter to one-third of the total body length. Simultaneously, the king cobra typically spreads its hood — the extended flattened neck region formed by elongated cervical ribs — to increase apparent size and create the characteristic cobra threat display.

The behavior depicted in the viral video is therefore not anomalous or implausible; it is a standard element of king cobra behavioral repertoire that herpetologists, wildlife photographers, and researchers in the species' range observe regularly. It is dramatic precisely because the scale of the animal makes the behavior visually striking, but there is nothing physiologically unusual about it.

The geographic origin of the video was not definitively established by Snopes at the time of the fact-check. The Malaysian Facebook page that appears to have been the first major amplification point suggests a Southeast Asian origin, and king cobras are found throughout Southeast Asia and South Asia — in India, southern China, mainland Southeast Asia (Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand, Laos, Myanmar), and the major Southeast Asian island systems including the Malay Peninsula, Borneo, Sumatra, and the Philippines. The species' natural range is entirely consistent with a Malaysian or broadly Southeast Asian origin for the footage.

The competing candidate species identified by social media commenters — the black mamba (*Dendroaspis polylepis*) — is Africa's longest venomous snake, reaching up to 14 feet (4.3 meters), with a more typical adult length of approximately 8.2 feet. Black mambas are also capable of raising the anterior portion of their bodies and can do so to a height of approximately four feet. However, black mambas are confined to sub-Saharan Africa and are not found in Southeast Asia. The snake in the video, if filmed in Malaysia or Southeast Asia, cannot be a black mamba, and Kleopfer's professional identification as a king cobra is consistent with the geographic context.


## 3. Epistemic Novelty — What the Fact-Checks Miss

The Snopes fact-check correctly identifies the video as authentic and the species as king cobra, supported by professional expert confirmation. What it does not explore is the genuinely interesting biomechanics and ecology that make the king cobra's vertical raising behavior possible, the species' specific life history and conservation status, or the epistemics of viral wildlife video identification in the age of social media.

**The biomechanics of snake body-raising.** The popular framing of the video as a snake "standing up" anthropomorphizes the behavior in a way that obscures its actual mechanism. Snakes do not "stand" in any meaningful sense comparable to bipedal or quadrupedal locomotion: they have no limbs and cannot support themselves on appendages. The vertical raising behavior is achieved through a combination of muscular control and ground contact geometry.

A snake that raises its anterior body while keeping the posterior on the ground is essentially performing the equivalent of a sit-up — contracting the longitudinal muscles along the dorsal surface of the raised portion while maintaining contact with the substrate below. The muscular system of large constrictors and large venomous snakes includes an extraordinarily dense network of longitudinal, oblique, and transverse muscle segments; a large king cobra may have more than 400 vertebral joints, each articulated by multiple muscle groups. This segmented muscular architecture gives cobras and other large snakes precise biomechanical control over the shape and angle of their bodies (Jayne, 1988, "Muscular Mechanisms of Snake Locomotion: An Electromyographic Study of Lateral Undulation of the Florida Banded Water Snake (*Nerodia fasciata*) and the Yellow Rat Snake (*Elaphe obsoleta*)," *Journal of Morphology*, 197(2), 159–181).

The specific species capable of pronounced vertical body-raising include king cobras, black mambas, and several other large elapid snakes. All of these species reach lengths that make the behavior visually dramatic in a way that smaller species' equivalent behaviors are not. A corn snake raising its anterior body performs essentially the same biomechanical maneuver but at a scale that does not register as remarkable.

**King cobra ecology and behavior.** The king cobra is not merely the world's longest venomous snake; it is also one of the most behaviorally complex and ecologically specialized. It is the only snake species known to build a nest for its eggs — a behavior unique among snakes that involves the female collecting leaf litter and constructing a mound in which the eggs are incubated, with the female remaining near the nest to guard it (Whitaker & Captain, 2004, *Snakes of India: The Field Guide*, Draco Books). The male and female may remain together near the nest site during the incubation period, exhibiting a form of parental investment unusual in squamates.

King cobras are also dietary specialists: they feed almost exclusively on other snakes, including other venomous species. *Ophiophagus* itself means "snake-eater" in Greek. This dietary specialization, combined with a large home range requirement (some individuals have been tracked over ranges of up to 80 square kilometers), makes the species particularly sensitive to habitat fragmentation and forest degradation. The king cobra is classified as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List (Stuart et al., 2012, *IUCN Red List of Threatened Species: Ophiophagus hannah*), with population declines attributed to habitat loss, persecution, collection for the pet trade and traditional medicine markets, and road mortality.

The king cobra's venom is not the most potent of any snake by lethal dose metrics — many smaller species have more potent venom per milligram. However, the king cobra injects large volumes of venom in a single bite — up to 7 milliliters — and the total dose is sufficient to kill an elephant. Its venom includes postsynaptic neurotoxins (three-finger toxins that block acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction) and phospholipases that degrade cell membranes. Human fatality from untreated king cobra envenomation is high; death can occur within 30 minutes in severe cases due to respiratory paralysis (Rajib Datta, 2015, "A Clinico-epidemiological Study of King Cobra (*Ophiophagus hannah*) Envenomation in Eastern India," *Indian Journal of Critical Care Medicine*, 19(7), 399–403).

**Viral wildlife video identification.** The viral spread of the king cobra video illustrates a broader challenge in online wildlife identification: large, dramatic wildlife videos routinely circulate with uncertain or incorrect species identification, frequently conflating species that are superficially similar but ecologically and geographically distinct. In this case, the confusion between king cobra and black mamba reflects a reasonable uncertainty — both are very large venomous snakes capable of raising their anterior bodies, and an observer unfamiliar with the significance of geographic range might treat them as equivalent alternatives. But they inhabit entirely different continents, and an identification that fails to consider distribution is fundamentally incomplete.

Professional wildlife identification under these conditions requires not just visual pattern matching but integration of behavioral, ecological, and geographic constraints. Kleopfer's identification — confirming king cobra — aligns the visual evidence with the biogeographic context. The Snopes fact-check does not explain the reasoning behind this identification in any detail, leaving readers to trust the expert opinion without understanding why the alternative (black mamba) was excluded or what visual features distinguished the king cobra.

For the reader who encounters similar viral videos in the future, the key heuristic is: species identification requires geographic context. An enormous, upward-raising venomous snake in Southeast Asia is a king cobra. An equivalent snake in sub-Saharan Africa might be a black mamba, green mamba, or forest cobra. These cannot be determined from visual characteristics alone for the average non-specialist.


## 4. Verdict

The claim is **true**. The video showing a large snake raising its head and a substantial portion of its body into the air is authentic, and the snake has been professionally identified as a king cobra (*Ophiophagus hannah*) by John Kleopfer, a state herpetologist with the Virginia Department of Wildlife Resources. The behavior depicted — a defensive or threat-display body-raising posture — is a documented, well-understood element of king cobra behavioral biology.

The geographic origin of the video was not definitively established at the time of the Snopes fact-check, but the oldest identified version was shared by a Malaysian Facebook page, consistent with the king cobra's natural Southeast Asian range. The competing identification of black mamba, suggested by social media commenters, is geographically inconsistent with a Southeast Asian origin and was not supported by the expert identification.

Snopes verdict: **True**.


## 5. The Wider Picture

King cobras occupy a distinctive place in both herpetology and human cultural history throughout their range. In India, cobras (including the Indian cobra, *Naja naja*, and the king cobra across their respective ranges) are central to religious iconography, appearing as the hood of Shiva and as sacred animals associated with both danger and divine power. In Southeast Asian cultures, large snakes are similarly embedded in cosmological frameworks: the naga, a serpentine deity figure, appears across Hinduism, Buddhism, and indigenous religious traditions throughout the region, and encounters with large cobras are frequently interpreted through this framework.

King cobras are commercially valuable in multiple markets that contribute to their conservation challenges. The live exotic pet trade creates significant demand, particularly for juveniles; despite legal protections under CITES Appendix II listing, illegal collection continues in several range states. Traditional medicine markets in East and Southeast Asia use king cobra products including venom, bile, and skin. Despite their fearsome reputation, king cobras are also captured and exhibited in street performances throughout Thailand and other Southeast Asian tourist markets, with cobras being handled and having their mouths forced open to expose fangs in conditions that cause chronic stress and injury to the animals.

Conservation research on king cobras has been facilitated by radio telemetry and GPS tracking studies. Research by Shankar et al. (2013, "Is the King Cobra (*Ophiophagus hannah*) a Generalist Predator?," *Reptile & Amphibian Research*, 14(1), 1–9) tracked king cobras in southern India and found large home ranges and significant seasonal movement, confirming that the species requires intact forested landscapes. Habitat fragmentation resulting from agricultural expansion and road-building is identified as the primary threat across most of the species' range.

The king cobra's nesting behavior — unique among snakes — has been studied by researchers including Rom Whitaker at the Agumbe Rainforest Research Station in Karnataka, India, who has documented nest construction, female guarding behavior over the incubation period, and the synchronization of hatching with seasonal prey availability (Whitaker & Captain, 2004, *Snakes of India: The Field Guide*, Draco Books). Clutch sizes range from approximately 20 to 40 eggs depending on female body size, and incubation lasts approximately 60–80 days. Hatchlings are fully venomous from birth.

The viral video, beyond its entertainment value, is a document of an encounter between a human (or humans in vehicle or at proximity) and one of Asia's most spectacular predators in what appears to be an agricultural or semi-rural landscape at the forest edge — one of the most common encounter contexts for king cobras as human land use expands into formerly forested habitat. The snake's behavioral response — raising, spreading the hood — is a threat display rather than a predatory posture, indicating that the animal perceived itself at risk and was communicating that status.

One ecological point worth noting: king cobras that adopt this threat display in encounters with vehicles or humans are responding appropriately to a perceived threat stimulus. Despite their potent venom and impressive size, king cobras do not seek out confrontations with humans and will retreat when given the opportunity. Most human envenomations involve accidental contact or deliberate handling attempts, not unprovoked attacks. The dramatic appearance of the threat display in the viral video should not be taken as evidence that king cobras are aggressive or prone to attacking human observers.


## 6. How Fact-Checkers Handle It

The Snopes fact-check by Jordan Liles, published August 12, 2022, correctly rates the claim as **True** and provides adequate sourcing: professional expert identification by John Kleopfer, the Smithsonian Zoo's king cobra size data, National Geographic's black mamba size data for comparison, and the Newsflare/social media circulation history. The check addresses the two primary questions the viral video raised — authenticity and species identity — and answers both correctly.

What the fact-check does not engage with is the biomechanics of the behavior, the reason the competing black mamba identification is geographically implausible, or the ecological and conservation context of king cobras. These omissions are understandable given the primary verification task, but their absence means the reader gains confidence in the video's authenticity without understanding *why* the behavior is possible or *what it means* in terms of the animal's biology.

From a fact-checking methodology standpoint, the key principles demonstrated here are: (1) professional expert consultation via email is the correct approach for wildlife species identification from video; (2) named credentials (state herpetologist at a named state wildlife agency) provide transparency about the basis for the identification; (3) reverse image search methods for video provenance — identifying the oldest available version across platforms — are an effective tool for establishing viral origin; and (4) size and behavioral comparison with the competing species (black mamba) helpfully frames the identification process.

The fact-check's treatment of the geographic uncertainty (Malaysia? India? Zimbabwe?) is appropriately honest: the older Malaysian page established one plausible geographic origin, but the video's actual filming location was not confirmed. This uncertainty does not affect the authenticity verdict — wherever it was filmed, the snake's behavior and identification are consistent with king cobra — but is worth maintaining transparency about in terms of what is and is not known.

The user comments noted in the fact-check — including jokes comparing the snake's head-raising behavior to looking for a spouse at retail stores — represent the characteristic humor that viral wildlife videos generate online, and speak to the strong emotional valence these encounters create: genuine awe-and-fear at the animal's scale and presence, contextualized with the humor that humans deploy to process the unfamiliar.
