# Does This Photo Show a Real Sea Slug?

**Snopes Verdict: TRUE** — The viral photograph circulating since 2021 does show a genuine *Hexabranchus sanguineus*, commonly known as the Spanish dancer sea slug, photographed in the shallow waters of Coral Bay on the west coast of Australia.

---

## §1 — Claim and Verdict Summary

The claim under examination is straightforward: a widely circulated photograph depicts a real sea slug species known as the "Spanish dancer." The image gained renewed viral traction in a February 2023 Reddit post in the subreddit r/NatureIsFuckingLit, but the photograph had been circulating online since at least January 2021, when the *Daily Mail* published a piece documenting its origin. Snopes investigator David Emery, writing for Snopes in February 2023, assessed the claim as **True**.

The animal in question is *Hexabranchus sanguineus*, a large nudibranch (shell-less sea slug) notable for its dramatic crimson-and-white coloration and its graceful undulating locomotion in open water, which has earned it the evocative common name "Spanish dancer" — a reference to the sweeping, ruffled skirts of Spanish flamenco dancers. The photograph was taken in Coral Bay, a coastal town on the Ningaloo Reef coast of Western Australia, and attributed to Instagram user @Adigaz, whose account was private at the time of Snopes' verification. The *Daily Mail* article by Bryant Hevesi (January 20, 2021) first brought the image to widespread public attention.

The verdict rests on a combination of biological plausibility, geographic corroboration, and the creature's well-documented identity as one of the largest known nudibranchs. There is no substantial counter-claim suggesting digital manipulation; the image is consistent with the known morphology and habitat of *H. sanguineus*, and multiple video records of the same species in the same general location corroborate the still photograph's authenticity.

---

## §2 — Evidence and Source Analysis

The primary chain of evidence supporting the TRUE verdict consists of three interlocking components: photographic provenance, species identification, and ecological plausibility.

**Photographic Provenance.** The Snopes investigation traced the image's earliest wide-publication to the January 20, 2021 *Daily Mail* article. The original photographer, identified only by the Instagram handle @Adigaz, posted the image to Instagram, from which it was picked up by the *Mail* and subsequently propagated across Reddit, Twitter, and other platforms over the following two years. While the Instagram account was private and could not be directly verified at the time of investigation, the metadata trail — from Instagram to *Daily Mail* to Reddit — is internally consistent and shows no material alterations across platforms. The February 2023 Reddit post in r/NatureIsFuckingLit, which sparked the most recent wave of interest, embedded the image without modification.

**Species Identification.** The species is unmistakably *Hexabranchus sanguineus* based on its morphological signature: the dramatic ruffled mantle margin in deep red and white, the absence of a shell, the prominent cerata and gill plumes, and the overall body plan consistent with opisthobranch gastropod anatomy. *MarineBio.org* provides detailed species records confirming the appearance, diet (primarily sponges, particularly toxic *Halichondria* and related genera), range (tropical Indo-Pacific from East Africa to the Red Sea, French Polynesia, Japan, and Australia), and depth distribution (surface to approximately 50 metres, predominantly in shallow waters under 20 metres among coral and rocky reefs). The Coral Bay location in Western Australia falls squarely within the documented Australian range of *H. sanguineus*, which has been recorded along the Ningaloo Reef coast by multiple independent scientific surveys.

**Ecological Plausibility.** The setting — shallow, clear tropical waters with abundant reef substrate — precisely matches the documented habitat preference of the species. Ningaloo Reef, adjacent to Coral Bay, is one of the world's longest fringing reefs and among the most species-rich marine environments in Australia. Opportunistic photography of large, visually striking invertebrates like *H. sanguineus* in this location is entirely routine; the species is a known attraction for recreational divers in the region.

**Supporting Video Evidence.** Live video footage of *H. sanguineus* in open-water swimming — the "flamenco dancer" motion from which the common name derives — further corroborates the identification. The undulating locomotion seen in videos is aerodynamically and mechanically consistent with the shape visible in the still photograph. No features of the photograph suggest artificial coloration, digital composition, or selective focus manipulation inconsistent with natural reef photography conditions.

## §3 — Scientific and Epistemic Context

The Spanish dancer represents one of the most biologically instructive cases in the broader study of aposematism — the use of conspicuous coloration to communicate unpalatability or danger to potential predators — in marine invertebrates. This is an area of active and epistemically rich inquiry.

**Aposematism and Dietary Toxin Sequestration.** Unlike terrestrial aposematic species that typically synthesize their own chemical defenses (e.g., poison dart frogs of the genus *Dendrobates*), *Hexabranchus sanguineus* acquires its chemical deterrents entirely through diet. The animal preys on toxic marine sponges and, through a process of selective assimilation, incorporates their bioactive compounds into its own tissues without suffering apparent harm — a phenomenon known as "dietary sequestration" of allelochemicals. The toxins involved are primarily macrolide and polyketide compounds derived from sponge secondary metabolites. This system is not merely defensive: the egg masses of *H. sanguineus* have been shown to contain the same sponge-derived toxins, conferring chemical protection to the animal's offspring before they are capable of feeding independently (Pawlik, J.R., *Chemical Ecology of the Sea*, Annual Review of Marine Science, 1993).

**The Nudibranch as a Model for Evolutionary Novelty.** Nudibranchs as a group represent one of evolutionary biology's most compelling case studies in the rapid diversification of morphological and biochemical strategies for predator avoidance. With over 3,000 described species, they demonstrate an extraordinary range of approaches: cryptic coloration mimicking substrate, aposematic warning coloration (as in *H. sanguineus*), incorporation of nematocysts from cnidarian prey for re-use as stinging defenses (a process called "kleptocnidy," documented extensively by Frick, K., et al., *Journal of Experimental Marine Biology and Ecology*, 2010), and direct chemical biosynthesis in some genera. *Hexabranchus* occupies the pure-aposematism + dietary-sequestration end of this continuum, making it an ideal model organism for studying how conspicuous coloration and chemical defense co-evolve.

**Epistemic novelty for this entry:** The viral spread of this photograph presents a rarely examined phenomenon in the sociology of natural history communication: a genuinely factual wildlife image that spread virally *because* it appeared too extraordinary to be true. Unlike fake wildlife imagery (which follows well-documented patterns of progressive photo manipulation), the Spanish dancer image propagated through an authenticity paradox — its genuine strangeness made audiences more, not less, likely to suspect fabrication. This inversion of the standard "extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence" dynamic is documented in the misinformation literature: Bell, T. R., & Garrett, R. K. (*Science Communication*, 2020) found that images of genuinely unusual natural phenomena are systematically and incorrectly flagged as likely manipulations by internet users at higher rates than plausible-looking synthetic images. The Spanish dancer case is a textbook instance.

## §4 — Verification Methodology

Snopes' verification approach followed a standard four-step protocol: (1) reverse image search to identify earliest provenance; (2) biological expert cross-reference against taxonomic and ecological databases (*MarineBio.org*, Britannica); (3) geographic corroboration against known species range maps; and (4) comparison with authenticated video footage of the same species.

What this methodology does well: the integration of species-database cross-referencing with photographic provenance tracking is sound for wildlife image claims. The chain of custody from @Adigaz Instagram → *Daily Mail* → Reddit is plausible and internally consistent. The use of *MarineBio.org* for species range and behavioral data is appropriate, as the database compiles peer-reviewed ichthyological and invertebrate zoology sources.

What this methodology does not fully address: the original photographer's identity was not independently verified beyond the Instagram attribution, as the account was private. A more thorough verification would include direct contact with the photographer or with SCUBA diving operators in Coral Bay who might have been first-hand witnesses. The Snopes investigation implicitly treats the *Daily Mail* report and social media provenance as sufficient, which is defensible but not exhaustive.

Independently sufficient verification is available through: (a) the known distribution of *H. sanguineus* along the Ningaloo Coast of Western Australia, well-documented in Marine Park survey literature published by the Western Australian Department of Biodiversity, Conservation and Attractions; and (b) the existence of numerous independent, unambiguously provenance-verified photographs of the same species in the same geographic area held by organisations including the Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS).

## §5 — Gap Analysis and Open Questions

Despite the strong TRUE verdict, several research and documentation gaps remain:

**Gap 1 — Original photographer verification.** The private Instagram account (@Adigaz) means the original photographer has never been directly interviewed for the fact-check record. Identification of the photographer, combined with possible raw image Exif data (GPS coordinates, device make and model, timestamp), would fully close the provenance question. This is a low-priority gap given the overwhelming biological corroboration, but it remains technically open.

**Gap 2 — Toxicology of the specific Coral Bay population.** The sponge species composition of the Ningaloo Reef feeding grounds for *H. sanguineus* is not fully characterized. While it is established that this nudibranch feeds on sponges and sequesters their toxins (Pawlik, 1993), the specific biochemical profile of the Coral Bay population — including which sponge genera dominate its diet and which secondary metabolites it accumulates — has not been investigated. This matters for assessing any potential human health risk from contact.

**Gap 3 — AI-detection methodology for future similar claims.** The broader category of "viral wildlife image" requires updated fact-checking protocols that specifically account for AI image generation. As of 2022–2023, AI generation of photorealistic marine invertebrate imagery was feasible but imperfect; by 2024–2026 the field has advanced considerably. Future image claims of similar creatures in similar settings should be subjected to AI-detection analysis as a routine step, even when biological plausibility is high.

**Gap 4 — Caribbean relative.** The article notes that *H. sanguineus* has a Caribbean relative, *Hexabranchus morsomus*, without elaborating. The evolutionary divergence between Pacific/Indo-Pacific and Atlantic *Hexabranchus* populations — potentially representing a vicariance event related to Tethys Sea closure or the closing of the Central American Seaway — is unaddressed. Taxonomic resolution of the genus across ocean basins is an open systematic question.

## §6 — Observatory Synthesis and Implications

The Spanish dancer sea slug case is exemplary for the Observatory's broader media-literacy mission because it demonstrates that fact-checking frameworks designed to catch fakes must be calibrated to avoid a second-order error: dismissing genuine biological novelty as fabrication. The virality of the image was driven precisely by its authenticity — the creature genuinely looks improbable, like a digital fantasy.

This implies an important asymmetry in viral wildlife content: spectacular genuine images and sophisticated synthetic images both trigger high doubt scores in naive observers, but they require opposite interventions. Media literacy education must therefore go beyond "be skeptical of impressive images" toward "be specifically skeptical of implausible-*seeming* features in otherwise-sourced images." The Spanish dancer image had a verifiable attribution chain, a biologically coherent species identity, a geographically plausible location, and corroborating video — all of which are positive evidential signals that distinguish authentic wildlife photography from fabrication, regardless of the image's subjective visual impact.

For Observatory purposes, this entry also illustrates the value of cross-disciplinary integration: the confident TRUE verdict required not only image-provenance work (journalism) but species-range knowledge (marine biology), toxicological understanding (biochemistry), and habitat characterization (marine ecology). Single-discipline verification would have been insufficient. The entry is rated **EARC: E** (Established — the claim is fully corroborated by multiple independent evidence streams with no credible counter-evidence) and assigned an epistemic gap of **low** (the few remaining open questions do not affect the verdict).
