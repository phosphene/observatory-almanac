## The Claim

On September 6, 2024, a video was shared to the social media platform X by the account @InternetH0F, under the caption: "Body armor company demonstrates their stab protection on their CEO." The footage showed a man standing calmly while another individual delivered a series of forceful stabbing motions directly at the man's torso, apparently using a bladed instrument. The recipient of the stabbing attempts showed no signs of pain or injury. The vest he was wearing appeared undamaged.

The video attracted substantial attention and commentary. "He was really going at it like he didn't like the CEO and was hoping it would fail," wrote one X user. Another noted the intensity and precision of the stabbing attempts: "Bro has oddly fast and accurate stabs." The video spread rapidly to YouTube, Instagram, and Facebook. The combination of the action's dramatic quality — a man being repeatedly stabbed in what appeared to be earnest — and the unusual character of the demonstration generated considerable viewer interest and skepticism.

The claim embedded in the video's caption is straightforwardly verifiable: either the man in the vest is the CEO of a body armor company, or he is not; either the vest was demonstrated in an official company context, or it was not. The viral spread of the video prompted fact-checkers at Snopes to verify both elements, which they did, rating the claim as True.

---

## What's Actually True

The video was genuine and correctly captioned. The man wearing the vest is Robert Kaiser, the founder and Chief Executive Officer of PPSS Group, a company headquartered in the United Kingdom that specialises in high-performance body armor and personal protective equipment (PPE). According to the company's official website, PPSS Group was established in 2009 and describes itself as "a global leader in the specialised field of high-performance body armour, personal protective equipment (PPE), clothing, and workwear solutions."

The specific footage shared in the September 2024 viral post was recorded at the International Security Expo in London on December 4, 2019, according to a caption on the PPSS Group website's video archive. The full video — uploaded to the PPSS Group YouTube channel on December 19, 2019 — was titled "PPSS Group Launch Next Generation Stab Vests at International Security Expo." Robert Kaiser was listed as the company's founder and CEO on the PPSS Group leadership page, confirming his identity.

The demonstration was not an isolated act of bravado but part of an established pattern of public presentations by Kaiser. The PPSS Group website describes the demonstration as something Kaiser performs "at many major global security events" to showcase his confidence in the company's protective technology. The International Security Expo in London is one of the largest professional security industry events in Europe and serves as a venue where suppliers demonstrate their products to security professionals, corporate buyers, and government procurement officers.

The vest being demonstrated was part of the PPSS Group's line of stab-resistant vests, which the company distinguishes from ballistic body armor. The two categories address fundamentally different threat profiles and use different materials and construction methods. Stab-resistant vests are engineered to resist knife penetration through the use of tightly woven chainmail, compressed laminated fabrics, or hybrid material systems. Ballistic vests address bullet penetration through energy-absorbing materials such as Kevlar (a para-aramid synthetic fiber, developed by Stephanie Kwolek at DuPont and first commercialised in the 1970s) and ceramic trauma plates.

The efficacy of the demonstration — Kaiser was visibly uninjured — reflects genuine engineering: the materials in a certified stab-resistant vest are specifically designed to prevent knife blades from penetrating the wearer's body. Whether a vest is effective depends on the standard to which it is tested and certified. PPSS Group's vests are certified to European and international standards for personal protective equipment, and Kaiser's public demonstrations are, in effect, live product trust signals in a market where the primary selling proposition is protection of human life.

## Why People Believe This — and Why Some Didn't

The claim in this case is true, but the video's viral spread produced significant skepticism alongside belief. The pattern of viewer responses is instructive about how audiences evaluate dramatic visual evidence.

**The "too good to be true" heuristic.** When a piece of visual content presents an outcome that is simultaneously desirable and improbable — in this case, a man standing unhurt after repeated stabbing — audiences trained on viral deception apply a skepticism heuristic. The initial pattern-matching response is: this seems implausible, therefore it is likely staged, faked, or misleading. This heuristic is useful as a starting point but can be over-applied. High-performance protective equipment does exactly what the video shows. The fact that the demonstration is visually dramatic does not make it false — it makes it effective marketing.

**Commentary revealing dual readings.** The audience comments collected by Snopes illustrate two simultaneously active readings of the video. One reading foregrounds the human drama: "He was really going at it like he didn't like the CEO and was hoping it would fail." This framing personalises the interaction, introducing a narrative of authentic antagonism that viewers found entertaining. The other reading foregrounds technical skepticism: what kind of knife is being used? Is the blade retractable? Are the stabbing motions genuine? The fact-check doesn't address blade type or the stabbist's identity, focusing instead on confirming the identities of the participants and the authenticity of the company context.

**The novelty of CEO-as-product-demonstrator.** The specific combination of product category (potentially life-saving protective equipment) and demonstrator identity (the CEO of the company that makes it) strikes most viewers as unusual. In most commercial contexts, product demonstrations are performed by marketing staff, by professional demonstrators, or by stunt performers using controlled conditions. The sight of a CEO personally absorbing the risk of a live product demonstration is unusual enough to trigger uncertainty about whether the identification is accurate. Kaiser's practice of personally demonstrating his company's products at security trade shows is a deliberate and distinctive corporate communication strategy — he is, in effect, staking his physical safety on his stated confidence in the product.

**The "product really works if your CEO trusts it with his life" dynamic.** This is an epistemic novelty worth noting explicitly: the CEO-as-live-demonstrator phenomenon is a specific form of credibility signalling that is extremely difficult to fake convincingly in the relevant professional context. At the International Security Expo, the audience consists largely of experienced security professionals who cannot be easily fooled by stagecraft. The demonstration is performed in front of professional peers for whom product integrity is a technical matter, not a performance. This audience context makes the demonstration significantly more credible than it might initially appear to a general social media audience.

---

## Verdict

**The claim is true.** The video shows exactly what the caption says it shows: the CEO of a body armor company, Robert Kaiser of PPSS Group, voluntarily wearing his company's stab-resistant vest while another person attempts to stab him with a knife, at a professional security industry event. Kaiser was uninjured. The vest performed as marketed.

The Snopes verdict of True is unambiguous and correctly applied. This is a case where the claim is fully verifiable through multiple independent sources: the PPSS Group's own website and YouTube channel, the event record of the International Security Expo 2019, and Kaiser's confirmation as the company's CEO through the company's leadership page. The viral framing introduced no inaccuracies and no distortions.

## The Wider Picture

The PPSS Group CEO demonstration video sits at an intersection of several substantive topics in personal safety, materials science, corporate communication, and the economics of trust in high-stakes product markets.

**The science of stab-resistant materials.** Stab resistance is a distinct and technically demanding protective property. A knife blade under manual stabbing force can concentrate enormous pressure per unit area — the narrow tip of a blade creates a stress concentration that overwhelms materials designed to absorb distributed impacts. Materials engineering approaches to stab resistance cluster into several strategies. Chain mail and metallic mesh prevent blade penetration by distributing the point load across interconnected metal rings, with the tensile strength of the links preventing penetration. Tightly woven para-aramid fabrics (Kevlar, Dyneema) or ultra-high-molecular-weight polyethylene (UHMWPE) create textile resistance through the molecular entanglement of extremely high-strength polymer chains. Hybrid systems combine layers of different materials to address different threat profiles (NIJ, 2020, *Ballistic Resistance of Body Armor* and related stab protection standards; Horsfall et al., 2010, *Journal of Materials Science: Materials in Medicine*).

The certification standards that govern stab-resistant vests specify test protocols including drop-weight and push-though tests using standardised blade geometries and penetration limits. In the European context, standards such as EN ISO 13998:2003 govern protective clothing against cuts and stab wounds from hand knives. PPSS Group's vests are tested and certified to applicable standards, and Kaiser's demonstrations are effectively public confirmations of that certified performance.

**The body armor market and trust infrastructure.** Body armor is a product sold into a market where buyers have extraordinary stakes in product authenticity. Law enforcement agencies, private security contractors, military units, and individual security professionals purchase protective equipment with the understanding that their lives may depend on its performance. This market structure creates powerful incentives for honest certification and powerful disincentives for misrepresentation — the reputational and legal consequences of certifying a product that fails to protect are severe.

In this context, Kaiser's practice of personally absorbing live demonstrations is a sophisticated trust communication strategy. The CEO's participation eliminates the most obvious skeptical counter — that the demonstration uses specially prepared equipment, a cooperative demonstrator, or controlled conditions — because Kaiser is accepting the personal risk. If the vest fails, he is injured. This self-exposure functions as a credibility guarantee that no amount of conventional marketing copy or third-party certification can fully replicate, because it directly addresses the gap between certified performance in controlled test conditions and real-world performance under actual threat conditions.

The market for personal protective equipment in security contexts is international and competitive. PPSS Group, headquartered in the UK, competes with manufacturers across Europe, the United States, and Asia. Trade show demonstrations are one of the primary channels through which product capabilities are communicated to professional buyers. Kaiser's distinctive demonstration format has generated substantial organic media coverage — including the September 2024 viral spread, occurring five years after the original demonstration — that substantially amplifies the company's visibility beyond what conventional trade advertising would achieve.

**The social media lifecycle of authentic industrial footage.** The September 2024 viral spread of a video originally filmed in December 2019 illustrates a characteristic pattern in how authentic industrial or corporate footage travels through social media platforms over time. The original footage was produced by PPSS Group for professional purposes and posted to its YouTube channel as documentation of a trade event. It circulated within security industry channels and received limited general attention.

Five years later, an account aggregating noteworthy internet content (@InternetH0F) discovered or rediscovered the footage and shared it in a framing stripped of its professional context — "body armor company demonstrates their stab protection on their CEO" — that foregrounded the dramatic human element (a man being stabbed) rather than the industrial context (a trade show demonstration at the International Security Expo). This reframing dramatically widened the potential audience while introducing uncertainty about authenticity that had not been present in the original professional circulation.

The Snopes fact-check, published September 15, 2024, responded to this viral moment by restoring the professional context — identifying the company, the CEO, the event, and the certification claim — that the viral framing had stripped away. This restoration function is one of the most valuable things fact-checkers provide in the social media context: not necessarily identifying falsehood, but reconstructing context that organic viral spread tends to lose.

**Corporate communication through embodied risk.** The specific communication strategy Kaiser employs — personally absorbing a live risk demonstration — has precedents in several industries. Pharmaceutical testing pioneers sometimes self-experimented with compounds they developed, though this practice is now heavily regulated and discouraged. Structural engineering and civil engineering professionals have historically performed public confidence demonstrations with their constructions — most famously, John Roebling demonstrated the stability of his wire rope by riding a platform across a test installation before the Brooklyn Bridge was constructed, and similar demonstrations attended various suspension bridge proposals of the 19th century. In food safety contexts, executives have publicly consumed products associated with contamination scares to signal confidence in their safety.

In each case, the embodied risk-taking functions as a credibility signal that is difficult to replicate through any purely testimonial or documentary means, because it transforms a claim ("our product is safe and effective") into direct personal stake. Kaiser's stab vest demonstrations belong to this tradition: his continued physical safety across multiple such demonstrations is itself ongoing evidence of product performance.

## How Fact-Checkers Handle It

The Snopes fact-check, authored by Aleksandra Wrona and published September 15, 2024, is a clean example of a verification case that is straightforwardly resolvable through source identification and provenance tracing.

**Verification through the originating organisation's records.** The primary verification step was identifying the footage's source in the PPSS Group's official digital archives — the company's video library and YouTube channel. The video's caption on the PPSS Group website explicitly identified Kaiser as the demonstrator and the International Security Expo as the venue, providing the metadata needed to confirm both elements of the claim. This source-to-original-publication approach is robust because the company's own records, produced for professional purposes and predating the viral spread by five years, have no incentive to misidentify either the person or the event.

**Leadership verification.** Confirming that Robert Kaiser is the company's CEO required checking the PPSS Group's published leadership information, which lists his name and role. This is a simple but necessary verification step — the claim is specifically that the CEO demonstrated the product, not merely "a company executive" or "a customer."

**Minimal scope and clean verdict.** The fact-check stays closely scoped to the verifiable claim: is this the CEO of a body armor company demonstrating his own product? Yes. It does not make claims about the vest's performance specifications, the identity of the demonstrator delivering the stabbing attempts, or the precise model of knife used, all of which would require additional verification. This scope discipline produces a clean True verdict without overreach.

The case is also a useful reminder that viral claims are not necessarily false claims. The social media spread of unusual or dramatic footage frequently introduces suspicion not because the footage is fabricated but because dramatic footage looks suspicious. Fact-checking provides its most obvious value when it catches falsehoods, but it provides genuine value in cases like this one by confirming that something remarkable genuinely occurred as described — and by providing the contextual detail that restores the full meaning of the footage to an audience that received it stripped of its original context.
