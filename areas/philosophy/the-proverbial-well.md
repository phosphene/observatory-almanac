# The Proverbial Well
## Two Hundred Voices from Every Corner of the Earth

*Draw from this well as needed. These sayings were not written — they were worn smooth by passing through thousands of mouths over thousands of years. A proverb that survives is one that has been found true enough, often enough, to be worth keeping. They are the compressed wisdom of people who had no room for the false and little patience for the useless. Read slowly. More than one will find you.*

*The continent that gave us our oldest language and our most practiced wisdom about community. African proverbs tend to hold the individual and the collective in the same sentence — a perspective the modern world spends considerable effort rediscovering.*

## A CLOSING WORD

*Two hundred and some voices, from a thousand cultures, across ten thousand years. They agree on more than they dispute: patience over haste, community over isolation, the earth as something held in trust, words as something wielded carefully, adversity as something that sharpens rather than breaks.*

*There is comfort in that agreement. These people — the Yoruba grandmother, the Japanese fisherman, the Lakota elder, the Irish farmer, the Maori navigator — they never met. They lived centuries apart, on opposite sides of the world, under different gods and different skies. And yet the lessons they learned from living carefully and paying attention were largely the same.*

*When enough people discover the same truth independently, it stops being opinion and starts being wisdom.*

*Draw from this well as needed. Return when the bucket is empty.*
