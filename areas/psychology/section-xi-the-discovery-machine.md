## Body & Survival: Field Diagnostics for Staying Alive

*Practical, actionable. No hedging. When you need to know, you need to know now.*

---

## PART ONE: DANGER ASSESSMENT

### Is This Electrical Wire Dangerous?

**Visual Inspection — Work outward to inward:**

| What You See | Urgency |
|---|---|
| Wire intact, insulation smooth, no discoloration | 🟢 Monitor |
| Insulation cracked, brittle, or discolored | 🟡 Address soon |
| Insulation melted, bubbled, or scorched | 🔴 Act immediately |
| Exposed copper conductor visible | 🔴 Act immediately |
| Arcing, sparking, or flickering | 🔴 Act immediately |
| Wire near or in water | 🔴 Act immediately |

**The smell test:** Burning plastic smell near an outlet, panel, or appliance = 🔴 Act immediately. This is pre-fire, not post-fire. Turn off the circuit at the breaker.

**Proximity rules:**
- Human body clearance from suspected live wire: minimum 10 feet if outdoors, more if wet
- Never touch a wire to test voltage — use a non-contact voltage tester (under $20, keep one)
- Water + electricity: assume any submerged wire is energized until proven otherwise

**Downed power line protocol:**
1. Stay back minimum 30 feet (the "step potential" danger zone extends further than the wire)
2. Do NOT approach to help someone in a car — call 911
3. If you are IN a car that hits a downed line: stay inside, call 911, wait for utility workers
4. If you MUST exit a car touching a live wire: jump clear with both feet together, shuffle-hop away — never walk normally (step potential kills)
5. Never use a rope, stick, or rubber glove to move a live line — consumer items are not rated for line voltage

### Is This Water Safe to Drink?

**Sensory hierarchy (run all three, not just one):**

**Visual:** Clear ≠ safe, but these are red flags:
- Cloudiness or turbidity → suspended bacteria/sediment
- Blue-green tint → algae bloom (cyanotoxins, extremely dangerous)
- Brown/orange → rust, iron, or tannins (may be harmless, may not)
- Oily sheen that breaks into geometric patterns (not rainbow-swirls) → petroleum contamination 🔴

**Smell:**
- Bleach/chlorine → municipal treatment, generally okay
- Rotten eggs (sulfur) → hydrogen sulfide, possibly bacterial; test before drinking
- Chemical/petroleum → 🔴 do not drink
- No smell → necessary but not sufficient for safety

**Taste (if smell passed):**
- Metallic → heavy metals or old pipes
- Chemical/bitter → contamination
- No taste → still may contain biologicals

**Biological vs chemical threat distinction — critical:**
- Biological (bacteria, protozoa, viruses): can be treated with boiling (1 minute, 3 minutes at altitude), filtration (0.1 micron or better), chemical treatment (iodine, chlorine tablets), or UV
- Chemical (pesticides, heavy metals, petroleum): **boiling concentrates chemical contaminants**. Filter only with activated carbon rated for the specific chemical, or don't drink it.

**Field testing sequence:**
1. Look → smell → small taste test (spit, don't swallow)
2. If in doubt: boil for biologicals, find an alternative source for suspected chemical contamination
3. Clear running water from upstream (above human habitation) is lowest-risk natural source
4. Stagnant water is always higher risk regardless of appearance

### Is This Ice Safe to Walk On?

**Thickness rules (clear, solid ice):**

| Ice Thickness | Safe For |
|---|---|
| Less than 2" | 🔴 No one — stay off |
| 2" | 🟡 One person on foot (marginal) |
| 4" | 🟢 One person on foot |
| 5-7" | 🟢 Snowmobile |
| 8-12" | 🟢 Small group / ATV |
| 12"+ | 🟢 Small vehicle |

**These numbers assume clear, solid, blue ice.** Reduce safe thickness by 50% for:
- White/opaque ice (air bubbles = 50% density)
- Layered ice with water between layers
- Ice near inflows/outflows, pressure cracks, or springs
- Ice formed on moving water

**Color reading:**
- Blue/black = dense, strong ice 🟢
- White/gray = air-saturated, weak 🟡
- Slush gray = near-melt condition 🔴
- Dark spot from below = thin zone, open water below 🔴

**Sound assessment:**
- Hollow boom when you step = ice flexing, water space below 🔴
- Sharp crack = stress fracture propagating — back off immediately using same tracks
- Creaking/groaning = normal thermal expansion in cold conditions 🟡

**If you fall through:**
- Don't panic — your clothing traps air (30–90 seconds before hypothermia impairs you)
- Turn toward direction you came (that ice held you)
- Kick feet to horizontal, claw elbows onto ice edge, slide out like a seal — do NOT stand up
- Roll away from the hole, don't walk

### Is This Building Structurally Sound?

**Crack assessment — cosmetic vs structural:**

| Crack Type | Indicator | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Hairline, paint only | Cosmetic settling | 🟢 Monitor |
| Vertical crack, <1/4" | Minor settling, common | 🟢 Monitor |
| Horizontal crack in basement wall | Lateral soil pressure — structural | 🔴 Act immediately |
| Diagonal crack from door/window corner | Differential settlement | 🟡 Address soon |
| Crack wider at top than bottom | Active settling | 🟡 Address soon |
| Crack with displacement (one side higher) | Structural failure | 🔴 Act immediately |
| Stair-step crack in brick/block | Foundation movement | 🟡→🔴 Evaluate urgency |

**Foundation check:**
- Walk the perimeter — look for soil pulling away from foundation (shrinkage/settling)
- Visible gaps between foundation and framing above = serious
- Efflorescence (white mineral deposits) on concrete = chronic water infiltration

**Doors and windows:**
- Doors/windows that suddenly won't close or have new gaps = building racking (structural shift)
- Multiple doors affected simultaneously after no weather event = 🔴 evaluate immediately

**Water damage indicators:**
- Soft floors (especially near bathrooms/kitchens) = subfloor rot
- Ceiling stains: active = darker/wet ring; old = dry, chalky ring
- Musty smell throughout = chronic moisture = mold likelihood

**After earthquake, flood, or fire:**
- Do not re-enter until cleared by structural engineer or building inspector
- Gas smell = leave immediately, do not use electrical switches

### Is This Neighborhood Safe? Environmental Reading

**Physical environment cues:**

| Observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Broken windows, boarded buildings | Disinvestment, reduced guardianship |
| Working street lights | Maintained infrastructure 🟢 |
| No working lights, bulbs broken | Low deterrence environment 🟡 |
| Graffiti: tags only | Territory marking, moderate signal |
| Graffiti: crossed-out tags | Active territorial conflict 🔴 |
| People on streets, mixed ages | Organic community presence 🟢 |
| Isolated, no foot traffic at peak hours | No natural surveillance |

**Situational awareness:**
- Dead ends and poor sight lines = reduced escape routes = 🟡
- Who's watching you? People who look without purpose, track your movement = stay aware
- Follow your threat detection instinct — discomfort without visible cause is data, not paranoia

### Rip Current Identification and Escape

**How to spot one from shore:**
- Discolored (murkier/foamy) channel cutting through breaking waves
- Choppy, churning surface in a channel 20–100 feet wide
- Noticeable gap where waves aren't breaking (the current is suppressing wave formation)
- Debris or foam moving steadily seaward

**Escape:**
1. Don't fight it swimming directly toward shore — you'll exhaust yourself
2. Swim **parallel to shore** until out of the current (typically 20–100 ft)
3. Then swim diagonally toward shore
4. If you can't escape: float, wave for help — rip currents don't pull you under, they pull you out

### Avalanche Terrain Assessment

**Red flags:**

| Sign | Urgency |
|---|---|
| Recent avalanche debris visible on slope | 🔴 Active avalanche path |
| "Whumpfing" sound underfoot (snow settling) | 🔴 Unstable snowpack |
| Shooting cracks radiating from footsteps | 🔴 Extremely unstable |
| Slopes 30–45° (prime avalanche angle) | 🟡 Requires assessment |
| Convex roll (crest of slope) | 🔴 Highest stress zone |
| Lee slopes with wind-deposited slabs | 🟡 Slab risk |
| Recent heavy snow (24–48hrs) | 🟡 Loading risk |
| Recent rapid warming or rain on snow | 🔴 Wet avalanche risk |

**Survival:**
- Triggered: try to ski/run to side of slide path
- Caught: discard poles, fight to stay on surface (swimming motion)
- Buried: create air pocket before snow sets (hand over face, expand chest), spit to find down (gravity), dig toward light — or stay still to conserve oxygen

### Lightning Risk (The 30/30 Rule)

**30/30 Rule:**
- If time between lightning flash and thunder is **30 seconds or less** → seek shelter immediately (storm within 6 miles)
- Wait **30 minutes** after the last thunder before resuming outdoor activity

**Position hierarchy (safest to most dangerous):**
1. 🟢 Substantial building with plumbing/electrical (Faraday cage effect)
2. 🟢 Hard-topped metal vehicle (not convertible)
3. 🟡 Low-lying open area, away from trees
4. 🔴 Open water, elevated terrain, lone trees, metal fences, open structures

**Lightning position (if caught in open):**
- Crouch low, feet together, on balls of feet (minimize ground contact area)
- Do NOT lie flat
- Move away from trees, poles, metal objects
- Separate from other people by 50+ feet (reduces group strike risk)

### Wildfire Evacuation Decision Tree

```
Is there an official evacuation order?
    YES → Leave immediately. Do not wait.
    NO ↓

Can you see smoke or flames?
    YES, visible flames < 1 mile → Leave now.
    YES, distant smoke only ↓

Is your route out still clear?
    UNKNOWN → Leave now (fire moves faster than you think)
    YES ↓

Do you have >2 hours of prep time?
    NO → Leave now
    YES → Implement "go bag" protocol, prepare structure, leave early
```

**Pre-evacuation actions (only if you have time AND route is clear):**
- Close all windows/doors (buys 30–60 min against embers)
- Move combustibles away from house
- Fill gutters with water if possible
- Leave lights on (visibility in smoke for firefighters)
- Leave doors unlocked

### Flash Flood Indicators

**Warning signs:**
- Rapid rise in stream/creek level
- Water turning muddy without local rain = upstream event
- Roaring sound from upstream canyon or drainage
- Rain of any intensity in narrow canyons — you don't need to see it raining where you are
- Official flash flood watch/warning in area

**In a canyon or wash:** 🔴 Move to high ground immediately if ANY of the above are present. Flash floods travel faster than a person can run.

**In a vehicle:** Do not drive through flooded roads — 2 feet of moving water can sweep away most vehicles. "Turn around, don't drown."

## PART TWO: FOOD SAFETY DIAGNOSTICS

### Meat Assessment

**Raw meat — use all senses:**

| Sign | Decision |
|---|---|
| Off-pink/gray surface | 🟡 Check smell — surface oxidation is normal |
| Slimy texture | 🔴 Discard |
| Sour, ammonia, or sulfur smell | 🔴 Discard |
| Gray throughout (not surface only) | 🔴 Discard |
| Normal pink/red with faint meat smell | 🟢 Safe to cook |

**Cooked meat:**
- Smell test: sour, off, or "wrong" = discard
- Slimy surface on cooked meat = bacterial growth = 🔴 Discard
- Left out >2 hours at room temp (>40°F) = 🔴 Discard (no exceptions — cooking won't neutralize toxins already produced)

### Fish Assessment

**Fresh fish indicators:**
- Smells like the ocean (clean, briny) = 🟢
- "Fishy" smell = beginning of spoilage 🟡
- Strong ammonia/sour smell = 🔴 Discard
- Eyes should be clear and slightly bulging (sunken/cloudy = old)
- Flesh should spring back when pressed (doesn't = spoiling)
- Gills should be bright red (gray = old)

### Dairy Assessment

**Milk:** Smell first. Sour smell = 🔴. Slightly off but not sour = drink today or cook with.
**Cheese:** Hard cheeses — cut 1" around visible mold, eat remainder. Soft cheeses — mold = 🔴 discard entire container (mold penetrates throughout).
**Yogurt:** Liquid on top = normal whey separation, stir it. Off smell or pink/orange mold = 🔴 Discard.

### Egg Float Test

Fill a bowl with cold water:
- Sinks, lies flat = 🟢 Fresh (air cell tiny)
- Sinks, stands on end = 🟡 Use soon (larger air cell)
- Floats = 🔴 Discard (large air cell = decomposition gas)

**Crack and smell:** Bad eggs have unmistakable sulfur smell. When in doubt, crack into a separate bowl before using.

### Bread Mold Rules

- **Visible mold on bread:** 🔴 Discard the entire loaf. Bread is porous — mold filaments penetrate invisibly throughout, even if only visible in one spot.
- Exception: Some artisan breads develop surface bloom that is not mold (white flour dusting). Rub it — flour wipes off, mold doesn't.

### Produce Assessment

- **Soft spots:** On firm produce (apple, potato, melon) = cut away generously + 1 inch around; the rest is safe. On soft produce (tomato, berry) = discard if >30% affected.
- **Mold on produce:** Firm items (cabbage, carrots, bell peppers): cut generous margin, safe to eat remainder. Soft items: discard.
- **Off smell in produce:** Always discard (bacteria or decomposition)

### Mushroom Safety — Absolute Rules

**The only safe rule:** 100% positive identification by an expert, or don't eat it.

**Why folk tests fail:**
- The "silver spoon turns black" test: false. Has no basis in chemistry.
- "Peeling cap = safe": false. Death Cap (Amanita phalloides) peels easily.
- "Animals eat it, so safe": false. Squirrels can process toxins humans cannot.
- "It doesn't taste bitter": false. Death Cap reportedly tastes pleasant.
- Cooking does NOT neutralize Amanita toxins.

**Species to categorically avoid without expert confirmation:**
- Any white gilled mushroom (Death Cap, Destroying Angel family)
- Any small brown mushroom (LBM = "Little Brown Mushroom" — dozens of toxic species)
- Any mushroom with a volva (cup at base) = suspect Amanita

**Symptoms of Amanita poisoning:** Delayed 6–24 hours. You feel fine, then organ failure begins. By the time symptoms appear, liver damage may be irreversible. If any mushroom consumption is suspected: emergency room, not wait-and-see.

### Expiration Dates Decoded

| Label | Meaning | What To Do |
|---|---|---|
| **Sell By** | Retailer's stock rotation guide | Product good days-weeks beyond this |
| **Best By / Best Before** | Peak quality date | Usually safe after; quality declines |
| **Use By** | Safety date (infant formula, some dairy) | Follow strictly for these categories |
| **Freeze By** | Optimal freeze date, not safety date | Freeze before this for best results |

**Real safety guide:** Your senses beat any date stamp for most foods. Dates are quality estimates, not microbial timers. Exceptions: infant formula, raw meat, and oysters — treat use-by dates as hard limits.

## PART THREE: BODY LITERACY

### Referred Pain Map

Pain location ≠ problem location. Key patterns:

| Where You Feel It | Could Be |
|---|---|
| Left arm/jaw/shoulder | Heart 🔴 |
| Right shoulder (especially tip) | Gallbladder/liver |
| Between shoulder blades | Aortic dissection 🔴, esophageal, gallbladder |
| Lower back | Kidneys, ovaries, aortic aneurysm |
| Groin | Kidney stones, hernia, hip |
| Shoulder tip (either) | Diaphragm irritation (internal bleeding, ruptured spleen) |
| Ear/jaw | Tooth, TMJ, heart (left side) |
| Navel area radiating right | Appendicitis (pain migrates to right lower quad) |

**Rule:** New, severe, or unusual pain with no obvious cause → medical evaluation. Don't self-diagnose based on location alone.

---

### ABCDE Mole Rule

| Letter | What to Check | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| **A** — Asymmetry | One half doesn't match the other | 🟡 |
| **B** — Border | Irregular, ragged, notched, or blurred edges | 🟡 |
| **C** — Color | Multiple colors, uneven distribution | 🟡 |
| **D** — Diameter | Larger than 6mm (pencil eraser) | 🟡 |
| **E** — Evolving | Any change in size, shape, color, or new symptoms (bleeding, itching) | 🔴 |

**Any single ABCDE flag = dermatologist appointment.** Evolving (E) is the most important — a changing mole is more concerning than a stable atypical one.

### Heart Attack Symptoms by Gender

**Classic symptoms (more common in assigned-male-at-birth individuals):**
- Chest pressure, tightening, or crushing sensation
- Pain radiating to left arm, jaw, or neck
- Shortness of breath
- Sweating, nausea

**Atypical symptoms (more common in assigned-female-at-birth individuals, but anyone can have these):**
- Unusual fatigue (days before event)
- Indigestion or jaw/back pain without chest symptoms
- Nausea and vomiting
- Shortness of breath without chest pain
- Feeling of dread or "something is wrong"

**The critical mistake:** Dismissing symptoms because "it doesn't feel like a heart attack" or waiting to see if it passes. The atypical presentation is the one that gets people killed.

🔴 **If suspected: call emergency services immediately, chew aspirin (325mg, if not allergic), sit or lie down, do not drive yourself.**

### FAST Stroke Assessment

| Letter | Test | Positive Sign |
|---|---|---|
| **F** — Face | Ask them to smile | One side droops |
| **A** — Arms | Ask them to raise both arms | One drifts down |
| **S** — Speech | Ask them to repeat a phrase | Slurred, strange, or unable |
| **T** — Time | Note exact time of symptom onset | Call emergency services NOW |

**Why time matters:** Stroke treatment (tPA, thrombectomy) has a 3–4.5 hour window from symptom onset. Minutes = brain tissue.

**Additional signs:** Sudden severe headache ("worst of my life"), sudden vision loss in one eye, sudden loss of balance.

---

### Concussion Post-Impact Checklist

Immediate red flags requiring emergency care:
- Loss of consciousness (any duration) 🔴
- Seizure 🔴
- Repeated vomiting 🔴
- Worsening headache over hours 🔴
- One pupil larger than the other 🔴
- Cannot recognize people or places 🔴
- Clear fluid from nose or ears 🔴

**Monitor for (someone must watch them for 24 hours):**
- Confusion, difficulty concentrating
- Sensitivity to light or noise
- Sleep disturbance
- Mood changes
- Headache that doesn't improve

**Return to activity:** Never return to sports or high-risk activity same day as suspected concussion. "When in doubt, sit it out."

### Dehydration Levels and Response

| Level | Signs | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | Thirst, dark yellow urine, slight fatigue | 🟡 Drink water now |
| **Moderate** | Headache, dizziness, dry mouth, muscle cramps, urine dark orange | 🟡 Oral rehydration, rest in shade/cool |
| **Severe** | Confusion, rapid heart rate, rapid breathing, sunken eyes, no urination >8hrs | 🔴 IV fluids needed — emergency care |

**Urine color guide:** Pale yellow = well hydrated. Dark yellow = drink more. Amber/orange = moderate dehydration. Brown = severe/possible kidney issue 🔴.

---

### Heat Exhaustion vs Heat Stroke

| Condition | Skin | Mental Status | Temperature | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Heat Exhaustion** | Cool, pale, sweating | Alert, dizzy | Normal–100°F | 🟡 Move to cool area, hydrate, cool skin |
| **Heat Stroke** | Hot, red, DRY | Confused, unresponsive | >103°F | 🔴 Emergency — cool aggressively while waiting for services |

**Heat stroke cooling:** Ice water immersion is fastest. If unavailable: cold wet towels to neck, armpits, groin (where blood vessels are close to surface). Fan the patient. Do not give fluids if they're confused (aspiration risk).

---

### Hypothermia Staging

| Stage | Core Temp | Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | 90–95°F | Shivering, poor coordination, slurred speech | 🟡 Remove wet clothing, insulate, warm beverages if conscious |
| **Moderate** | 82–90°F | Shivering stops (bad sign), confusion, stiff muscles | 🔴 Gentle handling, no rubbing, horizontal transport, warm core |
| **Severe** | <82°F | Unconscious, no palpable pulse | 🔴 Emergency — CPR if no pulse, hospital ASAP ("not dead until warm and dead") |

**Warning:** Shivering stopping is NOT improvement — it means the body has exhausted its ability to self-warm.

---

### Allergic Reaction Severity Scale

| Level | Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | Hives, itching, runny nose, watery eyes | 🟡 Antihistamine, monitor |
| **Moderate** | Widespread hives, swelling (not throat), GI upset | 🟡 Antihistamine + monitor closely |
| **Severe (Anaphylaxis)** | Throat tightening, difficulty breathing, voice change, drop in blood pressure, pale/blue skin, rapid pulse | 🔴 Epinephrine (EpiPen) immediately, call emergency services, lie down with legs elevated |

**Anaphylaxis decision tree:**
```
Exposure to known or suspected allergen
    + ANY of: throat swelling, difficulty breathing, voice change, 
      dizziness/fainting, rapid pulse, widespread hives + GI symptoms
    → Use epinephrine NOW (even if unsure)
    → Call emergency services
    → Second dose in 5-15 min if no improvement
    → Antihistamines are NOT a substitute for epinephrine in anaphylaxis
```

### Body Odor as Health Indicator

| Odor | Location | May Indicate |
|---|---|---|
| Fruity/sweet breath | Mouth | Diabetic ketoacidosis 🔴, ketogenic diet |
| Ammonia breath | Mouth | Kidney disease or failure |
| Fishy breath | Mouth | Liver disease, trimethylaminuria |
| Fecal breath | Mouth | Bowel obstruction 🔴, severe constipation |
| Sweet-musty breath | Mouth | Liver failure (fetor hepaticus) |
| Ammonia body odor | Skin | Kidney dysfunction |
| Fruity overall | Skin | Uncontrolled diabetes |
| Fishy body odor | Skin | Trimethylaminuria, liver issues |
| Foul-smelling urine | Urine | UTI, dehydration, certain foods |
| Sweet-smelling urine | Urine | Diabetes, PKU |
| Dark/black stool odor | Stool | Upper GI bleeding 🔴 (digested blood) |

**Note:** Single instances of unusual odor after specific foods are usually dietary. Persistent, new, or unexplained odor warrants medical evaluation.
