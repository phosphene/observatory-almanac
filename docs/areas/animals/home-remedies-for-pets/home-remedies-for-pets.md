# Home Remedies for Pets

*Safe, evidence-informed home care for common pet ailments — and when to go to the vet instead.*

> **This guide is for minor ailments only.** It does not replace veterinary diagnosis or treatment. When in doubt, call your vet. Do not delay professional care based on home treatment.

---

## The Core Rule

Home remedies are appropriate for **mild, isolated symptoms** in an otherwise healthy animal showing normal behavior. They are not appropriate when:
- Symptoms are severe or worsening
- The animal is very young, old, or immunocompromised
- More than one symptom is present
- The animal is not eating, drinking, or eliminating normally
- Symptoms persist more than 24–48 hours

## Digestive Issues

### Mild Upset Stomach (Occasional Vomiting or Soft Stool)

**Bland diet protocol:**
1. Fast for 12–24 hours (water only). Do not fast puppies or kittens under 3 months — hypoglycemia risk.
2. Offer small amounts of bland food: plain boiled chicken (no skin, no seasoning) + plain white rice (1:3 ratio). Or plain unseasoned boiled hamburger, drained.
3. Feed in small amounts every 4–6 hours for 1–2 days.
4. Gradually reintroduce normal food over 2–3 days by mixing.

**When to call the vet:** Vomiting more than 2–3 times in a few hours, blood in vomit, bloated abdomen, lethargy, or if the animal is a puppy/kitten.

---

### Constipation (Dogs)

**Try:** Add 1–2 teaspoons of plain canned pumpkin (not pie filling — must be 100% pumpkin, no spices) to food. High in fiber; well-tolerated by most dogs. Can repeat once daily for 2–3 days.

**Also:** Ensure adequate water intake. Add a small amount of low-sodium chicken broth to water bowl.

**When to call the vet:** No bowel movement for more than 2–3 days, straining, crying, lethargy.

---

### Diarrhea (Mild)

**Bland diet** as above. Add a probiotic formulated for pets (FortiFlora, Proviable) if available.

Plain unsweetened yogurt with live cultures can help restore gut flora in dogs — 1 teaspoon for small dogs, 1 tablespoon for large dogs. Do not use yogurt for cats (most are lactose-intolerant).

**When to call the vet:** Blood or mucus in stool, more than 24 hours in duration, accompanied by vomiting, or if the animal is a puppy/kitten.

## Skin & Coat

### Minor Skin Irritation, Hot Spots, or Mild Itching

**Oatmeal soak:** Blend plain unflavored oatmeal (not instant) into a fine powder. Dissolve in warm water. Apply to affected area or use as a soak for 5–10 minutes. Pat dry (do not rub). Soothes inflammation and relieves itch.

**Diluted apple cider vinegar:** Mix 1 part apple cider vinegar with 1 part water. Apply to affected area with a cloth. Antimicrobial properties; can help with minor yeast overgrowth. **Do not apply to open or raw skin** — it will sting and cause irritation.

**Coconut oil (topical):** Apply a small amount to irritated, dry, or cracked skin. Has mild antimicrobial and moisturizing properties. Safe if licked in small amounts.

**When to call the vet:** Wound is deep or infected (pus, foul smell), the animal is constantly licking or chewing an area (may need an e-collar), large area of hair loss, or symptoms persist more than 3 days.

---

### Dry Paw Pads

Apply a small amount of unscented, plain petroleum jelly (Vaseline), shea butter, or a commercial paw balm. Massage in and put a sock on the paw for 10–15 minutes to allow absorption before the pet licks it off. Safe if ingested in small amounts.

**Prevention:** In winter, rinse paws after walks on salted or de-iced surfaces.

---

### Skunk Spray

See the Wildlife Safety section. Mix 1 quart 3% hydrogen peroxide + ¼ cup baking soda + 1 teaspoon liquid dish soap. Work into wet coat, leave 5 minutes, rinse thoroughly. Repeat if needed. Do not get in eyes. Do not store the mixture.

## Eyes & Ears

### Eye Discharge (Minor, Clear)

Gently wipe away discharge with a clean, damp cotton ball or gauze — wipe away from the eye, outward. Use a fresh piece for each eye.

**When to call the vet:** Yellow or green discharge, squinting, pawing at eye, red or swollen eye, cloudiness, or change in vision (bumping into things).

---

### Ear Cleaning (Maintenance)

Use a commercial ear cleaning solution or a 50/50 mix of warm water and white vinegar (not for ears with open sores or perforated eardrum). Apply a few drops, gently massage the base of the ear for 20–30 seconds, then let the pet shake its head. Wipe the outer canal with a cotton ball. **Never insert cotton swabs into the ear canal.**

**When to call the vet:** Foul odor, dark discharge, persistent head shaking, scratching at ears, ear appears painful to touch — these suggest infection requiring prescription medication.

---

## Minor Wounds

### Superficial Cuts and Scrapes

1. Rinse with clean water or saline for several minutes.
2. Apply diluted chlorhexidine solution (0.05%): dilute 2% chlorhexidine to 1 part solution in 40 parts water. Or use plain hydrogen peroxide once to clean (not repeatedly — it impairs healing).
3. Apply a small amount of plain triple antibiotic ointment (Neosporin without pain relief, which contains pramoxine — avoid pramoxine for pets). Keep the animal from licking the wound.

**When to call the vet:** Wound is more than ½ inch long, deep, gaping, won't stop bleeding, or shows signs of infection after 24 hours (swelling, redness, discharge, odor).

## Fleas

### Environmental Control
Wash all pet bedding in hot water weekly. Vacuum carpets and upholstered furniture thoroughly and empty the vacuum immediately.

### On the Pet
**Dish soap bath:** Dawn or similar liquid dish soap kills fleas on contact by disrupting their exoskeleton. Lather the pet thoroughly, leaving on for 5 minutes before rinsing. This is temporary relief only — it does not prevent re-infestation and can dry out skin with repeated use.

**Fine-toothed flea comb:** Dip comb in soapy water after each pass to drown fleas. Most effective on short-coated pets.

**Note:** Home remedies do not address flea eggs, larvae, or pupae in the environment. For established infestations, a vet-prescribed monthly flea prevention product is necessary.

## Stress & Anxiety

### Mild Situational Anxiety (Thunderstorms, Travel, Fireworks)

**Compression wraps:** A snug-fitting shirt or commercial anxiety wrap (Thundershirt) can reduce anxiety symptoms in many dogs via sustained, gentle pressure. Not effective for all animals.

**Calming scents:** Lavender and chamomile have mild calming effects on some dogs. Apply a small amount of lavender essential oil to a bandana worn loosely around the neck — keep away from nose, mouth, and eyes. Do not apply essential oils directly to skin or cats (cats metabolize essential oils poorly and can be toxified).

**Safe space:** Provide a covered crate or enclosed space with familiar-smelling bedding. For dogs, this simulates a den. Allow the pet to choose to enter and exit freely.

**When to call the vet:** Anxiety is persistent, severe, or affecting quality of life. Behavioral modification and/or medication may be needed.

## Things to Avoid

| Item | Risk |
|------|------|
| Acetaminophen (Tylenol) | Fatal in cats; toxic to dogs |
| Ibuprofen/aspirin | Toxic to both dogs and cats |
| Tea tree oil | Toxic to cats and dogs, especially undiluted |
| Garlic, onion (in any form) | Causes red blood cell destruction in dogs and cats |
| Xylitol (sugar-free sweeteners) | Causes rapid, dangerous hypoglycemia in dogs |
| Hydrogen peroxide (to induce vomiting) | Only use on explicit vet instruction — can cause internal damage |
| Human antifungal creams | Many contain harmful compounds for pets |
| Alcohol on wounds | Kills cells and delays healing |

---

## When Home Care Is Never Appropriate

Go directly to an emergency vet for:
- Difficulty breathing
- Suspected poisoning
- Collapse or inability to stand
- Suspected broken bones
- Penetrating wounds (eyes, chest, abdomen)
- Seizures
- Repeated vomiting or inability to keep water down
- Swollen or distended abdomen
- Signs of severe pain
- Pale, blue, or yellow gums
