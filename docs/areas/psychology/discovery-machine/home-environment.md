## PART ONE: HOME SAFETY

### Electrical Safety Audit

**Outlet testing (DIY):**
- Plug in a lamp or phone charger to each outlet — if no power, outlet may be dead or on a GFCI circuit that tripped
- Check all GFCI outlets (kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoors) — press TEST button, power should cut; press RESET, power should return
- Non-functional GFCI = 🟡 Replace within a week
- Outlet that sparks on plug insertion = 🟡 Address soon; if repeated = 🔴

**Outlet visual inspection:**
| What You See | Urgency |
|---|---|
| Discoloration (brown/black) around outlet | 🔴 Act immediately — arcing |
| Cracks in outlet cover | 🟡 Replace cover |
| Outlet feels warm to touch | 🔴 Act immediately |
| Two-prong outlet (no ground) in bathroom/kitchen | 🔴 Shock/electrocution risk — replace |

**Panel inspection (visual only — do not touch components):**

| Sign | Urgency |
| --- | --- |
| Breakers labeled clearly | 🟢 Good |
| Unlabeled breakers | 🟡 Label them |
| Double-tapped breakers (two wires in one slot) | 🟡 Electrician needed |
| Burn marks, scorching, or melted insulation | 🔴 Electrician immediately |
| Rust or moisture inside panel | 🔴 Electrician immediately |
| Breakers that won't stay reset | 🔴 Persistent fault — electrician |
| Federal Pacific or Zinsco panels (name visible on door) | 🟡 Known defective brands — replacement recommended |

**Arc fault signs:**
- Lights that flicker specifically on one circuit
- Breaker that trips repeatedly without obvious overload
- Burning smell at outlets or along a wall (inside the wall)
- Crackling/sizzling sound at outlets

### Plumbing Health Assessment

**Water pressure test:**
- Normal residential: 40–80 PSI
- Test with a gauge (under $10 at hardware store) on an outdoor hose bib
- Below 40 PSI = 🟡 (municipal pressure issue or pressure regulator failing)
- Above 80 PSI = 🟡 (accelerated fixture wear, potential pipe stress — install pressure reducer)

**Drain speed test:**
- Fill sink, let it drain — should empty within 60 seconds
- Slow drain = partial blockage 🟡 (try plunger or drain snake before chemicals)
- Complete non-drain = 🔴 (obstruction, possible venting issue)
- Gurgling drains elsewhere when one drains = venting problem 🟡

**Water heater assessment:**

| Sign | Urgency |
| --- | --- |
| Age >10 years (standard tank) | 🟡 Plan replacement |
| Rust-colored hot water | 🟡 Sacrificial anode depleted — replace anode or tank |
| Popping/rumbling sounds | 🟡 Sediment buildup — flush annually |
| Water around base of tank | 🔴 Active leak — turn off cold supply valve |
| Temperature >120°F (scalding at tap) | 🟡 Set thermostat to 120°F to prevent burns and save energy |
| TPR valve dripping constantly | 🔴 Pressure issue or faulty valve |

**Leak detection:**
- Check meter: turn off all water, note meter reading, wait 1 hour, check again — any movement = leak
- Toilet leak test: add food coloring to tank, wait 15 min without flushing — color in bowl = flapper leak 🟡
- Check under sinks, around toilet bases, around tub/shower seals for soft spots or discoloration

### Structural Integrity Checklist

**Foundation:**
- Walk perimeter, look at grade: soil should slope away from house (6" drop in 10 feet)
- Flat or toward-house grade = water against foundation 🟡 (regrade or add drainage)
- Cracks: vertical/diagonal < 1/4" = monitor; horizontal = 🔴 lateral pressure, engineer needed
- Voids or tunneling near foundation = possible pest damage or erosion 🟡

**Roof:**
- Ground-level visual: sagging ridgeline or valley = 🟡–🔴 structural issue
- Missing/curling shingles = 🟡 potential leak entry
- Dark staining on sheathing visible in attic = past/current leak 🟡
- Daylight visible through roof sheathing in attic = 🔴 immediate repair needed
- Attic check after rain: flashlight scan for dripping or wet insulation

**Walls:**
- Press on drywall — spongy areas near exterior walls or plumbing = moisture behind wall 🟡
- Bubbling paint = moisture intrusion 🟡
- Diagonal cracks at door/window corners = differential settlement (monitor frequency of change)

**Windows and doors:**
- All exterior doors should seal completely when closed (daylight = energy loss + pest entry)
- Windows should lock securely and open/close without binding
- Rotted wood at window/door frames = 🟡 water infiltration path

### Fire Safety Audit

**Smoke detectors:**
- Required placement: every bedroom, outside each sleeping area, each floor including basement
- Test monthly: press test button — alarm should sound within 5 seconds
- Replace batteries annually (or install 10-year sealed battery units)
- Replace entire unit every 10 years (sensors degrade)
- Interconnected alarms (all sound when one triggers) = 🟢

| Status | Urgency |
| --- | --- |
| Missing detector in bedroom area | 🔴 Install today |
| Detector >10 years old | 🟡 Replace |
| Detector fails test | 🔴 Replace immediately |
| Detector chirps (low battery) | 🟡 Replace battery within 24hrs |

**Fire extinguisher:**
- At minimum: kitchen + garage
- Class ABC rated for home use
- Check pressure gauge monthly — needle should be in green zone
- Replace or recharge if discharged even partially
- Extinguisher >12 years old = 🟡 replace (reliability degrades)

**Using an extinguisher (PASS):**
- **P**ull the pin
- **A**im at base of fire (not flames)
- **S**queeze handle
- **S**weep side to side

**Escape routes:**
- Every bedroom needs two exit options (door + window)
- Windows on upper floors: escape ladder available? 🟡 if not
- Family meeting point outside: designated? 🟡 if not
- Cooking fire protocol: lid to smother pan fires, never water on grease 🔴

### Carbon Monoxide Risk Assessment

**Sources in home:**
- Gas appliances (furnace, water heater, range, dryer)
- Attached garage (car running, even briefly)
- Fireplaces and wood stoves
- Portable generators run indoors or near windows 🔴

**CO detector requirements:**
- At least one on each floor, near sleeping areas
- Replace every 5–7 years (electrochemical sensors degrade)
- CO detector alarm = 🔴 Leave immediately, call emergency services, do not re-enter until cleared

| Sign | Urgency |
| --- | --- |
| No CO detectors in home | 🔴 Install today |
| CO detectors >7 years old | 🟡 Replace |
| Symptoms cluster (multiple people/pets ill simultaneously): headache, dizziness, nausea | 🔴 CO poisoning — evacuate |
| Pilot lights that frequently blow out | 🟡 Combustion air problem — service furnace |
| Yellow/orange flame on gas burner (should be blue) | 🟡 Incomplete combustion — service appliance |

**Generator rule:** Never run a generator inside a garage, basement, or within 20 feet of a window or door. CO travels fast, kills without warning.

### Radon Risk Assessment

**Geography-based risk:**
- EPA radon zones: Zone 1 (high risk, predicted average >4 pCi/L) covers much of the US Great Plains, Appalachia, and upper Midwest
- Any area with granite bedrock = elevated risk
- Basement and first-floor spaces are highest risk

**Testing:**
- Short-term test kit (hardware store, ~$15): place in lowest livable area for 2–7 days
- Long-term test (>90 days): more accurate seasonal average
- Action level: ≥4 pCi/L = 🔴 mitigate (EPA recommends mitigation even at 2–4 pCi/L)

**Mitigation:** Sub-slab depressurization by a certified contractor; typically $800–2500, reduces levels by 50–99%.

### Lead Paint Assessment (Pre-1978 Homes)

**Risk factors:**
- Home built before 1978 = potential lead paint
- Home built before 1940 = high likelihood of lead paint
- Intact, well-adhered lead paint = lower risk (don't disturb)
- Peeling, chipping, or deteriorating paint = 🔴 immediate risk especially for children

**Testing:**
- DIY swab test kits (~$10): work for screening, false negatives possible
- Professional XRF testing: gold standard, ~$300–500
- Dust wipe test for contamination (especially if renovation has occurred)

**Actions if found:**
- Intact paint on surfaces not subject to friction: paint over, monitor
- Deteriorating paint: professional remediation (not DIY sanding — generates toxic dust)
- Pre-renovation notification: federal law requires disclosure to occupants before renovation in pre-1978 homes

### Asbestos Identification

**Where it hides:**
- Textured ceiling paint ("popcorn ceilings") pre-1978
- Floor tiles 9"×9" or 12"×12", especially mastic adhesive
- Pipe insulation (gray, chalky wrap)
- Attic insulation resembling vermiculite (pebble-like, gray-brown)
- Around furnaces, boilers, ductwork (especially gray/white wrap)

**Critical rule:** Asbestos is only dangerous when disturbed (friable = airborne fibers). Intact, sealed asbestos = 🟡 monitor, do not disturb.

**Before any renovation:** Test suspect materials — professional sampling or DIY kits (send to certified lab). Never sand, drill, or demo untested materials in a pre-1980 home without testing first.

🔴 If you've already disturbed suspected asbestos: leave area, close off space, call a licensed abatement contractor.

### Mold Assessment

**Visual:**
- Black, green, white, or orange fuzzy patches on surfaces
- Water staining + musty smell even without visible growth = hidden mold
- Check: under sinks, around shower/tub grout, behind refrigerators, in crawl spaces/basements, attic decking

**Smell:** Musty, earthy odor — strongest when HVAC runs (recirculating spores) = likely mold in ducts or near air handler

**Health symptoms pointing to mold:**
- Symptoms improve when away from home, worsen on return
- Chronic respiratory irritation, coughing, congestion
- Eye irritation
- Fatigue or brain fog

| Finding | Urgency |
| --- | --- |
| Small patch (<10 sq ft), non-porous surface | 🟡 Clean with detergent, fix moisture source |
| Large patch (>10 sq ft) | 🟡 Professional remediation recommended |
| In HVAC system | 🔴 Professional remediation (recirculating throughout home) |
| Black mold (Stachybotrys) confirmed | 🔴 Professional remediation, avoid area |
| Mold return after cleaning | 🔴 Moisture source not fixed — find and repair |

### Indoor Air Quality Self-Test

**Warning signs:**
- Eyes/throat/skin irritation that improves outdoors
- Persistent headaches at home
- Allergy symptoms worse indoors
- Condensation on windows (>30% indoor humidity)
- Visible cooking smoke that lingers (inadequate ventilation)

**Common sources:**
- VOCs: new carpet, furniture, paint (off-gassing, typically dissipates in weeks)
- Combustion: gas cooking without exhaust ventilation, tobacco, candles
- Biological: mold, dust mites, pet dander
- Radon (see above)

**Basic mitigation:**
- Ventilate when cooking (exhaust fan or open window)
- HEPA air purifier in bedrooms
- Replace HVAC filters every 90 days (monthly in high-use/allergy households)
- Keep humidity 30–50% (prevents mold and dust mite growth)

### Home Security Vulnerability Assessment

**Perimeter:**

| Vulnerability | Urgency |
| --- | --- |
| Overgrown shrubs near entry points | 🟡 Concealment for intruders — trim |
| No exterior lighting at doors | 🟡 Motion-activated lights reduce risk |
| Hidden spare key (rock, mat, etc.) | 🟡 Known to intruders — use lockbox |
| Visible valuables through windows | 🟡 Opportunity signals |

**Doors:**
- Exterior door frames: solid wood or metal? Hollow-core exterior doors = 🔴 replace
- Strike plate screws: 3" screws into studs vs 3/4" into doorframe only = major difference in kick-in resistance
- Sliding doors: security bar in track + pin through overlapping frame = 🟢

**Locks:**
- Deadbolts on all exterior doors: 🟢 if yes; 🟡 if only knob locks
- Smart locks with good encryption: 🟢; cheap keypad locks with known bypass: 🟡

### Ergonomic Workstation Assessment

**Chair:**
- Feet flat on floor (or footrest)
- Knees at 90°, hips at or above knee level
- Low back supported by lumbar cushion or chair back
- Armrests at elbow height when typing

**Monitor:**
- Top of screen at or slightly below eye level
- Distance: arm's length away (20–28 inches)
- Screen tilt: slight backward tilt (10–20°) to match natural gaze angle
- No glare from windows (position screen perpendicular to windows)

**Keyboard/mouse:**
- Wrists flat or neutral (not bent up or down)
- Mouse at same level as keyboard
- Keyboard tray preferable to desk surface if desk is too high

**Warning signs of poor ergonomics:**
- Neck/shoulder pain after short sessions 🟡
- Wrist pain or tingling in fingers (carpal tunnel risk) 🟡
- Eye strain, dry eyes, headaches (monitor position/brightness) 🟡
- Back pain that resolves away from desk 🟡

### Childproofing Comprehensive Checklist

**Universal (0–5 years):**
- [ ] Outlet covers on all unused outlets 🔴 (critical under 3)
- [ ] Cabinet locks on cleaning products, medications, sharp tools
- [ ] Stair gates top and bottom
- [ ] Furniture anchored to walls (tip-over fatalities from dressers and TVs)
- [ ] Window stops (prevents opening more than 4 inches)
- [ ] Blind cord loops cut or replaced with cordless
- [ ] Medications in child-resistant caps, stored above reach
- [ ] Cleaning products in locked cabinet, never in food containers
- [ ] Small objects (coin-sized) out of reach (choking hazard)
- [ ] Carbon monoxide and smoke detector on each floor

**Kitchen:**
- [ ] Stove knob covers
- [ ] Hot liquid handling: cook on back burners, handles turned inward
- [ ] Dishwasher locked when running or loaded with sharp items

**Bathroom:**
- [ ] Water heater set to 120°F (prevents scalding)
- [ ] Toilet lock (drowning risk for infants)
- [ ] Non-slip bath mat
- [ ] All medications secured

**Garage/outdoor:**
- [ ] Power tools stored and locked
- [ ] Chemicals (pesticides, antifreeze, pool chemicals) locked
- [ ] Pool: four-sided fence with self-latching gate (not house as one side)

### Pet Safety Assessment

**Toxic plants (universal — all pets):**
Lilies (lethal to cats — all parts), Sago Palm (lethal to all — seeds), Oleander, Foxglove, Yew, Autumn Crocus, Azalea/Rhododendron, Dieffenbachia (common houseplant)

**Dogs specifically:**
- 🔴 Toxic: Grapes/raisins, xylitol (sugar-free gum/products), chocolate, onions/garlic, macadamia nuts, alcohol, caffeine
- Hazard: Unattended trash, cooked bones (splintering), string/rope toys if swallowed

**Cats specifically:**
- 🔴 Toxic: All lily species (kidneys), essential oil diffusers (respiratory/neurological), string/linear objects (intestinal obstruction if swallowed)
- Outdoor cats: traffic, predators, antifreeze (sweet taste, lethal in tiny amounts — clean spills immediately)

**Birds:**
- 🔴 Fumes: non-stick cookware (PTFE/Teflon), scented candles, aerosol sprays — birds have extremely sensitive respiratory systems, can die from fumes that are safe for mammals

## PART TWO: LAND & GARDEN

### Soil Health Assessment

**The Jar Test (soil composition):**
1. Fill a mason jar 1/3 with soil, fill rest with water, add 1 tsp dish soap
2. Shake vigorously, let settle 24–48 hours
3. Layers from bottom: sand → silt → clay
4. Ideal loam: roughly 40% sand / 40% silt / 20% clay

**pH test:** Litmus/pH strips or soil test kit. Most vegetables prefer 6.0–7.0. Blueberries: 4.5–5.5. If outside ideal range → amend (lime raises pH, sulfur lowers pH).

**Earthworm count:** Dig a 1 cubic foot hole, count worms. >10 = excellent biology 🟢. 5–10 = moderate 🟡. <5 = compacted/low organic matter 🟡.

**Drainage test:**
- Dig hole 12" deep, fill with water, observe
- Drains in <1 hour = 🟢 good drainage
- 1–6 hours = 🟡 moderate (add organic matter, consider raised beds)
- >6 hours = 🔴 poor drainage (amend heavily or install French drain)

**Compaction test:** Push a metal rod or screwdriver into moist soil — should go in easily to 6". Resistance at 2–3" = compaction (root growth impaired).

### Tree Health Assessment

**Red flags requiring arborist evaluation:**

| Sign | Urgency |
| --- | --- |
| Fungal conks/shelf mushrooms on trunk | 🔴 Internal rot — structural failure risk |
| Cavities in trunk | 🟡–🔴 Evaluate size/location |
| Cracks through trunk | 🔴 Imminent failure risk |
| Leaning that has increased recently | 🔴 Root/structural failure |
| Dead wood in crown (>25% of canopy) | 🟡 Remove dead wood |
| Roots circling base (girdling roots) | 🟡 Will strangle tree over time |
| Bark peeling in large sections (not natural for species) | 🟡 Disease or vascular damage |
| Two or more trunks from same base (co-dominant stems) | 🟡 Install cable or monitor split risk |

**Near structures/power lines:** Any tree with dead crown, lean toward structure, or fungal fruiting = professional assessment before next wind event.

---

### Slope and Erosion Risk

**Slope angle assessment:**
- <15% slope: 🟢 Low erosion risk
- 15–25%: 🟡 Moderate risk — vegetation cover critical
- >25%: 🔴 High risk — engineered solutions (retaining walls, bioswales) may be needed

**Warning signs:**
- Exposed roots on slope = erosion has already removed soil 🟡
- Rills (small erosion channels) after rain = surface runoff concentrated 🟡
- Slump or bulge in slope = possible slip plane 🔴 (geotechnical evaluation)
- Trees leaning downhill in a line = slope creep 🟡

## PART THREE: DISASTER PREPAREDNESS

### Emergency Preparedness Score

Score yourself on a 0–10 scale across these categories:

**72-Hour Kit (0–3 pts):**
- [ ] 1 gallon water per person per day × 3 days (+1 pt)
- [ ] 3-day food supply, non-perishable, no cooking required (+1 pt)
- [ ] Medications (30-day supply in portable bag), first aid kit, flashlight, radio (+1 pt)

**Communication Plan (0–3 pts):**
- [ ] Out-of-area contact person designated and known to all household members (+1 pt)
- [ ] Meeting point if home is inaccessible (+1 pt)
- [ ] Household members know plan without looking it up (+1 pt)

**Documents (0–2 pts):**
- [ ] Copies of critical documents (ID, insurance, medical records) in waterproof container or cloud storage (+1 pt)
- [ ] Know location of physical originals (+1 pt)

**Evacuation Readiness (0–2 pts):**
- [ ] "Go bag" assembled and accessible within 5 minutes (+1 pt)
- [ ] Multiple evacuation routes known and practiced (+1 pt)

**Score interpretation:**
- 8–10: 🟢 Well-prepared
- 5–7: 🟡 Partial preparation — identify gaps
- 0–4: 🔴 Significant vulnerabilities — address systematically

## PART FOUR: VEHICLE ASSESSMENT

### Used Car Assessment Checklist

**Exterior (before it warms up):**
- [ ] Consistent panel gaps (uneven = crash repair)
- [ ] Paint match under different lighting (mismatched sections = repainted after damage)
- [ ] Check wheel wells for rust
- [ ] Look under car: fluid puddles on pavement? (water from AC = normal; oil, coolant, transmission fluid = 🔴)

**Frame inspection:**
- [ ] Look at frame rails under hood: straight and unpainted? Welded seams or overspray = frame damage 🔴
- [ ] Open hood from above: shock towers should be symmetric and un-crinkled
- [ ] Run finger along door jamb welds: factory welds are uniform; repair welds are lumpy

**Engine (cold start is key — sellers often warm up first):**
- [ ] Cold start smoke: blue smoke = burning oil 🔴; white smoke = coolant in combustion 🔴; no smoke = 🟢
- [ ] Idle quality: smooth and quiet after 30 sec?
- [ ] Check oil dipstick: level, color (black = due for change, but not failure), consistency (milky = coolant contamination 🔴)
- [ ] Coolant reservoir: level and color (rusty/dark = old; should be green, orange, or pink per type)

**Transmission (test drive):**
- [ ] Automatic: smooth shifts without hesitation, clunking, or slipping
- [ ] Fluid smell on dipstick (automatics): burnt smell = worn fluid or slipping clutch packs 🔴
- [ ] Manual: clutch engages smoothly throughout pedal travel (not just at top or bottom)

**Test drive checklist:**
- [ ] Steering: no pulling, no vibration, no play
- [ ] Brakes: no pulsing, no grinding, straight stop
- [ ] Acceleration: no hesitation, no smoke
- [ ] Listen for rattles, knocks, or hums at different speeds

### Is My Car Safe to Drive Right Now?

**🔴 Critical — do not drive:**
- Brake pedal goes to floor or feels spongy
- Steering wheel hard to turn (power steering failure)
- Temperature gauge in red zone
- Oil pressure warning light on
- Tire visibly flat or severely low (below 20 PSI)
- Smoke from under hood or exhaust
- Check engine light flashing (solid = 🟡; flashing = 🔴 catalytic converter damage risk)

**🟡 Address soon — drive carefully/short distances:**
- Solid check engine light (non-flashing)
- Brake vibration or pulling to one side
- Unusual noise that's new and consistent
- Tire pressure low but above 20 PSI
- AC failure (monitor engine temp)

### Tire Assessment

**Tread depth — Penny Test:**
- Insert penny Lincoln-head first into tread groove
- If top of Lincoln's head is visible: 🔴 Replace immediately (2/32" or less)
- If top of head is at tread level: 🟡 Replace soon (at limit)
- If top of head is covered: 🟢 Adequate tread

**Quarter test:** Insert Washington's head — if top is covered, you have more than 4/32" (recommended replacement point for wet/snow conditions).

**Sidewall inspection:**
- Bulges or blisters: 🔴 Replace immediately (structural failure imminent)
- Deep cracks in sidewall: 🔴 Replace immediately
- Minor surface cracking: 🟡 Monitor, replace within 6 months
- Cuts, embedded objects: 🟡–🔴 Evaluate depth; objects sealing hole vs actively leaking

**Tire age:**
- DOT code on sidewall: last 4 digits = week/year (e.g., "3219" = week 32 of 2019)
- Tires >6 years old: 🟡 Inspect professionally
- Tires >10 years old: 🔴 Replace regardless of appearance (rubber degrades internally)

**Pressure:**
- Find correct pressure on door jamb sticker (not tire sidewall — that's max pressure)
- Check cold (before driving)
- 10% low: 🟡 Affects fuel economy, handling
- 20%+ low: 🔴 Blowout risk at highway speeds
